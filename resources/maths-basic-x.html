🔢 Real Numbers
Ch 1 · Euclid's Division · FTA · Irrational Numbers · Decimal Expansions

📖 Key Concepts

Euclid's Division Lemma
For any two positive integers a and b, unique integers q and r exist such that a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b. Used to find HCF step-by-step.
HCF by Euclid
Divide larger by smaller; replace with (smaller, remainder); repeat until remainder = 0. Last non-zero remainder = HCF(a, b).
Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic
Every composite number is a unique product of primes (order aside). Forms basis for LCM and HCF via prime factorisation.
LCM × HCF = a × b
Valid for exactly two numbers. If HCF = 12 and LCM = 360, product = 12 × 360 = 4320 = a × b.
Irrational Numbers
Cannot be written as p/q (q ≠ 0). Decimal is non-terminating, non-repeating. √2, √3, √5, π, e are all irrational.
Terminating Decimal
p/q terminates ⟺ q = 2m × 5n. If denominator has any other prime factor, decimal is non-terminating repeating.

🔑 Master Formulae

Euclid: a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b
HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b (two numbers only)
Terminating: denominator = 2m · 5n only
√p is irrational for any prime p (proof by contradiction)

✍️ Short Answer (2–3 Marks)

SA 1 (2M) Prove that √3 is irrational.
Ans: Assume √3 = p/q (lowest terms). Then 3 = p²/q², so p² = 3q². Thus 3 | p², so 3 | p. Let p = 3m. Then 9m² = 3q², so q² = 3m², meaning 3 | q. But then 3 is a common factor of p and q, contradicting our assumption. ∴ √3 is irrational. ✓
SA 2 (2M) Find HCF and LCM of 510 and 92 using prime factorisation.
Ans: 510 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 17; 92 = 2² × 23. HCF = 2. LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 × 17 × 23 = 23460.

🧮 Polynomials
Ch 2 · Zeroes · Relationship with Coefficients · Division Algorithm

📖 Key Concepts

Zero of a Polynomial
Value of x for which p(x) = 0. Linear p(x) has 1 zero; quadratic has at most 2; cubic has at most 3.
Sum & Product of Zeroes
For ax² + bx + c: α + β = −b/a; α × β = c/a. Key shortcuts — memorise both!
Geometrical Meaning
Zero = x-intercept of the graph. Number of zeroes = number of times graph cuts x-axis.
Division Algorithm
p(x) = g(x) × q(x) + r(x), where degree(r) < degree(g). Same as Euclid but for polynomials.

🔑 Quadratic Zeroes Formulae

α + β = −b/a  |  αβ = c/a
Quadratic with zeroes α, β: x² − (α+β)x + αβ = 0
If zeroes are given, form: k[x² − (sum)x + (product)]

⚖️ Pair of Linear Equations
Ch 3 · Graphical · Substitution · Elimination · Cross-Multiplication

📖 Key Concepts

Consistent System
a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ → unique solution (lines intersect). a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂ → infinitely many solutions (coincident lines).
Inconsistent System
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ → no solution (lines are parallel). Graph shows parallel lines that never meet.
Substitution Method
Express one variable from one equation; substitute into the other. Best when a coefficient is 1.
Elimination Method
Multiply equations to make coefficients equal; add/subtract to eliminate one variable. Most reliable method.

🔑 Consistency Conditions

Unique solution: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂
No solution (parallel): a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂
Infinite solutions (same line): a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

📐 Quadratic Equations
Ch 4 · Factorisation · Quadratic Formula · Discriminant · Nature of Roots

📖 Key Concepts

Standard Form
ax² + bx + c = 0, a ≠ 0. Every quadratic has at most two real roots.
Quadratic Formula
x = [−b ± √(b²−4ac)] / 2a. Always works even when factorisation is hard.
Discriminant (D)
D = b²−4ac. D > 0: two distinct real roots. D = 0: two equal roots. D < 0: no real roots.
Completing the Square
Write ax²+bx+c = a(x+b/2a)² + (c−b²/4a). Useful method to derive the formula.

🔑 Key Formulae

x = [−b ± √(b²−4ac)] / 2a
D = b²−4ac: D>0 distinct; D=0 equal; D<0 no real roots
For equal roots: D = 0 → b² = 4ac

✏️ Worked — Discriminant (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: Find the nature of roots: 2x² − 4x + 3 = 0.

2

🔢 Arithmetic Progressions
Ch 5 · nth Term · Sum of n Terms · Identifying APs

📖 Key Concepts

Arithmetic Progression
Sequence where difference between consecutive terms is constant. Common difference d = a₂ − a₁ = a₃ − a₂.
nth Term (aₙ)
aₙ = a + (n−1)d, where a = first term, d = common difference. Last term l = a + (n−1)d.
Sum of n Terms (Sₙ)
Sₙ = n/2 × [2a + (n−1)d] = n/2 × (first + last term). Also: nth term = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁.
Three Terms in AP
Take as (a−d), a, (a+d). Four terms: (a−3d), (a−d), (a+d), (a+3d). Simplifies calculations.

🔑 AP Formulae

aₙ = a + (n−1)d
Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n−1)d] = n/2 (a + l)
d = (l−a)/(n−1)
nth term from end = l − (n−1)d

✏️ Worked — Sum problem (Board 2022, 4M)

Q: Find number of terms in AP 3, 7, 11... whose sum = 406.

1
a=3, d=4. Sₙ = n/2[6+(n−1)4] = n(2n+1).
2
n(2n+1)=406 → 2n²+n−406=0 → n=14 (taking positive root).
✅ 14 terms needed.

🔺 Triangles
Ch 6 · Similarity · Pythagoras Theorem · Criteria AA/SAS/SSS

📖 Key Concepts

Basic Proportionality Theorem
If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides proportionally. (Thales' Theorem)
Similarity Criteria
AA: two angles equal. SAS: two sides proportional with included angle equal. SSS: all three sides proportional.
Areas of Similar Triangles
Ratio of areas = square of ratio of corresponding sides. (area₁/area₂ = (side₁/side₂)²)
Pythagoras Theorem
In right △, (hypotenuse)² = sum of squares of other two sides. Converse: if a²+b²=c², it is a right triangle.

🔑 Key Results

BPT: DE ∥ BC ⟹ AD/DB = AE/EC
Area ratio: Δ₁/Δ₂ = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (CA/RP)²
Pythagoras: AC² = AB² + BC² (right angle at B)

🗺️ Coordinate Geometry
Ch 7 · Distance Formula · Section Formula · Area of Triangle

📖 Key Concepts

Distance Formula
Distance PQ = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]. Always positive. Used to show collinearity, equidistance, etc.
Section Formula
Point dividing P(x₁,y₁) and Q(x₂,y₂) in ratio m:n internally: x = (mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), y = (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n).
Midpoint Formula
Special case of section (1:1): midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2).
Area of Triangle
Area = ½ |x₁(y₂−y₃) + x₂(y₃−y₁) + x₃(y₁−y₂)|. Zero means points are collinear.

🔑 Key Formulae

Distance = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]
Section (m:n): x = (mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n)
Midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
Area = ½|x₁(y₂−y₃)+x₂(y₃−y₁)+x₃(y₁−y₂)|

📏 Introduction to Trigonometry
Ch 8 · Trig Ratios · Standard Angles · Complementary Angles · Identities

📖 Key Concepts

Six Trig Ratios
sin A = P/H; cos A = B/H; tan A = P/B; cosec A = H/P; sec A = H/B; cot A = B/P. (P=perpendicular, B=base, H=hypotenuse)
Standard Angles
0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°. sin grows 0→1; cos decreases 1→0 as angle increases from 0° to 90°.
Complementary Angles
sin(90°−A) = cos A; cos(90°−A) = sin A; tan(90°−A) = cot A. Used to simplify expressions.
Pythagorean Identities
sin²A + cos²A = 1 (most important!); 1 + tan²A = sec²A; 1 + cot²A = cosec²A.

🔑 Standard Values Table

sin: 0°=0, 30°=½, 45°=1/√2, 60°=√3/2, 90°=1
cos: 0°=1, 30°=√3/2, 45°=1/√2, 60°=½, 90°=0
tan: 0°=0, 30°=1/√3, 45°=1, 60°=√3, 90°=∞
Identities: sin²+cos²=1; sec²−tan²=1; cosec²−cot²=1

✏️ Worked — Identity Proof (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: Prove: (sin A − 2sin³A)/(2cos³A − cos A) = tan A.

1

2
= sinA(1−2sin²A) / cosA(2(1−sin²A)−1) = sinA(1−2sin²A) / cosA(1−2sin²A)
3
= sinA / cosA = tanA = RHS ✓
✅ Proved.

Angle between horizontal and the line of sight when looking up at an object. Observer is below object.

📖 Key Concepts

Tangent
Line touching circle at exactly one point (point of tangency). Always perpendicular to radius at that point.
Tangents from External Point
Two tangents from same external point are equal in length. If PA and PB are tangents, PA = PB.

Radius ⊥ tangent at point of contact
Tangents from external point are equal

📊 Statistics
Ch 13 · Mean · Median · Mode · Ogives

📖 Key Concepts

Mean (Grouped Data)
Direct Method: x̄ = Σfᵢxᵢ/Σfᵢ. Assumed Mean Method: x̄ = a + (Σfᵢdᵢ/Σfᵢ). xᵢ = class midpoint.
Median (Grouped)
Median = l + [(n/2−cf)/f] × h. l=lower class boundary; cf=cumulative freq before median class; f=freq of median class; h=class width.
Mode (Grouped)
Mode = l + [(f₁−f₀)/(2f₁−f₀−f₂)] × h. Modal class = class with highest frequency. f₁ = max freq.
Ogive
Cumulative frequency curve. Less-than ogive: plot (upper limit, cf). More-than ogive: plot (lower limit, more-than cf). Intersection point gives median.

🔑 Key Formulae

Mean: x̄ = Σfᵢxᵢ/Σfᵢ (direct method)
Median: l + [(n/2−cf)/f] × h
Mode: l + [(f₁−f₀)/(2f₁−f₀−f₂)] × h
Empirical: Mode = 3×Median − 2×Mean

✏️ Worked — Mode (Board 2023, 3M)

🎲 Probability
Ch 14 · Classical Probability · Complementary Events · Cards & Dice

📖 Key Concepts

Classical Probability
P(E) = (Number of favourable outcomes) / (Total possible outcomes). Always between 0 and 1.
Complementary Event
P(not E) = 1 − P(E). P(E) + P(Ē) = 1 always.
Equally Likely Outcomes
A fair die has 6 equally likely outcomes. A fair coin has 2 equally likely outcomes. Standard deck: 52 equally likely cards.
Impossible & Sure Events
Impossible event: P = 0. Sure (certain) event: P = 1. All other probabilities are strictly between 0 and 1.

🔑 Common Probability Facts

P(E) = Favourable/Total; 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
Standard deck: 52 cards; 4 suits; 13 per suit; 12 face cards; 4 aces
Die: sample space {1,2,3,4,5,6}; P(even) = 3/6 = ½
Two coins: sample space {HH, HT, TH, TT}; P(at least 1H) = 3/4

✏️ Worked — Card Problem (Board 2023, 2M)

Quadratic Formula — Sign Error in Discriminant
Mistake: Computing b²−4ac as (−5)²−4(1)(6) = 25−24 = 1 but then writing x = −b ± √1 / 2a with wrong sign of b.
Fix: Write the formula explicitly: x = [−(−5) ± √1] / 2 → [5 ± 1]/2 = 3 or 2. Box the formula first, substitute second.
Statistics — Wrong Class Mark
Mistake: For class 10–20, using class mark = 10 instead of 15 (lower + upper limit divided by 2).
Fix: Class mark = (lower + upper) / 2 always. For 10–20 → (10+20)/2 = 15. This error affects the entire mean calculation.
Probability — Not Simplifying Fraction
Mistake: Writing P = 4/52 without simplifying to 1/13 — CBSE marking scheme shows simplified form; unsimplified may lose ½ mark.
Fix: Always simplify probability fractions. Check HCF of numerator and denominator before writing final answer.
BPT — Mixing AD/DB with AB/DB
Mistake: In Basic Proportionality Theorem, writing AD/AB = AE/AC instead of AD/DB = AE/EC.
Fix: BPT states: if DE ∥ BC then AD/DB = AE/EC (parts, not whole). Draw the diagram and label before applying formula.
Circles — Tangent Length Formula
Mistake: Forgetting that tangent from external point: PA² = PC × PB (power of a point) or PA = PB (equal tangents from same external point).
Fix: Equal tangents from external point is used in 90% of CBSE circle tangent problems. Draw the figure — PT² = PO² − r².

👁️ Examiner's Eye — Mathematics Basic
What the CBSE Maths Basic examiner checks — how to earn step marks
Step Marks — How They Work
3-mark question: 1 mark for correct method/formula + 1 mark for substitution + 1 mark for answer.
5-mark question: 1 mark formula + 2 marks working + 1 mark correct steps + 1 mark final answer.
If final answer is wrong but all steps are correct → 4/5 marks. Never leave a question blank.
Proof Questions — What Examiner Checks
Mandatory: "Given", "To Prove", and "Proof" headings. Missing "Given" or "To Prove" → −1 mark. The proof must use valid mathematical steps — circular proofs (assuming what you need to prove) get zero.
Construction Questions
Examiner checks: Correct arc marks visible (don't erase them), angles within ±1°, lengths within ±1mm. Write steps of construction below the figure. Each step = ½ to 1 mark. Pencil + compass only — no freehand allowed.
Case-Based Questions (4 marks each)
Pattern: A real-life scenario + 4 sub-questions (1 mark each). Read the scenario once, underline key measurements. The 4 questions are usually: formula identification, substitution, calculation, interpretation.
These are the easiest marks in the paper — don't skip them.
Diagram and Labelling
Mandatory for: All geometry proofs, trigonometry height/distance, surface area problems. Unlabelled or missing diagram = −1 mark in 5-mark questions. Draw neatly but quickly — 30 seconds per diagram.
MCQ and Assertion-Reason
No step marks here — right or wrong. Common trap in Assertion-Reason: both A and R are true but R does NOT explain A → answer is (b), not (a). For each A-R question, check: (1) Is A true? (2) Is R true? (3) If both true, does R justify A? Answer logically.
🎯 Maths Basic Examiner Secret: Write units in every answer — cm², m³, degrees. "96π cm²" not just "96π". Missing units can cost ½ mark per question. In a 5-mark question with 4 sub-answers, 4 missing units = 2 marks lost.
🔥 High-Probability — Mathematics Basic
Topics that appear in Maths Basic board every year — don't miss these
📌 Probability — 100% probability
Every year: 1-mark card probability, 3-mark two-dice sum problem, case-based probability.
Must know: P(event) = favourable outcomes / total outcomes. Cards: 52 total, 4 suits of 13 each. Dice: 36 outcomes for two dice. These are calculation-free if sample space is memorised.
📌 Statistics (Mean/Median/Mode) — 100% probability
Every year: Find mean using assumed mean method (3 marks), mode from frequency table (1 mark), median using ogive (5 marks).
Formulas: Mean = A + Σfd/Σf; Mode = l + [(f₁−f₀)/(2f₁−f₀−f₂)]×h; Median = l + [(n/2−cf)/f]×h
📌 Quadratic Equations — 92% probability
Every year: Solve by factorisation (2 marks) + word problem (5 marks).
Word problems: Train speed, age problems, rectangles. Identify: let x = ?, form equation, solve by factorisation or formula, reject negative root if needed.
📌 Trigonometric Identities — 90% probability
Every year: Prove identity (3 marks) + evaluate expression using standard angles (1-2 marks).
Must know: sin 30°=½, cos 30°=√3/2, tan 45°=1, sin 90°=1, cos 0°=1. Memorise 5 identities and use them in proofs.
📌 Surface Area & Volume — 88% probability
Every year: Cylinder + hemisphere or cone + cylinder combined volume (5 marks).
Formula list: CSA cylinder = 2πrh; CSA cone = πrl; SA sphere = 4πr²; Volume: πr²h (cyl), ⅓πr²h (cone), ⁴⁄₃πr³ (sphere).
📌 Similar Triangles / BPT — 85% probability
Every year: BPT application (2 marks) + similarity condition proof (3-5 marks).
Must know: AA, SSS, SAS similarity criteria. Ratio of areas = (ratio of sides)². Pythagoras theorem for right triangles.
📌 Arithmetic Progressions — 90% probability
Every year: Find nth term (1-2 marks) + sum of n terms (3 marks) + word problem on AP (5 marks).
Formulas: aₙ = a + (n−1)d; Sₙ = n/2 × [2a + (n−1)d] or n/2 × (a + l). Always identify a, d, n first.
📌 HCF/LCM + Proof of Irrationality — 88% probability
Every year: HCF by prime factorisation (2 marks) + prove √2 or √3 irrational (3 marks).
Proof structure: Assume √2 = p/q (rational, HCF(p,q)=1) → 2 divides p → 2 divides p → 2 divides q → contradiction → irrational. This exact proof appears 4/6 years.
