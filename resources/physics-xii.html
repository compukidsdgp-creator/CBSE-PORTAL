⚡ Chapter 1: Electric Charges & Fields
Coulomb's Law · Electric Field · Dipole · Gauss's Theorem

📖 Core Theory

1.1 Electric Charge

Electric charge is an intrinsic property of matter. It exists in two types: positive (proton, +e) and negative (electron, −e). Charge is quantised — q = ne where n is an integer and e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Charge is conserved in all processes (the algebraic sum of charges in an isolated system remains constant).

Quantisation of Charge
Charge exists only in integral multiples of e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C. q = ne
Conservation of Charge
Total charge in an isolated system is always constant. Charge can neither be created nor destroyed.
Additive Nature
Total charge = algebraic sum of individual charges. +3C + (−1C) = +2C
Point Charge
A hypothetical charge concentrated at a single geometric point — used for simplification in Coulomb's law.

1.2 Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force between two point charges q₁ and q₂ separated by distance r in a medium of permittivity ε is:

🔑 Key Formulae — Coulomb's Law

F = k·q₁·q₂ / r²    where k = 1/(4πε₀) = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²
ε₀ = 8.854×10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²)  (permittivity of free space)
In medium: F_med = F_vac / ε_r   (ε_r = relative permittivity/dielectric constant)
Vector form: F⃗₁₂ = k·q₁·q₂·r̂₁₂ / r²

1.3 Electric Field

Electric field at a point is the force per unit positive test charge placed at that point. It is a vector field.

🔑 Electric Field Formulae

E⃗ = F⃗/q₀   (definition)
E = kq/r²   (due to point charge q at distance r)
E_dipole (axial) = 2kp / r³
E_dipole (equatorial) = kp / r³
Torque on dipole in uniform field: τ = pE sinθ  or  τ⃗ = p⃗ × E⃗

1.4 Gauss's Theorem

The total electric flux through any closed surface (Gaussian surface) equals the net charge enclosed divided by ε₀. This is one of Maxwell's four equations.

🔑 Gauss's Law & Applications

Φ = ∮E⃗·dA⃗ = q_enc / ε₀
Infinite line charge (λ C/m): E = λ/(2πε₀r)
Infinite plane sheet (σ C/m²): E = σ/(2ε₀)
Uniformly charged sphere (outside): E = kQ/r²
Uniformly charged sphere (inside): E = kQr/R³
Conducting sphere surface: E = σ/ε₀

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1 — Coulomb's Law (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: Two point charges +4 μC and −3 μC are placed 20 cm apart in air. Find the force between them.

1
Identify: q₁ = +4×10⁻⁶ C, q₂ = −3×10⁻⁶ C, r = 0.20 m, k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²
2
Apply Coulomb's law: F = k|q₁||q₂|/r² = 9×10⁹ × 4×10⁻⁶ × 3×10⁻⁶ / (0.20)²
3
Numerator = 9×10⁹ × 12×10⁻¹² = 108×10⁻³ = 0.108 N·m²
4
Denominator = (0.20)² = 0.04 m²
5
F = 0.108 / 0.04 = 2.7 N (attractive, since charges are opposite)
✅ Answer: F = 2.7 N (attractive force)

Example 2 — Electric Field due to Dipole (Board 2022, 5M)

Q: An electric dipole of moment p = 2×10⁻⁹ C·m is placed along the x-axis. Find E at axial and equatorial points both 30 cm away.

1
Given: p = 2×10⁻⁹ C·m, r = 0.30 m, k = 9×10⁹
2
Axial field: E_ax = 2kp/r³ = 2×9×10⁹×2×10⁻⁹ / (0.30)³
3
= 36 / 0.027 = 1333 N/C (along dipole direction)
4
Equatorial field: E_eq = kp/r³ = 9×10⁹×2×10⁻⁹ / 0.027
5
= 18 / 0.027 = 667 N/C (opposite to dipole direction)
✅ E_axial = 1333 N/C; E_equatorial = 667 N/C = E_axial/2

Example 3 — Gauss's Law (Board 2024, 5M)

Q: Using Gauss's theorem, derive the expression for E due to a uniformly charged infinite plane sheet of surface charge density σ.

1
Choose a cylindrical Gaussian surface (pillbox) symmetric about the sheet, with each flat face of area A parallel to the sheet.
2
By symmetry, E is perpendicular to the sheet. Flux through curved surface = 0 (E ⊥ dA).
3
Total flux: Φ = E·A + E·A = 2EA (two flat faces)
4
Charge enclosed: q_enc = σA
5
Gauss's law: 2EA = σA/ε₀ ⟹ E = σ/(2ε₀)
✅ E = σ/(2ε₀), directed away from sheet on both sides

🔲 Chapter 2: Electrostatic Potential & Capacitance
Potential · Equipotential Surfaces · Capacitors · Dielectrics

📖 Core Theory

2.1 Electric Potential

Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a test charge from infinity to that point, without acceleration. It is a scalar quantity.

🔑 Potential Formulae

V = W/q₀   (definition)
V = kq/r   (due to point charge at distance r)
V_dipole (axial) = kp/r²
V_dipole (equatorial) = 0   (always)
Relation: E = −dV/dr  (E is negative gradient of V)
Potential difference: V_A − V_B = W_AB/q

2.2 Capacitance & Capacitors

A capacitor stores electrical energy in the form of an electric field. Capacitance C = Q/V. For a parallel plate capacitor with plate area A, separation d:

🔑 Capacitance Formulae

C = Q/V   (definition)
Parallel plate (air): C₀ = ε₀A/d
Parallel plate (dielectric K): C = Kε₀A/d = KC₀
Series: 1/C_eff = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + …   (V divides, Q same)
Parallel: C_eff = C₁ + C₂ + …   (Q divides, V same)
Energy stored: U = ½CV² = Q²/(2C) = QV/2
Energy density: u = ½ε₀E²

2.3 Equipotential Surfaces

An equipotential surface is a surface where every point is at the same potential. Key property: the electric field is always perpendicular to equipotential surfaces. No work is done moving a charge along an equipotential surface.

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1 — Capacitor Energy (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: A 4 μF capacitor is charged to 200 V. Find (a) charge stored, (b) energy stored.

1
C = 4×10⁻⁶ F, V = 200 V
2
Charge: Q = CV = 4×10⁻⁶ × 200 = 8×10⁻⁴ C = 0.8 mC
3
Energy: U = ½CV² = ½ × 4×10⁻⁶ × (200)² = ½ × 4×10⁻⁶ × 40000
4
U = 2×10⁻⁶ × 40000 = 0.08 J = 80 mJ
✅ Q = 0.8 mC; U = 80 mJ

Example 2 — Series-Parallel Combination (Board 2022, 5M)

Q: Three capacitors of 2 μF, 3 μF, and 6 μF are connected in series across a 12 V battery. Find (a) equivalent capacitance, (b) charge on each capacitor.

1
Series: 1/C_eff = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 3/6 + 2/6 + 1/6 = 6/6 = 1
2
C_eff = 1 μF
3
In series, charge is same on all: Q = C_eff × V = 1×10⁻⁶ × 12 = 12 μC
4
V₁ = Q/C₁ = 12/2 = 6 V; V₂ = 12/3 = 4 V; V₃ = 12/6 = 2 V. Check: 6+4+2 = 12 V ✓
✅ C_eff = 1 μF; Q on each = 12 μC

Example 3 — Effect of Dielectric (Board 2024, 3M)

Q: A parallel plate capacitor (A = 100 cm², d = 2 mm) is connected to 100 V battery. A dielectric slab of K = 5 is inserted. Find new capacitance and energy stored.

1
A = 100×10⁻⁴ m² = 10⁻² m²; d = 2×10⁻³ m; K = 5
2
C₀ = ε₀A/d = 8.85×10⁻¹² × 10⁻² / 2×10⁻³ = 44.25×10⁻¹² F = 44.25 pF
3
New C = KC₀ = 5 × 44.25 pF = 221.25 pF
4
U = ½CV² = ½ × 221.25×10⁻¹² × (100)² = 1.106 × 10⁻⁶ J ≈ 1.1 μJ
✅ C = 221.25 pF; U = 1.1 μJ

🔋 Chapter 3: Current Electricity
Ohm's Law · Drift Velocity · Cells · Kirchhoff's Laws · Wheatstone Bridge

📖 Core Theory

3.1 Electric Current and Drift Velocity

Electric current is the rate of flow of charge. In a metallic conductor, free electrons drift under an applied electric field with an average drift velocity v_d. The relation between current and drift velocity is fundamental.

🔑 Current & Drift Formulae

I = Q/t = nAev_d   (n = no. density of electrons)
v_d = eEτ/m = eVτ/(mL)   (τ = relaxation time)
Ohm's Law: V = IR
Resistivity: ρ = m/(ne²τ)   (temperature dependent)
R = ρL/A   (resistance of wire)
ρ_T = ρ₀(1 + αT)   (α = temp. coefficient of resistance)

3.2 EMF, Internal Resistance & Cell Combinations

A cell maintains a potential difference by doing work on charges. EMF (ε) is the work done per unit charge through the cell. Terminal voltage V = ε − Ir (during discharge) where r is internal resistance.

🔑 Cell Combination Formulae

Terminal PD (discharge): V = ε − Ir
Terminal PD (charge): V = ε + Ir
n cells in series: ε_eq = nε; r_eq = nr; I = nε/(R + nr)
n cells in parallel: ε_eq = ε; r_eq = r/n; I = ε/(R + r/n)
Mixed (m rows, n in each): I = mnε/(mR + nr)

3.3 Kirchhoff's Laws

KCL (Junction Rule): Sum of currents entering a junction = sum leaving. Based on conservation of charge.
KVL (Loop Rule): The algebraic sum of changes in potential around any closed loop is zero. Based on conservation of energy.

3.4 Wheatstone Bridge & Meter Bridge

🔑 Bridge Conditions

Balanced Wheatstone: P/Q = R/S (no current through galvanometer)
Meter bridge: R/S = l/(100−l)   (l = balancing length in cm)
Potentiometer (EMF comparison): ε₁/ε₂ = l₁/l₂
Internal resistance: r = R(l₁−l₂)/l₂

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1 — Drift Velocity (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: A copper wire of length 2 m, cross-section 2×10⁻⁶ m² carries a current of 3 A. Find the drift velocity. (n = 8.5×10²⁸ m⁻³)

1
I = nAev_d → v_d = I/(nAe)
2
v_d = 3 / (8.5×10²⁸ × 2×10⁻⁶ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹)
3
Denominator = 8.5×10²⁸ × 2×10⁻⁶ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 27.2×10³ = 2.72×10⁴
4
v_d = 3 / 2.72×10⁴ ≈ 1.1×10⁻⁴ m/s = 0.11 mm/s
✅ v_d ≈ 1.1×10⁻⁴ m/s (extremely slow — electrons take minutes to travel 1 m!)

Example 2 — Kirchhoff's Laws (Board 2022, 5M)

Q: In a circuit, two cells: ε₁ = 6V, r₁ = 1Ω and ε₂ = 4V, r₂ = 2Ω connected in opposition with external R = 5Ω. Find current.

1
Net EMF = ε₁ − ε₂ = 6 − 4 = 2 V (cells oppose each other)
2
Total resistance = R + r₁ + r₂ = 5 + 1 + 2 = 8 Ω
3
I = Net EMF / Total R = 2/8 = 0.25 A
4
Current flows in direction of stronger cell (ε₁)
✅ I = 0.25 A in the direction of the 6V cell

Example 3 — Meter Bridge / Internal Resistance (Board 2024, 5M)

Q: In a potentiometer, the balancing length for a cell of EMF 1.25 V is 50 cm. When a resistance of 2Ω is connected across the cell, the balance length becomes 40 cm. Find internal resistance of the cell.

1
Formula: r = R(l₁ − l₂)/l₂
2
l₁ = 50 cm (open circuit), l₂ = 40 cm (with R = 2Ω), R = 2Ω
3
r = 2 × (50 − 40)/40 = 2 × 10/40 = 2 × 0.25 = 0.5 Ω
✅ Internal resistance r = 0.5 Ω

🧲 Chapter 4: Moving Charges & Magnetism
Biot-Savart Law · Ampere's Law · Force on Charges · Cyclotron · Galvanometer

📖 Core Theory

4.1 Magnetic Force on Charges & Conductors

🔑 Key Formulae

F⃗ = q(v⃗ × B⃗) = qvB sinθ   (force on moving charge)
F⃗ = I(L⃗ × B⃗) = BIL sinθ   (force on current-carrying conductor)
Force between parallel wires: F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd)
Torque on loop: τ = NIAB sinθ  or  τ⃗ = M⃗ × B⃗
Magnetic moment: M = NIA   (N turns, area A)

4.2 Biot-Savart Law

🔑 Biot-Savart & Applications

dB = (μ₀/4π) × I dl sinθ / r²   (magnetic field due to element)
B at centre of circular loop (radius R): B = μ₀I/(2R)
B on axis of circular loop: B = μ₀IR²/[2(R²+x²)^(3/2)]
B due to long straight wire at distance r: B = μ₀I/(2πr)
μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A  (permeability of free space)

4.3 Ampere's Circuital Law & Solenoid

🔑 Ampere's Law & Solenoid/Toroid

∮B⃗·dl⃗ = μ₀I_enc   (Ampere's law)
B inside solenoid (n turns/m): B = μ₀nI
B inside toroid: B = μ₀NI/(2πr)  (N total turns, r = radius)

4.4 Cyclotron & Galvanometer

Cyclotron: charged particles spiral outward in a magnetic field, accelerated by an electric field. Frequency is independent of velocity (resonance condition). A galvanometer measures small currents; can be converted to ammeter (low shunt S) or voltmeter (high series R).

🔑 Cyclotron & Meter Conversions

Cyclotron frequency: f = qB/(2πm)   (independent of speed!)
Cyclotron radius: r = mv/(qB)
Shunt for ammeter: S = I_g·G/(I−I_g)
Series R for voltmeter: R_s = V/I_g − G

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1 — Magnetic Field at Centre of Loop (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: A circular coil of 50 turns and radius 10 cm carries a current of 2 A. Find B at centre.

1
B = μ₀NI/(2R) = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 50 × 2)/(2 × 0.10)
2
= (4π×10⁻⁷ × 100) / 0.20 = (4π×10⁻⁵) / 0.20
3
= 20π×10⁻⁵ = 6.28×10⁻⁴ T = 0.628 mT
✅ B = 6.28×10⁻⁴ T, directed perpendicular to the plane of the coil

Example 2 — Galvanometer to Ammeter (Board 2022, 3M)

Q: A galvanometer (G = 25 Ω, I_g = 10 mA) is to be converted to an ammeter reading up to 5 A. Find shunt resistance.

1
S = I_g × G / (I − I_g) = (0.01 × 25)/(5 − 0.01)
2
= 0.25/4.99 ≈ 0.0501 Ω ≈ 0.05 Ω
✅ Shunt S = 0.05 Ω (very small, so it doesn't affect circuit)
📌 Always use right-hand rule to find direction of force. In board exams, direction must be stated explicitly to score full marks.

Bar Magnet · Earth's Magnetism · Diamagnetic / Paramagnetic / Ferromagnetic

PE of dipole: U = −MB cosθ
Intensity of magnetisation: I = M/V
Magnetic susceptibility: χ = I/H; Permeability: μ_r = 1 + χ
Curie's law (paramagnetic): χ ∝ 1/T
Diamagnetic
Paramagnetic
Weakly repelled by magnet
Weakly attracted by magnet
χ is small negative (−1 ≤ χ < 0)
χ is small positive (0 < χ < ε)

♾️ Chapter 6: Electromagnetic Induction
Faraday's Law · Lenz's Law · Motional EMF · Self & Mutual Inductance

📖 Core Theory

6.1 Faraday's Laws

1st Law: Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an EMF is induced.
2nd Law: The magnitude of induced EMF = rate of change of magnetic flux.
Lenz's Law: The direction of induced current opposes the change that caused it (conservation of energy).

🔑 EMI Formulae

Magnetic flux: Φ = NBA cosθ   (N turns, B field, A area)
Induced EMF: ε = −dΦ/dt = −N·dΦ_per_turn/dt
Motional EMF: ε = Bvl   (conductor of length l moving at v⊥B)
Self-inductance: L = NΦ/I   (flux linkage per unit current)
Self-induced EMF: ε = −L·dI/dt
Energy in inductor: U = ½LI²
Mutual inductance: M₁₂ = N₂Φ₂₁/I₁  ; ε₂ = −M·dI₁/dt
Solenoid inductance: L = μ₀N²A/l = μ₀n²Al

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1 — Motional EMF (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: A conductor of length 0.5 m moves at 4 m/s perpendicular to a magnetic field B = 0.2 T. Find induced EMF and current if resistance = 0.4 Ω.

1
ε = Bvl = 0.2 × 4 × 0.5 = 0.4 V
2
I = ε/R = 0.4/0.4 = 1 A
✅ EMF = 0.4 V; Current = 1 A

Example 2 — Self Inductance of Solenoid (Board 2022, 3M)

Q: A solenoid has 500 turns, length 0.5 m, area 4 cm². Find self-inductance.

1
L = μ₀N²A/l = 4π×10⁻⁷ × (500)² × 4×10⁻⁴ / 0.5
2
= 4π×10⁻⁷ × 250000 × 8×10⁻⁴ = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 200 = 800π×10⁻⁷
3
= 2513×10⁻⁷ ≈ 2.51×10⁻⁴ H = 0.251 mH
✅ L ≈ 0.25 mH
📌 Lenz's law direction is crucial — always explain why the induced current creates a force opposing the change. This qualitative reasoning is worth 2 marks in every derivation question.

Power: P = V_rms·I_rms·cosφ  (cosφ = power factor)
Transformer: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p = I_p/I_s  (ideal transformer)

Capacitor in AC
V and I in phase
I leads V by 90°

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1 — LCR Circuit Impedance (Board 2023, 5M)

Q: A series LCR circuit has R = 10 Ω, L = 0.5 H, C = 40 μF, connected to 220 V, 50 Hz supply. Find Z, I_rms, phase angle.

1
ω = 2πf = 2π×50 = 314 rad/s
2

💻 Chapter 14: Semiconductor Electronics
Intrinsic & Extrinsic · p-n Junction · Diode · Transistor · Logic Gates

📖 Core Theory

14.1 Semiconductors

Semiconductors have conductivity between conductors and insulators. In intrinsic semiconductors (pure Si, Ge), n_e = n_h. Extrinsic semiconductors are doped: n-type (donor impurity like P, As — extra electrons) or p-type (acceptor impurity like B, In — extra holes).

p-n Junction
Junction of p-type and n-type semiconductor. At junction, diffusion creates a depletion layer with built-in potential barrier (~0.7V for Si).
Forward Bias
p connected to +ve, n to −ve terminal. Barrier reduces; current flows. Acts as closed switch.
Reverse Bias
p connected to −ve, n to +ve terminal. Barrier increases; only tiny reverse saturation current flows. Acts as open switch.
Zener Diode
Designed to operate in reverse breakdown. Used as voltage regulator. Breakdown voltage called Zener voltage (V_Z).

14.2 Transistor

A transistor (BJT) has three regions: Emitter (heavily doped), Base (thin, lightly doped), Collector (moderately doped). Used as amplifier or switch.

❌ Common Mistakes Bank
The errors that cost students marks every year — and how to fix them

Mistake 1 — Wrong Sign in Mirror/Lens Formula

Students forget the sign convention and get the wrong image position, losing 3–5 marks.

❌ WRONG: For concave mirror, f = +20 cm → 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/20 − 1/(-30)
✅ CORRECT: For concave mirror, f = −20 cm (negative in Cartesian convention). Always write convention first: "All distances measured from pole; direction of incident light = positive"

Mistake 2 — Confusing E and V for Dipole

At equatorial point: E ≠ 0 but V = 0. Students reverse this and lose 2 marks.

❌ WRONG: Electric field at equatorial point of dipole = 0 (since V = 0 there)
✅ CORRECT: V = 0 at equatorial point, but E = kp/r³ ≠ 0 (the field points opposite to dipole moment). V = 0 does NOT mean E = 0.

Mistake 3 — Wrong Resonant Frequency Formula

❌ WRONG: f₀ = 1/(2πLC) or f₀ = 1/(√LC)
✅ CORRECT: f₀ = 1/(2π√LC). At resonance, X_L = X_C: ω₀L = 1/(ω₀C) → ω₀ = 1/√(LC) → f₀ = ω₀/(2π) = 1/(2π√LC)

Mistake 4 — Intensity vs KE in Photoelectric Effect

❌ WRONG: Increasing light intensity increases KE of photoelectrons
✅ CORRECT: Increasing intensity increases the NUMBER of photoelectrons (photocurrent), NOT their KE. KE depends only on frequency: KE_max = hf − φ. Stopping potential is independent of intensity.

Mistake 5 — Direction of Induced Current (Lenz's Law)

❌ WRONG: Stating direction without explaining WHY (no reference to opposing change)
✅ CORRECT: Always write: "By Lenz's law, the induced current flows in a direction such that the magnetic force it creates opposes the motion/change causing it." Then state the specific direction using right-hand rule.

Mistake 6 — Capacitor Energy Formula Error

❌ WRONG: U = CV² (missing the ½)
✅ CORRECT: U = ½CV² = Q²/(2C) = ½QV. All three forms are equivalent. The factor of ½ arises because charge and voltage build up gradually from zero.

Mistake 7 — Transistor Current Relations

❌ WRONG: I_C = I_B + I_E (wrong order — collector always ≫ base current)
✅ CORRECT: I_E = I_B + I_C. Emitter current splits into large collector current (α·I_E) and tiny base current ((1−α)·I_E). β = I_C/I_B is large (20–200).

Mistake 8 — Nuclear Decay Equations (Charge/Mass Not Balanced)

❌ WRONG: ²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + He (missing subscripts/charge numbers)
✅ CORRECT: ⁹²²³⁸U → ⁹⁰²³⁴Th + ₂⁴He. Both mass numbers (A) and atomic numbers (Z) must balance. Check: 238=234+4 ✓; 92=90+2 ✓. Write subscripts and superscripts ALWAYS.
👁️ Examiner's Eye
What board examiners look for — and what costs marks silently

🎯 Tip 1 — State the Law Before Using It

Every derivation question awards 1–2 marks just for stating the law. Never dive into math without writing: "According to Gauss's law / Faraday's law / Coulomb's law…" Examiners mark this as a separate step.

🎯 Tip 2 — Labelled Diagrams Are Compulsory

For any question asking you to "derive" or "explain" a device (transformer, cyclotron, microscope, potentiometer), a labelled diagram is worth 1–2 marks. The marking scheme explicitly allocates marks for it. Draw it first, then derive.

🎯 Tip 3 — Units in Every Numerical

The marking scheme awards 1 mark specifically for the correct unit in the final answer. "E = 2.7" scores 0 for that step. "E = 2.7 N/C" scores full. Units are not optional.

🎯 Tip 4 — Define Symbols in Formulae

When you write "F = kq₁q₂/r²" and then calculate, define what each symbol means. Examiners check that you know the formula, not just copied it. One line of definitions gets you the "formula mark".

🎯 Tip 5 — "State vs Derive" — Know the Difference

"State" = write in one sentence with no proof. "Derive" = full mathematical proof from basics. "Explain" = conceptual explanation in words. Giving a derivation when asked to "state" wastes time but doesn't lose marks. Giving only a statement when asked to "derive" loses all derivation marks.

🎯 Tip 6 — Phasor Diagrams for AC Questions

Any AC circuit question about LCR — even if not explicitly asked — should include a phasor diagram showing V_R (along I), V_L (90° ahead of I), V_C (90° behind I), and resultant V. This is worth 2 marks in the marking scheme and takes 20 seconds to draw.

🎯 Tip 7 — Direction of Magnetic Force

When F = qvB sinθ or F = BIL sinθ — always state the direction using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule or right-hand rule. "F = 0.4 N" without direction scores partial credit only.

🎯 Tip 8 — Two-Marks "Why" Questions

Board frequently asks "why does [device] work?" or "why is [material] used?" These need cause-effect answers: e.g. "Nichrome is used because it has high resistivity AND low temperature coefficient of resistance — both conditions needed." One condition alone = 1 mark instead of 2.

⚡ Electrostatics — Quick Hits

  • Coulomb's constant k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²; ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹² C²/N·m²
  • Gauss's law: Φ = q_enc/ε₀; E for infinite sheet = σ/(2ε₀)

  • Capacitor energy U = ½CV² = Q²/2C; energy density = ½ε₀E²
  • Battery connected → V constant, C increases with dielectric
  • Battery disconnected → Q constant, V decreases with dielectric

🔋 Current Electricity — Quick Hits

  • I = nAev_d; drift velocity is ~10⁻⁴ m/s (very slow!)
  • Terminal V = ε − Ir (discharge); = ε + Ir (charge)
  • Potentiometer internal resistance: r = R(l₁−l₂)/l₂
  • Wheatstone balanced: P/Q = R/S (no galvanometer deflection)
  • Resistivity increases with temperature for metals (α positive)

🧲 Magnetism + EMI — Quick Hits

  • B at centre of N-turn loop: B = μ₀NI/(2R)
  • B inside solenoid: B = μ₀nI (n = turns per metre)
  • Cyclotron frequency: f = qB/(2πm) — independent of speed!
  • Motional EMF: ε = Bvl; self-induced: ε = −L·dI/dt
  • Energy in inductor: U = ½LI²; solenoid L = μ₀N²A/l
  • Lenz's law = conservation of energy (induced current opposes change)

〰️ AC + EM Waves — Quick Hits

  • V_rms = V₀/√2; I_rms = I₀/√2
  • Z = √[R² + (X_L − X_C)²]; at resonance Z_min = R
  • Resonant frequency f₀ = 1/(2π√LC)
  • Transformer: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p; step-up voltage → step-down current
  • EM spectrum order: Radio → Micro → IR → Visible → UV → X → Gamma
  • All EM waves travel at c = 3×10⁸ m/s in vacuum

🔭 Optics — Quick Hits

  • Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f; lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
  • Concave mirror f = −R/2; Convex lens f = positive
  • TIR: sinθ_c = n₂/n₁ (n₁ > n₂); occurs when light goes from denser to rarer
  • YDSE fringe width β = λD/d; bright fringe at nλD/d
  • Malus's law: I = I₀cos²θ; Brewster's law: tanθ_B = n

⚛️ Modern Physics — Quick Hits

  • Photoelectric: KE_max = hf − φ; stopping potential eV₀ = KE_max
  • de Broglie: λ = h/mv; for electron: λ = 1.227/√V nm
  • Bohr: E_n = −13.6/n² eV; r_n = 0.529n² Å
  • Radioactivity: N = N₀/2ⁿ (n half-lives); Activity A = λN
