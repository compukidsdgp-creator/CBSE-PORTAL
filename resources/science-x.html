⚗️ Chemistry
Chemical Reactions · Acids & Bases · Metals · Carbon · Periodic Table

📖 Core Theory

Ch 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations

A chemical reaction involves breaking and forming bonds to produce new substances. Reactions are classified by the change that occurs: combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, and redox reactions. Reactions must be balanced — atoms of each element must be equal on both sides (Law of Conservation of Mass).

Combination
Two or more substances combine to form one product. A + B → AB. Example: 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO.
Decomposition
Single compound breaks down. AB → A + B. Thermal (heat), electrolytic (electricity), photolytic (light). Example: 2H2O → 2H2 + O2.
Displacement
More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal from its salt solution. Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu.
Double Displacement
Exchange of ions between two compounds. NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H2O. Produces precipitate, gas, or water.
Oxidation
Gain of oxygen OR loss of hydrogen OR loss of electrons.
Reduction
Loss of oxygen OR gain of hydrogen OR gain of electrons. Redox: both happen simultaneously.

🔑 Key Reactions — Class X Chemistry

Quicklime: CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + Heat (slaked lime — exothermic)
Photosynthesis: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2 (light energy)
Thermite reaction: Fe2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Fe + Heat
Bleaching powder: Ca(OH)2 + Cl2 → CaOCl2 + H2O

Ch 2: Acids, Bases and Salts

Acids produce H+ ions in water (turn litmus red, react with metals to give H2). Bases produce OH- ions (turn litmus blue). Neutralisation: Acid + Base → Salt + Water. pH scale 0–14: below 7 acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic.

Strong Acid
Fully dissociates: HCl, H2SO4, HNO3. Low pH. Corrosive.
Weak Acid
Partially dissociates: CH3COOH (acetic acid), carbonic acid. Higher pH than strong acid at same concentration.
Baking Soda
NaHCO3 — mild base. Used in cooking. Reacts with acid to release CO2.
Washing Soda
Na2CO3·10H2O — used in glass, soap, paper making. Removes permanent hardness of water.

🔑 Key Reactions — Acids and Bases

Dilution of acid: ALWAYS add acid to water slowly (never water to acid — exothermic)
Metal + Acid → Salt + H2 gas (Zn + H2SO4 → ZnSO4 + H2)
Metal oxide + Acid → Salt + Water (CuO + H2SO4 → CuSO4 + H2O)
Plaster of Paris: CaSO4 · 0.5H2O + 1.5H2O → CaSO4·2H2O (setting)

Ch 3: Metals and Non-metals

Metals are lustrous, malleable, ductile, good conductors, and form basic oxides. Non-metals are brittle, poor conductors, and form acidic oxides. Reactivity series determines which metals displace others. Ionic bonding: metal donates electron to non-metal.

Reactivity Series
K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu, Hg, Ag, Au, Pt (most to least reactive).
Corrosion
Slow oxidation of metal surface. Rusting of iron: Fe + O2 + H2O → Fe2O3·xH2O. Prevented by painting, galvanising, alloying.
Thermite Reaction
More reactive metal (Al) reduces oxide of less reactive metal (Fe2O3). Used in welding railway tracks.
Ore vs Mineral
Mineral: naturally occurring compound. Ore: mineral from which a metal can be profitably extracted.

Ch 4: Carbon and its Compounds

Carbon forms 4 covalent bonds — tetravalent. Catenation: carbon chains with itself. Huge variety of organic compounds (hydrocarbons, alcohols, acids, esters). Homologous series: compounds with same functional group differing by -CH2-.

Saturated HC
Alkanes (single bonds): methane CH4, ethane C2H6. Less reactive. Undergo substitution.
Unsaturated HC
Alkenes (double bond): ethene C2H4. Alkynes (triple bond): ethyne C2H2. More reactive. Undergo addition.
Ethanol
C2H5OH. Drinking alcohol. Burns cleanly. Reacts with Na to give H2. Oxidised to acetic acid.
Saponification
Hydrolysis of ester with NaOH to give soap (sodium salt of fatty acid) + glycerol. Base + fat/oil → soap.

🔑 Key Facts — Carbon Chemistry

Homologous series: each member differs by CH2 (14 mass units)
Functional groups: -OH (alcohol), -COOH (acid), -CHO (aldehyde), -CO- (ketone)
Esterification: Alcohol + Acid (H2SO4 catalyst) → Ester + Water
Soap: long hydrocarbon tail (hydrophobic) + ionic head (hydrophilic) — micelle formation

Ch 5: Periodic Classification

Mendeleev's Periodic Table arranged elements by atomic mass; left gaps for undiscovered elements. Modern Periodic Table arranged by atomic number (Moseley). Periods = 7 rows; Groups = 18 columns. Properties vary periodically.

Atomic Radius
Decreases across period (increased nuclear pull). Increases down group (more shells added).
Metallic Character
Decreases across period. Increases down group. Metals on left; non-metals on right.
Valence Electrons
Electrons in outermost shell. Determines group number (for groups 1–2 and 13–18). Determines reactivity.
Electronegativity
Increases across period (left to right). Decreases down group. Fluorine = highest electronegativity.

Q: A solution has pH = 2 and another has pH = 11. (a) Which is acid? (b) What colour will litmus show in each?

1
pH below 7 = acidic. pH = 2 is strongly acidic. pH = 11 is strongly basic (alkaline).
2
pH 2 solution: turns litmus RED. pH 11 solution: turns litmus BLUE.

Case — Acids and Bases: A student tests four solutions with universal indicator. Solution A turns red (pH 2), Solution B turns green (pH 7), Solution C turns blue (pH 10), Solution D turns yellow (pH 5). She then adds a few drops of Solution A to Solution C and observes the colour change. She also tests which solution reacts with a piece of zinc metal to release a gas that burns with a 'pop' sound.

Q1 (1M) Which solution is strongly acidic? Ans: Solution A (pH 2).
Q2 (1M) Which gas is released when zinc reacts with Solution A? Ans: Hydrogen gas (H2) — burns with a 'pop' sound.
Q3 (2M) When Solution A is added to Solution C, what will happen? Name the reaction. Ans: Solution A (acid, pH 2) reacts with Solution C (base, pH 10) — neutralisation reaction. Salt and water are produced. The pH will shift towards neutral (near 7 if equal moles). The indicator colour will change from blue towards green/yellow as the base is neutralised.
Q4 (1M) Which solution is neither acidic nor basic? Ans: Solution B (pH 7) — neutral.
SA (3M) What is a homologous series? Give two features with an example.
Ans: A homologous series is a group of organic compounds that have the same functional group, same general formula, and similar chemical properties — and whose consecutive members differ by one CH2 unit (14 mass units). Features: (1) Each successive member differs by CH2 in molecular formula and 14 in molecular mass. (2) Physical properties (melting point, boiling point, solubility) show gradual variation. (3) All members have the same functional group and similar chemical properties. Example: Alkanes: Methane (CH4), Ethane (C2H6), Propane (C3H8) — general formula CnH2n+2. All undergo substitution and combustion reactions.
LA (5M) Explain the activity series of metals and its significance. Give three practical applications.
Ans: The Activity Series (Reactivity Series) is an arrangement of metals in decreasing order of their chemical reactivity: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au > Pt. Significance: (1) A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution. (2) Metals above hydrogen displace it from dilute acids; those below hydrogen cannot. (3) The position in the series determines the method of extraction of the metal from its ore. Applications: (1) Displacement Reactions: Zinc displaces copper from copper sulphate solution (Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu) — explained by Zn being more reactive. (2) Metal Extraction: Highly reactive metals (Na, Ca, K) are extracted by electrolysis; moderately reactive (Zn, Fe) by reduction with carbon; least reactive (Cu, Ag, Au) found free in nature or easily reduced. (3) Thermite Reaction: Aluminium's high reactivity (above Fe in series) allows it to reduce iron oxide — used in welding railway tracks.

🧬 Biology

📖 Core Theory

Ch 6: Life Processes

Life processes maintain living state: nutrition (autotrophic/heterotrophic), respiration (aerobic/anaerobic), transportation, and excretion. Photosynthesis converts CO2 + H2O to glucose using light energy in chloroplasts.

Photosynthesis
6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2 in presence of sunlight and chlorophyll. Stomata take in CO2; roots absorb water.
Aerobic Respiration
Glucose + O2 → CO2 + H2O + 38 ATP. Occurs in mitochondria. Complete oxidation of glucose.
Anaerobic Respiration
Without O2. In muscles: glucose → lactic acid (causes cramps). In yeast: glucose → ethanol + CO2.
Double Circulation
Blood passes through heart twice per circuit. Pulmonary circulation (heart-lungs-heart). Systemic (heart-body-heart).

🔑 Key Facts — Life Processes

Nephron: functional unit of kidney. Glomerular filtration → reabsorption → secretion → urine
Dialysis: artificial kidney — blood cleaned by diffusion through semipermeable membrane
Transpiration: evaporation of water from stomata — creates suction for water movement up xylem
Peristalsis: wave-like muscular contractions move food through alimentary canal

Ch 8: How do Organisms Reproduce?

Reproduction is essential for species survival. Asexual: binary fission (Amoeba), budding (Hydra), regeneration (Planaria), vegetative propagation (plants), spore formation (Rhizopus). Sexual: involves gametes, fertilisation, and offspring with genetic variation.

Binary Fission
Cell divides into two. Amoeba (irregular), Leishmania (longitudinal), Plasmodium (multiple fission).
Pollination
Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. Self (same flower); Cross (different flower). Agents: wind, insects, water.
Sexually Transmitted Diseases
HIV/AIDS, gonorrhoea, syphilis, warts. Prevented by condom use. Spread through sexual contact, blood.
Contraception
Barrier (condom, diaphragm), chemical (pills), surgical (vasectomy, tubectomy). Controls population growth.

Ch 9: Heredity and Evolution

Mendel's laws: Law of Dominance, Law of Segregation. Traits inherited through genes on chromosomes. Acquired traits (Lamarck) NOT inherited — Weismann's experiment with mice. Evolution: gradual change in heritable traits over generations.

Dominant/Recessive
Dominant allele (T) expresses over recessive (t). Tt = tall (dominant expressed). tt = short.
Sex Determination
Humans: XX = female, XY = male. Y chromosome from father determines sex of offspring.
Homologous Organs
Same basic structure, different function — indicate common ancestry. Human arm, whale flipper, bat wing, frog forelimb.
Analogous Organs
Different structure, same function — evolved separately. Bird wing and insect wing. Indicate convergent evolution.

Ch 15: Our Environment / Ch 16: Management of Natural Resources

Food Chain
Producers → Herbivores → Carnivores → Decomposers. Energy lost at each level (~90%). 10% rule.
Biodegradable
Can be broken down by bacteria. Paper, vegetable peels. Non-biodegradable: plastics, DDT, heavy metals.
Ozone Depletion
CFCs destroy ozone layer → UV radiation increases → skin cancer, mutation. Montreal Protocol limits CFCs.
Chipko Movement
1970s, Uttarakhand — villagers hugged trees to prevent felling. Sustainable forest management.

Q: A tall plant (TT) is crossed with a short plant (tt). What is the F1 phenotype? On self-crossing F1, what is the F2 ratio?

Case — Reproduction in Plants: A biology teacher grows new rose plants from stem cuttings and grafts a high-quality apple branch onto a hardier rootstock. She also cultivates Bryophyllum by placing its leaves in soil, observing new plants form at the leaf edges. Simultaneously, she observes a fern releasing spores from its underside.

Q1 (1M) Classify each method: stem cuttings, grafting, Bryophyllum leaf, spores.
Ans: Stem cuttings — vegetative propagation (artificial). Grafting — vegetative propagation (artificial). Bryophyllum leaf — vegetative propagation (natural). Spore formation — asexual reproduction.
Q2 (1M) Which method ensures the offspring is genetically identical to the parent? Ans: All asexual/vegetative methods — cuttings, grafting, Bryophyllum leaf — produce genetically identical offspring (clones).
Q3 (2M) What is the advantage of grafting over growing from seeds?
Ans: Grafting combines the desirable qualities of two plants — the rootstock (hardy, disease-resistant) and the scion (high quality fruit/flower). The resulting plant is stronger than either parent alone. Also, plants grown by grafting often produce fruit/flowers much earlier than seed-grown plants, and the offspring is genetically identical to the desirable parent (no genetic variation that seeds would introduce).
SA (3M) What are the differences between arteries and veins?
Ans: Arteries: Carry oxygenated blood (except pulmonary artery) away from the heart. Have thick, elastic walls to withstand high pressure. Blood flows in pulses. No valves (except at origin from heart). Lie deep within body. Veins: Carry deoxygenated blood (except pulmonary vein) towards the heart. Have thinner, less elastic walls — blood flows at lower pressure. Have valves to prevent backflow of blood. Often lie closer to skin surface. Exception: Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood; pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.
LA (5M) Describe the process of photosynthesis. What factors affect the rate of photosynthesis?
Ans: Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants synthesise food (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll. Equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2 (chlorophyll + light energy). Process: It occurs in two stages. (1) Light reactions (in thylakoid membrane): Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll. Water is split (photolysis) releasing O2 and energising electrons. ATP and NADPH are produced. (2) Dark reactions / Calvin Cycle (in stroma): CO2 is fixed using ATP and NADPH to produce glucose. Factors affecting rate: (1) Light intensity: Rate increases with light up to a saturation point. (2) CO2 concentration: Higher CO2 increases rate (up to a limit). (3) Temperature: Increases rate up to ~35°C, decreases beyond (enzyme denaturation). (4) Water availability: Shortage leads to stomatal closure, reducing CO2 entry. (5) Chlorophyll content: More chlorophyll = more absorption = higher rate.

📖 Core Theory

Ch 12: Electricity

Electric current (I) is the rate of flow of charge. Potential difference (V) drives current through a conductor. Ohm's Law: V = IR. Resistance depends on material, length, area of cross-section, and temperature.

Ohm's Law
V = IR (Voltage = Current x Resistance). Valid when temperature is constant. Slope of V-I graph = Resistance.
Series Circuit
R_total = R1 + R2 + R3. Same current through all. Different voltage across each. If one fails, all fail.
Parallel Circuit
1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3. Same voltage across all. Different current through each. One failure doesn't stop others.
Electric Power
P = VI = I^2 R = V^2/R. Unit: Watt (W). 1 kWh = 3,600,000 J (unit of electrical energy consumed).

🔑 Key Formulae — Electricity

Ohm's Law: V = I × R
Resistance: R = rho × L / A (rho = resistivity, L = length, A = cross-section area)
Power: P = V × I = I^2 × R = V^2 / R
Energy: E = P × t = V × I × t (in joules; divide by 3.6×10^6 for kWh)
Heating effect: H = I^2 × R × t (Joule's Law)

Ch 13: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field around it (Oersted, 1820). The direction is given by the Right-Hand Thumb Rule. Electromagnets and electric motors use this principle. Electromagnetic induction (Faraday) — changing magnetic field induces EMF.

Fleming's Left Hand Rule
For MOTORS (force on conductor): Thumb = motion, Index = field (B), Middle = current (I). F = BIL sin(theta).
Fleming's Right Hand Rule
For GENERATORS (induced current): Thumb = motion, Index = field (B), Middle = induced current.
AC vs DC
DC: constant direction (battery). AC: alternating direction at 50 Hz in India, 220V. AC transmitted over long distance (less energy loss).
Transformer
Works on mutual induction. Step-up increases voltage (fewer turns primary). Step-down decreases voltage. V1/V2 = N1/N2.

Ch 10 & 11: Light — Reflection and Refraction

Mirror Formula
1/f = 1/v + 1/u. Sign convention: distances measured from pole. Real object: u is negative. f = R/2 for spherical mirrors.
Lens Formula
1/f = 1/v − 1/u. Power P = 1/f (in dioptres, when f in metres). Convex lens: positive f. Concave lens: negative f.
Snell's Law
n1 sin(i) = n2 sin(r). Refractive index n = c/v = sin(i)/sin(r). Light bends toward normal when entering denser medium.
Dispersion
White light splits into VIBGYOR by prism. Violet bends most; Red least (speed in glass: red fastest). Rainbow: same effect by water droplets.

🔑 Key Formulae — Light

Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u ; Magnification m = -v/u = h2/h1
Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u ; Magnification m = v/u
Snell's Law: n1 × sin(i) = n2 × sin(r)
Power of lens: P = 1/f(metres); for combined lenses: P_total = P1 + P2

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1 — Ohm's Law / Resistance (Board 2023, 3M)

Q: Three resistors of 3Ω, 6Ω and 2Ω are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance and total current if the voltage is 12V.

1
1/R_total = 1/3 + 1/6 + 1/2 = 2/6 + 1/6 + 3/6 = 6/6 = 1. So R_total = 1Ω.
2
Total current I = V/R = 12/1 = 12A.
✅ Equivalent resistance = 1Ω; Total current = 12A.

Example 2 — Mirror Formula (Board 2022, 3M)

Q: An object is placed 30cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 20cm. Find image distance and magnification.

1
u = -30cm, f = -20cm (concave mirror). 1/v = 1/f - 1/u = 1/(-20) - 1/(-30) = -1/20 + 1/30 = -3/60 + 2/60 = -1/60. So v = -60cm.
2
Magnification m = -v/u = -(-60)/(-30) = -2. Negative m → real, inverted, magnified image.
✅ Image distance = 60cm (real, in front of mirror). Magnification = -2 (real, inverted, 2x enlarged).

Case — Electricity: A circuit has a battery of 12V connected to two resistors R1 = 4Ω and R2 = 8Ω. First, the circuit is connected in series. Then, the same components are reconnected in parallel.

Q1 (2M) In series: find total resistance and current.
Ans: R_total = R1 + R2 = 4 + 8 = 12Ω. Current I = V/R = 12/12 = 1A.
Q2 (2M) In parallel: find equivalent resistance and total current.
Ans: 1/R_eq = 1/4 + 1/8 = 2/8 + 1/8 = 3/8. R_eq = 8/3 = 2.67Ω. Total current = 12 / (8/3) = 12 × 3/8 = 4.5A.
Q3 (1M) Which connection gives brighter bulbs (greater power) — explain.
Ans: Parallel — lower total resistance → more total current → greater power (P = VI = V²/R). In parallel, each resistor also receives the full 12V.
SA (3M) State the rules for image formation by a convex lens for different object positions.
Ans: (1) Object at infinity: Image at F, real, inverted, point-sized. (2) Object beyond 2F: Image between F and 2F, real, inverted, diminished. (3) Object at 2F: Image at 2F, real, inverted, same size. (4) Object between F and 2F: Image beyond 2F, real, inverted, magnified. (5) Object at F: Image at infinity. (6) Object between F and lens: Image same side as object, virtual, erect, magnified (used in magnifying glass).
LA (5M) Define Ohm's Law. Derive the formula for equivalent resistance when resistors are connected (a) in series (b) in parallel.
Ans: Ohm's Law: The current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it, provided temperature remains constant: V = IR. R = resistance (Ω), V = voltage (V), I = current (A). (a) Series: Same current I flows through all. Voltage divides: V = V1 + V2 + V3 = IR1 + IR2 + IR3 = I(R1+R2+R3). So R_series = R1 + R2 + R3. Equivalent resistance is greater than any individual resistance. (b) Parallel: Same voltage V across all. Current divides: I = I1 + I2 + I3 = V/R1 + V/R2 + V/R3 = V(1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3). So 1/R_parallel = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3. Equivalent resistance is less than the smallest individual resistance. Advantage of parallel: Each device gets full voltage; failure of one does not affect others (household wiring uses parallel).

📜 PYQ Bank CBSE 2022–2024
2024 5 Marks Chemical Reactions
What is a redox reaction? Explain oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen, hydrogen, and electron transfer with one example each.
Redox Reaction: A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.

Oxidation: Gain of oxygen / Loss of hydrogen / Loss of electrons
Example: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (Mg is oxidised — gains oxygen)

Reduction: Loss of oxygen / Gain of hydrogen / Gain of electrons
Example: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O (CuO is reduced — loses oxygen)

In the reaction CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O: CuO = oxidising agent | H₂ = reducing agent
2023 4 Marks Acids, Bases & Salts
What happens when acid reacts with a metal carbonate? Write the chemical equation for the reaction of dilute HCl with sodium carbonate.
Acid + Metal Carbonate → Salt + Water + Carbon dioxide gas

Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑

Test for CO₂: Pass gas through lime water (Ca(OH)₂) → turns milky due to formation of CaCO₃.
If excess CO₂ passed: CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂ (milkiness disappears)
2024 5 Marks Life Processes
Draw a labelled diagram of the human digestive system. Name the digestive juices and enzymes secreted at each stage.
Digestion stages and enzymes:
• Mouth: Salivary amylase → starch to maltose
• Stomach: HCl + Pepsin → proteins to peptides | HCl kills bacteria, activates pepsin
• Small intestine (Duodenum): Bile (from liver) emulsifies fats | Pancreatic juice: amylase, trypsin, lipase
• Small intestine (Ileum): Intestinal juice (succus entericus) → final breakdown, absorption through villi
• Large intestine: Water absorption, formation of faeces

Diagram must show: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum + ileum) → large intestine → anus, with liver and pancreas labelled.
2023 3 Marks Heredity
In Mendel's experiment, a tall pea plant (TT) was crossed with a dwarf pea plant (tt). What are the phenotypes and genotypes in F1 and F2 generations?
P generation: TT (tall) × tt (dwarf)
F1: All Tt → all TALL (100% tall phenotype; tall is dominant)

F1 × F1: Tt × Tt
F2 genotypes: TT : Tt : tt = 1:2:1
F2 phenotypes: Tall : Dwarf = 3:1

Mendel's ratio of 3:1 in F2 is the phenotypic ratio for monohybrid cross.
2024 5 Marks Electricity
Derive the expression for the equivalent resistance when three resistors are connected in series. Then derive for parallel combination.
Series: Same current I through R₁, R₂, R₃.
V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ = IR₁ + IR₂ + IR₃ = I(R₁+R₂+R₃)
∴ Rs = R₁ + R₂ + R₃

Parallel: Same voltage V across R₁, R₂, R₃.
I = I₁ + I₂ + I₃ = V/R₁ + V/R₂ + V/R₃ = V(1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃)
∴ 1/Rp = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃

Parallel Rp < smallest individual resistance; Series Rs > largest individual resistance.
⚠️ Mistake Bank Common Errors

Balancing Chemical Equations — Not Balancing

Students write unbalanced equations and lose 1 mark per equation.

❌ WRONG: Na + HCl → NaCl + H₂ (unbalanced — left has 1 Na, 1 H, 1 Cl; right has 1 Na, 1 Cl, 2 H)
✅ CORRECT: 2Na + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂. Always count atoms on each side before writing the equation. 1 mark is specifically for a balanced equation.

Photosynthesis Equation — Wrong Products

Students write CO₂ or H₂ instead of O₂ as a product.

❌ WRONG: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6CO₂ (CO₂ as product)
✅ CORRECT: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Oxygen is released, not CO₂. The O₂ comes from the splitting of water molecules (photolysis), NOT from CO₂.

Ohm's Law Formula Inversion

Students write I = VR instead of I = V/R.

❌ WRONG: "Current = Voltage × Resistance → I = VR"
✅ CORRECT: V = IR → I = V/R → R = V/I. More resistance → less current (for same voltage). A simple check: higher R should give lower I. If your formula gives I increasing with R, it's wrong.

Series vs Parallel: Equivalent Resistance Rule

Students write smaller resistance for series and larger for parallel.

❌ WRONG: "In parallel combination, equivalent resistance is larger than individual resistances."
✅ CORRECT: Series: Equivalent R is LARGER than any individual R. Parallel: Equivalent R is SMALLER than the smallest individual R. More paths → less opposition to current → less resistance.

Heredity: Dominant vs Recessive Mix-up

Students identify the wrong trait as dominant in Mendel's experiments.

❌ WRONG: "In Mendel's monohybrid cross, dwarf was dominant because it reappeared in F2."
✅ CORRECT: The trait that appears in F1 (100%) is DOMINANT. Tall appeared in all F1 plants → tall is dominant. Recessive trait (dwarf) disappears in F1 but reappears in F2 at 1/4 ratio.

Reflection: Laws of Reflection Application

Students confuse normal, incident ray, and reflected ray.

❌ WRONG: "Angle of incidence = angle between incident ray and the mirror surface."
✅ CORRECT: Angle of incidence is measured from the NORMAL (perpendicular to mirror at point of incidence), NOT from the mirror surface. If angle from mirror = 30°, angle of incidence = 90° − 30° = 60°.

Lens Formula Sign Convention

Students forget to apply sign convention for object/image distances.

❌ WRONG: Using u = +30 cm for object placed in front of lens (object distance is positive).
✅ CORRECT: Using the New Cartesian Sign Convention: object is always on the left → u is always NEGATIVE. So u = −30 cm. Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive; against it are negative. 1/v − 1/u = 1/f.

Acid/Base: Confusing Indicators

Students mix up litmus, phenolphthalein, and methyl orange colours.

❌ WRONG: "Litmus turns red in base and blue in acid."
✅ CORRECT: Litmus: RED in acid, BLUE in base. Phenolphthalein: colourless in acid, PINK in base. Methyl orange: RED in acid, YELLOW in base. Remember litmus with "A-RED" (Acid = Red) and "B-BLUE" (Base = Blue).
🎯 Examiner's Eye Scoring Tips

🎯 Tip 1 — Label Diagrams Completely

In Biology and Physics, diagrams carry 2 marks: 1 for the drawing + 1 for correct labels. Always label EVERY structure asked. A beautifully drawn neuron with no labels scores 0 on the label mark. Write neatly in pencil, use a scale for circuits.

🎯 Tip 2 — Chemistry: Always Balance the Equation

Every chemical reaction question awards 1 mark for a balanced equation. Write the word equation first, then symbol equation, then balance. Unbalanced equations lose this mark automatically. Check atom count on both sides before moving to the next step.

🎯 Tip 3 — Physics Numericals: Units in Every Step

Write units alongside every quantity in every step of a numerical. "V = 12 V, R = 4 Ω, I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A." Missing units in the final answer costs 0.5 marks. Examiners check units explicitly in Section D numericals.

🎯 Tip 4 — Heredity: Always Draw the Cross Table

For Mendel's cross questions, draw the Punnett Square. Even for monohybrid crosses, a correct Punnett Square earns 1 mark for process (even if the final ratio is wrong). F1 genotypes → F1 × F1 → F2 Punnett Square → phenotypic ratio.

🎯 Tip 5 — Electricity: Show the Circuit and Formula

For resistance combination numericals, draw a quick circuit diagram showing the arrangement, then write the formula for equivalent resistance. This earns the diagram + formula marks before you even calculate. Many students lose 2 marks by jumping straight to numbers.

🎯 Tip 6 — Life Processes: Name the Enzyme, Not Just the Organ

In digestion questions, naming the organ (stomach) earns partial marks. Naming the enzyme (pepsin) earns full marks. For nutrition: name gland + enzyme + substrate + product. "Pancreas secretes lipase which breaks down fats into fatty acids and glycerol."

🎯 Tip 7 — Light: Draw Ray Diagrams with Arrow Directions

All rays in optics diagrams must have arrows showing direction of light travel. Examiners deduct 0.5 marks for missing arrow directions on incident, refracted, or reflected rays. Also mark the normal (dotted line) at the point of incidence.

🎯 Tip 8 — Assertion-Reason: Eliminate Systematically

For Assertion-Reason questions: (a) Is A true? (b) Is R true? (c) If both true, does R correctly explain A? Don't guess. Eliminate false options first. The most common trap: both A and R are true but R does NOT explain A → correct answer is (b) not (a).

🔥🔥🔥 VERY HIGH Electricity — Ohm's Law + Series/Parallel Numericals (5 Marks)

Every CBSE Science paper carries a 5-mark numerical on equivalent resistance (series, parallel, or mixed), current, voltage, or power. Master all formulas: V=IR, Rs=R₁+R₂+R₃, 1/Rp=1/R₁+1/R₂+1/R₃, P=VI=I²R=V²/R. Draw circuit diagram first.

Monohybrid cross with F1 and F2 Punnett squares, laws of inheritance, and sex determination in humans (XX/XY) appear every other year. Know 3:1 phenotypic ratio in F2, 1:2:1 genotypic ratio. Prepare both monohybrid and dihybrid cross patterns.

Fleming's Left Hand Rule (motor effect) vs Right Hand Rule (generator effect), electromagnetic induction, and AC vs DC appear as 3-mark questions. Know which rule is used when and state all three fingers' directions (thumb=force, index=field, middle=current).
