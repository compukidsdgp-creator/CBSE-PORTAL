      Aim, theory and procedure

      Aim

      Class 10: To study the dependence of the potential difference (V) across a resistor on the current (I) through it, and to find its resistance. Also to plot a graph between V and I.

      Class 12: To find the resistivity of the material of a given wire by plotting a graph of potential difference against current.

      Apparatus

      A battery of four dry cells (1.5 V each), a plug key, an ammeter (0–500 mA), a voltmeter (0–5 V), a rheostat (0–25 Ω), a coil of nichrome wire of unknown resistance, connecting wires and a piece of sandpaper. For resistivity: a metre scale and a screw gauge.

      Theory

      Ohm’s law: at constant temperature (and other physical conditions), the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends.

      V ∝ I  ⇒  V = I R  ⇒  R = V / I

      So the V–I graph of an ohmic conductor is a straight line through the origin, and its slope ΔV/ΔI is the resistance R. The SI unit of resistance is the ohm (1 Ω = 1 V A⁻¹).

      The resistance of a uniform wire of length L and diameter D is R = ρL/A with A = πD²/4, so

      ρ = R π D² / (4 L)  (Ω m)

      Procedure

      1. Clean the ends of the connecting wires with sandpaper. Note the range and least count of the ammeter and voltmeter.
      2. Connect the cells, plug key, ammeter, resistance coil and rheostat in series, with the + terminal of the ammeter towards the + of the battery.
      3. Connect the voltmeter in parallel across the resistance coil, its + terminal on the side joined to the battery’s + terminal.
      4. With the plug key out, note where each needle rests. This is the zero error (+ if right of zero, − if left).
      5. Set the rheostat for the maximum resistance, insert the key and read the ammeter and voltmeter with your eye in line with the needle and its image.
      6. Take out the key. Move the rheostat contact to increase the current and take the next pair of readings. Take at least six readings.
      7. Subtract the zero errors, find R = V/I for each pair, and plot V (y-axis) against I (x-axis). Find the slope.
      8. (Class 12) Measure the length L of the wire with a metre scale and its diameter D with a screw gauge, and find ρ.

      Precautions

      • Connections should be neat and tight; wire ends should be cleaned with sandpaper.
      • The ammeter is always in series and the voltmeter always in parallel with the resistor; + terminals go towards the + of the battery.
      • Do not let either needle go beyond full scale: start with the rheostat at maximum resistance.
      • Use enough cells that the largest readings lie in the upper half of each scale; a reading of a few divisions has a large percentage error.
      • Insert the plug key only while taking a reading, so the coil does not heat up.
      • Read each meter with your eye vertically above the needle (needle covers its mirror image) to avoid parallax.

      Sources of error

      • Current heats the coil, so its resistance rises slightly during the experiment.
      • Loose or dirty connections add extra resistance.
      • Zero error or parallax in reading the meters; the needle can only be read to the nearest division.
      • The voltmeter draws a small current, so the ammeter reads a little more than the current through the coil.
      • (Class 12) Error in measuring the diameter: it appears squared in ρ, so its error counts twice.
