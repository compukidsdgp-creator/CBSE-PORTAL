    Aim, theory and procedure

    Aim

    To find the focal length of a convex lens by plotting graphs between u and v, and between 1/u and 1/v.

    Apparatus

    An optical bench with a metre scale and three uprights, a convex lens with a lens holder, a candle (object), a white screen, and a matchbox.

    Theory

    For a thin lens, the object distance u, image distance v and focal length f are related by the lens formula:

    1/v − 1/u = 1/f  ⇒  f = uv / (u − v)

    With the New Cartesian sign convention, u is negative and v is positive for a real image formed by a convex lens, so f comes out positive.

    When the object is at 2F, the image is also at 2F and of the same size, so u = −2f and v = 2f. That is why the line v = u meets the u–v curve at (2f, 2f).

    Procedure

    1. Rough focal length: focus a distant object (a window or tree) on the screen. The distance between the lens and the screen is roughly f.
    2. Fix the lens upright near the middle of the bench. Make the flame tip, the centre of the lens and the centre of the screen the same height, so they lie on the principal axis.
    3. Place the candle at a distance between 1.5f and 3f from the lens.
    4. Move the screen until a sharp, inverted image of the flame appears. Approach from both sides; the sharpest position is in the middle.
    5. Read the positions of the candle, lens and screen on the scale, with your eye vertically above each index mark.
    6. Repeat for at least six different object distances, some less than 2f and some more than 2f.
    7. Calculate u, v and f for each reading, then plot the two graphs.

    Precautions

    • The lens should be clean, thin and vertical.
    • The flame tip, the centre of the lens and the centre of the screen should be at the same height.
    • Keep the object beyond the focus (|u| > f); otherwise no real image forms on the screen.
    • Darken the room so the image is easy to see.
    • Read the scale with your eye directly above the index mark to avoid parallax.

    Sources of error

    • Judging the sharpest image: the image looks sharp over a small range (depth of focus).
    • The optical centre of the lens may not lie exactly above the index mark of its upright (index error).
    • The uprights may not be perfectly vertical.
    • The flame flickers and changes shape, which makes focusing harder.
