      Aim, theory and procedure

      Aim

      Class 10: To determine the equivalent resistance of two resistors when they are connected in parallel.

      Class 12 link: To verify the law of combination of resistances in parallel, 1/Rp = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ (in the board practical this is done with a metre bridge; here with an ammeter and a voltmeter).

      Apparatus

      A battery of four dry cells (1.5 V each), a main plug key K, an ammeter (0–500 mA), a voltmeter (0–5 V), a rheostat (0–25 Ω), a resistance board carrying two resistance coils R₁ and R₂, each with its own branch plug key (K₁, K₂), connecting wires and a piece of sandpaper.

      Theory

      Resistors are in parallel when each is connected between the same two points (junctions). Then:

      • the potential difference V is the same across each resistor, and
      • the total current divides between the branches: I = I₁ + I₂.

      Using Ohm’s law for each branch, I₁ = V/R₁ and I₂ = V/R₂. If Rp is the single resistance that would draw the same current, I = V/Rp, so

      V/Rp = V/R₁ + V/R₂  ⇒  1/Rp = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂

      Rp = R₁R₂ / (R₁ + R₂)

      Rp is always less than the smallest resistance in the combination, because every new branch gives the current one more path. The larger current flows through the smaller resistance: I₁/I₂ = R₂/R₁.

      Procedure

      1. Clean the ends of the connecting wires with sandpaper. Note the range and least count of the ammeter and the voltmeter, and check that both needles rest on zero.
      2. Join one end of coil R₁ to one end of coil R₂, and the other end of R₁ to the other end of R₂. The two coils are now in parallel between two junctions.
      3. Connect the battery, main key K, ammeter (+ towards the battery’s +), the parallel combination and the rheostat in series.
      4. Connect the voltmeter across the combination (between the two junctions), with its + terminal on the side joined to the battery’s +.
      5. Put in only branch key K₁. Insert K, read the ammeter and voltmeter without parallax, then take K out. Move the rheostat and take two more readings. Find R₁ = V/I each time.
      6. Repeat with only K₂ in, to find R₂.
      7. Now put in both K₁ and K₂. Take three readings of V and I and find Rp = V/I.
      8. Calculate R₁R₂/(R₁+R₂) from your mean R₁ and R₂ and compare it with the measured Rp.

      Precautions

      • Connections should be neat and tight; the ends of the wires should be cleaned with sandpaper.
      • The ammeter goes in series in the main line (before the junction) and the voltmeter across the whole combination; + terminals go towards the + of the battery.
      • Start with the rheostat at maximum resistance so that no needle goes beyond full scale, especially when both branches are in and the current is largest.
      • Insert the main key only while taking a reading, so that the coils do not heat up.
      • Read each meter with your eye vertically above the needle (needle covers its mirror image) to avoid parallax.

      Sources of error

      • The coils heat up when current flows, so their resistance rises a little.
      • Loose or dirty connections and the connecting wires themselves add a small resistance.
      • The meters can only be read to the nearest division (10 mA, 0.1 V); parallax or a zero error adds to this.
      • The voltmeter draws a small current, so the ammeter reads slightly more than the current through the coils.
      • With both branches in, the p.d. is small, so the same reading error is a larger percentage of Rp.
