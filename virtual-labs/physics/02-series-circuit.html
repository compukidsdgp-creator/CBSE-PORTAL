    Aim, theory and procedure

    Aim

    To determine the equivalent resistance of two resistors when connected in series, and to verify that Rs = R1 + R2.

    Apparatus

    A battery of three 1.5 V cells (or a battery eliminator), two resistors of unknown resistance, an ammeter (0–1 A, least count 0.01 A), a voltmeter (0–5 V, least count 0.05 V), a rheostat, a one-way plug key, connecting wires and a piece of sandpaper.

    Theory

    Ohm's law: at constant temperature, the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends.

    V = I R  ⇒  R = V / I

    In a series connection the same current I flows through each resistor, and the total potential difference is shared: V = V1 + V2. Dividing by I:

    Rs = V/I = V1/I + V2/I = R1 + R2

    The ammeter is joined in series (it must carry the whole current, so it has a very low resistance). The voltmeter is joined in parallel across the resistor(s) being measured (it has a very high resistance, so it draws almost no current).

    Procedure

    1. Clean the ends of the connecting wires with sandpaper. Check that both meter needles rest exactly on zero (no zero error).
    2. Connect the battery, plug key, ammeter, resistor R1 and rheostat in series. Join the voltmeter across R1, with its + terminal towards the + side of the battery.
    3. Set the rheostat for maximum resistance, then insert the plug. Check that the needles deflect to the right.
    4. Read the ammeter and voltmeter, keeping your eye where the needle hides its reflection in the mirror strip. Remove the plug.
    5. Move the rheostat slider and take two more readings. Find R1 = V/I for each and take the mean.
    6. Repeat steps 2–5 with R2 alone.
    7. Now join R1 and R2 in series and connect the voltmeter across the combination. Take three readings and find the mean Rs.
    8. Compare Rs with R1 + R2. Plot V against I for all three.

    Precautions

    • All connections should be clean and tight; a loose contact adds resistance or breaks the circuit.
    • The ammeter is always in series and the voltmeter in parallel, each with + towards the battery's + side.
    • Start with the rheostat at maximum resistance so the ammeter is not overloaded.
    • Insert the plug only while taking a reading; current left flowing heats the resistors and changes their resistance.
    • Read each meter without parallax and note any zero error before starting.

    Sources of error

    • The resistance of the resistors rises slightly as they warm up.
    • Parallax error, and the limited least count of the meters (a needle can only be read to about one division).
    • Resistance of the connecting wires and contact resistance at the terminals.
    • The voltmeter draws a tiny current, so the ammeter reads slightly more than the current through the resistor.
