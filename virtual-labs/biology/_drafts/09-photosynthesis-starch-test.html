    Aim, theory and procedure

    Aim

    To show experimentally that light is necessary for photosynthesis (and, with a variegated leaf, that chlorophyll is necessary), using the iodine test for starch.

    Apparatus

    A potted plant with broad leaves (such as balsam or hibiscus) and a variegated plant (croton or money plant), a strip of black paper, paper clips, a beaker, a boiling tube, a tripod stand with wire gauze, a burner, a water bath, alcohol (ethanol or methylated spirit), dilute iodine solution, a dropper, a petri dish, forceps, a white tile and a 1 cm grid drawn on a transparent sheet.

    Theory

    Green plants make glucose by photosynthesis, using carbon dioxide and water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll:

    6CO₂ + 12H₂O —(sunlight, chlorophyll)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O

    Extra glucose is quickly stored as starch in the leaf. So starch in a leaf is evidence that photosynthesis has taken place there.

    Iodine test: iodine solution is yellowish-brown. With starch it gives a blue-black colour. Parts without starch stay yellowish-brown.

    Destarching: a plant kept in the dark for 48–72 hours uses up and moves out the starch already in its leaves, so any starch found later must have been made during the experiment.

    The part of the leaf under the black paper gets no light, so it cannot photosynthesise and stays starch-free. In a variegated leaf, the white or yellow parts have no chlorophyll, so they make no starch either.

    Procedure

    1. Keep the potted plant in a dark room or cupboard for 48–72 hours to destarch it.
    2. Fix a strip of black paper across a leaf on both its upper and lower surfaces with paper clips, so that no light reaches the covered part.
    3. Keep the plant in bright sunlight for 4–6 hours.
    4. Pluck the leaf and remove the black paper. Boil the leaf in water for 3–5 minutes.
    5. Put the leaf in a boiling tube of alcohol and heat it in a water bath until the leaf becomes pale (about 8–10 minutes).
    6. Rinse the leaf in warm water and spread it in a petri dish.
    7. Add a few drops of dilute iodine solution to cover the whole leaf and observe the colour after a minute.
    8. Place a transparent 1 cm grid over the leaf and count the leaf squares and the blue-black squares.
    9. Repeat with a variegated leaf that has been in light, and compare the green and non-green parts.

    Precautions

    • Destarch the plant fully (48–72 hours in the dark) before covering the leaf.
    • Fix the black paper on both surfaces of the leaf so that no light leaks in from below or the sides.
    • Never heat alcohol directly on a flame: it is inflammable. Always use a water bath.
    • Boil the leaf in water first so that the cells are killed and iodine can enter.
    • Remove all the chlorophyll before adding iodine, or the green colour will hide the result.
    • Handle the boiled leaf with forceps; it tears easily.

    Sources of error

    • A plant that is not fully destarched still has old starch, which can make covered parts turn blue.
    • Light leaks under the edges of the strip, so the boundary is not perfectly sharp.
    • Chlorophyll that is not fully removed masks the iodine colour.
    • Counting squares is an estimate: squares cut by the leaf edge or the strip edge need judgement, and a 1 cm grid is coarse compared with a strip only 2–3 cm wide.
    • Different parts of a leaf photosynthesise at slightly different rates, so a short exposure gives patchy staining.
