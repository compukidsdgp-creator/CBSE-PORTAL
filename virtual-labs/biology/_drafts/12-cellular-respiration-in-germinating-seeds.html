Respirationin Germinating Seeds

Sprouting seeds breathe in oxygen. Seal them in a tube with KOH, watch the coloured index creep along the capillary, read the scale against a stopwatch — then find how fast an unknown seed respires and what food it is burning.

    Aim, theory and procedure

    Aim

    To show that germinating seeds take in oxygen during respiration, to find their rate of oxygen uptake with a simple respirometer, and to find the respiratory quotient (RQ) of the seeds.

    Apparatus and materials

    Germinating seeds (soaked for 24 hours), the same number of boiled and cooled seeds (control), two boiling tubes with rubber bungs, two capillary tubes (internal diameter 1.5 mm) bent at right angles, millimetre scales, wire gauze platforms, moist cotton wool, 10% potassium hydroxide (KOH) solution, water, coloured liquid (eosin) for the index, screw clips, syringes, a water bath with thermometer, a stopwatch, a balance and petroleum jelly.

    Theory

    Germinating seeds respire very actively, because the growing embryo needs a lot of energy (ATP). In aerobic respiration, food is oxidised and oxygen is used up:

    C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy

    KOH absorbs the carbon dioxide given out: 2KOH + CO₂ → K₂CO₃ + H₂O. So in the KOH tube the volume of gas falls by the volume of O₂ taken in, the pressure drops and the coloured index moves towards the seeds. Volume of O₂ = A × x, where A is the cross-section of the capillary.

    With water instead of KOH, CO₂ stays in the tube, so the index moves only by (O₂ taken in − CO₂ given out) = y. Hence:

    RQ = CO₂ given out / O₂ taken in = (x − y) / x

    For carbohydrates RQ = 1 (6CO₂ ÷ 6O₂). For fats it is less than 1, about 0.7 (tripalmitin: 102CO₂ ÷ 145O₂ = 0.7). For proteins it is about 0.9. The control tube of boiled (dead) seeds does not respire; any movement of its index is due to changes in temperature or air pressure, and is subtracted.

    Procedure

    1. Soak the seeds in water for 24 hours. Kill an equal number by boiling them, then cool them to room temperature.
    2. Blot the germinating seeds dry and weigh them.
    3. Pour 2 mL of KOH solution into each boiling tube. Place a wire gauze platform above it and a layer of moist cotton wool on the gauze, so the seeds never touch the KOH.
    4. Put the germinating seeds in the test tube and the boiled seeds in the control tube. Fit the bungs carrying the capillary tubes and seal them with petroleum jelly so they are airtight.
    5. Introduce a drop of coloured liquid into each capillary as an index. Stand both tubes in a water bath at a fixed temperature.
    6. Leave the screw clips open for at least 5 minutes so that the air inside reaches the bath temperature (equilibration). Use the syringe to bring each index near the start of its scale.
    7. Close the screw clips and start the stopwatch at once. Read the position of both indices immediately, and then every 2 minutes for at least 10 minutes.
    8. Repeat with water in place of KOH, using the same seeds.
    9. Work out x and y, plot graphs of movement against time, and calculate the rate of oxygen uptake per gram per hour and the RQ.

    Precautions

    • The apparatus must be airtight: seal the bungs with petroleum jelly.
    • The seeds must not touch the KOH; it is corrosive and would kill them. Handle KOH with care.
    • Equilibrate for at least 5 minutes with the clips open before taking readings.
    • Keep the tubes in the water bath and do not hold them in your hand; body heat expands the air and moves the index.
    • Use the same mass (or number) of seeds and the same volume of liquid in the test and the control tube.
    • Always read the same edge of the index, with your eye directly above it.

    Sources of error

    • Small leaks at the bung or rubber tubing let air in and reduce the movement.
    • Changes of room temperature or pressure during the run (only partly corrected by the control tube).
    • KOH may not absorb all the CO₂ at once, so the O₂ uptake is underestimated slightly.
    • The bore of the capillary may not be perfectly uniform.
    • Seeds germinate unevenly, and microbes on the seeds also respire.
    • Parallax and judging the curved edge of the index.
