How to use the microscope
1. Select a specimen and begin with the scanning objective. 2. Use coarse focus to bring the image close to focus. 3. Switch to fine focus for sharp detail. 4. Increase objective power only after locating the specimen. Adjust illumination to improve contrast.

Interpretation prompt
As objective magnification increases, the specimen appears larger, but the field of view becomes smaller and less of the specimen can be seen at once. Higher magnification also requires more careful focusing and suitable illumination.
Investigation design
Independent variable: objective magnification.
Dependent observations: apparent size, field diameter and image clarity.
Controlled variables: specimen, eyepiece, illumination and focusing procedure.

PROCEDURE & SAFETY

GOOD PRACTICE
  1. Carry the microscope with two hands.
  2. Place it on a stable, level bench.
  3. Begin with the lowest-power objective.
  4. Use coarse focus only on low power.
  5. Use fine focus at high power.
  6. Do not allow the objective lens to touch the slide.
Safety note
This is a digital model. In a real laboratory, handle glass slides carefully, follow teacher instructions and never force the focus controls.

CONCEPT COMPANION

KEY IDEAS
TOTAL MAGNIFICATION
Eyepiece × Objective

Example: 10× eyepiece × 40× objective = 400× total magnification.

FIELD OF VIEW

The visible circular area generally becomes smaller as objective magnification increases.

FOCUSING

Coarse adjustment makes large focus changes; fine adjustment makes small, precise changes.
