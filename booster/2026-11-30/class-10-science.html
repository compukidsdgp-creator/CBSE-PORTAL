Assertion and Reasoning 10

Read both statements and choose the correct option from the key.

Key: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A. (C) A is true, but R is false. (D) A is false, but R is true.
1

Assertion (A): When a magnesium ribbon is burnt in air, it leaves behind a white powder.

Reason (R): Magnesium combines with oxygen of the air to form magnesium oxide.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; the white powder is magnesium oxide, so R explains A.
2

Assertion (A): Calcium oxide reacts vigorously with water to produce slaked lime and releases a large amount of heat.

Reason (R): A solution of slaked lime is used for whitewashing walls.

Answer: (B) — Both statements are true, but the use of slaked lime in whitewashing does not explain why the reaction CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ is vigorous and exothermic.
3

Assertion (A): Iron articles are painted to protect them from rusting.

Reason (R): Rusting of iron is a reduction reaction.

Answer: (C) — A is true: paint keeps air and moisture away from the iron. R is false: rusting is the slow oxidation of iron by oxygen in the presence of water.
4

Assertion (A): The decomposition of silver chloride in sunlight is a photolytic decomposition reaction.

Reason (R): Silver chloride is used in black-and-white photography.

Answer: (B) — Both are true: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂ in sunlight, and this property is used in photography. But R only states a use; A is true because light supplies the energy for decomposition.
5

Assertion (A): In the reaction CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O, copper(II) oxide gets oxidised.

Reason (R): In this reaction hydrogen gains oxygen and gets oxidised.

Answer: (D) — A is false: copper(II) oxide loses oxygen, so it is reduced. R is true: hydrogen gains oxygen to form water, so it is oxidised.
6

Assertion (A): Manufacturers flush bags of chips with nitrogen gas.

Reason (R): Nitrogen is an unreactive gas that keeps oxygen away and prevents the fats in the chips from turning rancid.

Answer: (A) — Rancidity is the oxidation of fats and oils. Replacing air with nitrogen removes oxygen, so R correctly explains A.
7

Assertion (A): Bleaching powder is prepared by passing chlorine gas through quicklime.

Reason (R): Bleaching powder is used to disinfect drinking water.

Answer: (D) — A is false: bleaching powder is made by passing chlorine through dry slaked lime, Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O. R is true.
8

Assertion (A): Pure water has a pH of 7.

Reason (R): Water ionises completely into H⁺ and OH⁻ ions.

Answer: (C) — A is true because pure water has equal concentrations of H⁺ and OH⁻ ions. R is false: water ionises only very slightly.
9

Assertion (A): Dry HCl gas does not change the colour of dry blue litmus paper.

Reason (R): Acids produce H⁺ ions only in the presence of water.

Answer: (A) — Without water, HCl does not ionise, so no H⁺ (H₃O⁺) ions are formed and litmus does not change. R correctly explains A.
10

Assertion (A): Sodium hydrogen carbonate is used as an ingredient in antacids.

Reason (R): Sodium hydrogen carbonate is a strong base.

Answer: (C) — A is true: it neutralises excess acid in the stomach. R is false: it is a mild, non-corrosive base, which is exactly why it is safe to swallow.

Case Studies 16

Read each passage and answer the questions that follow.

Case 1: Heating lead nitrate

In the school laboratory, Ritu heated a small amount of white lead nitrate powder in a dry boiling tube held with a test-tube holder. She heard a crackling sound and saw brown fumes coming out of the tube. A glowing splinter brought near the mouth of the tube burst into flame. A yellowish residue was left at the bottom. In a second experiment, she mixed lead nitrate solution with potassium iodide solution and a bright yellow solid settled at the bottom of the test tube.
11

The brown fumes Ritu observed are of:

  • (a) Oxygen
  • (b) Nitrogen dioxide
  • (c) Nitrogen
  • (d) Lead oxide
Answer: (b) Nitrogen dioxide — 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂. NO₂ is the reddish-brown gas.
12

Which gas made the glowing splinter burst into flame?

  • (a) Nitrogen dioxide
  • (b) Hydrogen
  • (c) Oxygen
  • (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (c) Oxygen — Oxygen supports combustion, so it relights a glowing splinter.
13

In the balanced equation 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + x NO₂ + O₂, the value of x is:

  • (a) 2
  • (b) 3
  • (c) 4
  • (d) 6
Answer: (c) 4 — Left side: 4 N and 12 O. Right side with x = 4: 4 N; oxygen = 2 (in PbO) + 8 (in NO₂) + 2 (in O₂) = 12.
14

The yellow solid in the second experiment and the type of reaction are:

  • (a) PbI₂; double displacement (precipitation)
  • (b) KNO₃; combination
  • (c) PbI₂; decomposition
  • (d) KI; displacement
Answer: (a) PbI₂; double displacement (precipitation) — Pb(NO₃)₂ + 2KI → PbI₂↓ + 2KNO₃. The ions are exchanged and insoluble yellow lead iodide precipitates.

Case 2: The bicycle left in the rain

Aman left his bicycle outside through the monsoon. After a few weeks, its unpainted chain and spokes were covered with a flaky reddish-brown layer, while the painted frame and the zinc-coated handlebar stayed shiny. His grandmother's silver anklet, kept in an open drawer, had turned black, and an old copper vessel in the kitchen had a green coating. His sister said that the oil in an open packet of namkeen had also started to smell and taste bad.
15

The reddish-brown layer on the chain is:

  • (a) Iron(II) oxide
  • (b) Hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O
  • (c) Iron sulphide
  • (d) Iron carbonate
Answer: (b) Hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O — Rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide, formed when iron reacts with oxygen and water together.
16

The handlebar stayed shiny because it was protected by:

  • (a) Electroplating with chromium
  • (b) Galvanisation
  • (c) Alloying
  • (d) Anodising
Answer: (b) Galvanisation — A coating of zinc on iron or steel is called galvanisation; zinc corrodes in preference to iron.
17

The black coating on the silver anklet is mainly:

  • (a) Silver oxide
  • (b) Silver chloride
  • (c) Silver sulphide
  • (d) Silver nitrate
Answer: (c) Silver sulphide — Silver reacts with hydrogen sulphide (sulphur compounds) in the air to form black silver sulphide, Ag₂S.
18

The change in the namkeen is called rancidity. It can be slowed down by:

  • (a) Keeping the packet open in sunlight
  • (b) Adding water to the namkeen
  • (c) Storing it in an airtight container
  • (d) Heating the packet
Answer: (c) Storing it in an airtight container — Rancidity is oxidation of fats and oils. Airtight storage limits contact with oxygen (antioxidants and refrigeration also help).

Case 3: pH in everyday life

A dentist explained to a group of students that bacteria in the mouth break down sugar and food particles left on the teeth and produce acids. Tooth enamel, made of calcium hydroxyapatite, starts to corrode when the pH in the mouth falls below 5.5. Toothpastes are generally basic. The same evening, one of the students was stung by an ant, and his father, a farmer, mentioned that he was adding a substance to his field because the soil had become too acidic for the crop.
19

Tooth decay starts when the pH in the mouth falls below:

  • (a) 7.0
  • (b) 6.5
  • (c) 5.5
  • (d) 4.0
Answer: (c) 5.5 — Below pH 5.5 the enamel (calcium hydroxyapatite) begins to dissolve.
20

Toothpastes help prevent tooth decay because they:

  • (a) are acidic and kill bacteria
  • (b) are basic and neutralise excess acid
  • (c) are neutral and wash away sugar
  • (d) add calcium to the enamel
Answer: (b) are basic and neutralise excess acid — Basic toothpaste neutralises the acid produced by bacteria and raises the pH.
21

An ant sting injects methanoic acid. The best home remedy is to rub the area with:

  • (a) Lemon juice
  • (b) Vinegar
  • (c) Baking soda paste
  • (d) Common salt solution
Answer: (c) Baking soda paste — Baking soda (NaHCO₃) is a mild base that neutralises methanoic acid.
22

To treat the acidic soil, the farmer is most likely adding:

  • (a) Ammonium sulphate
  • (b) Quicklime or slaked lime
  • (c) Vinegar
  • (d) Common salt
Answer: (b) Quicklime or slaked lime — Bases such as quicklime (CaO), slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂) or chalk (CaCO₃) neutralise excess acidity in soil.

Case 4: From common salt to useful chemicals

Common salt is a raw material for many chemicals. When electricity is passed through brine (a concentrated solution of sodium chloride), sodium hydroxide, chlorine gas and hydrogen gas are produced; this is called the chlor-alkali process. The chlorine is used to make bleaching powder. Sodium hydrogen carbonate, made using sodium chloride, is used in baking. Many salts contain a fixed number of water molecules in their crystals; for example, blue copper sulphate crystals turn white when heated.
23

In the chlor-alkali process, the gases formed at the anode and cathode respectively are:

  • (a) Hydrogen and chlorine
  • (b) Chlorine and hydrogen
  • (c) Oxygen and hydrogen
  • (d) Chlorine and oxygen
Answer: (b) Chlorine and hydrogen — 2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl₂(g) + H₂(g). Chlorine is released at the anode, hydrogen at the cathode, and NaOH forms near the cathode.
24

Bleaching powder is obtained by the action of chlorine on:

  • (a) Dry slaked lime
  • (b) Quicklime
  • (c) Limestone
  • (d) Lime water
Answer: (a) Dry slaked lime — Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O.
25

On heating, sodium hydrogen carbonate gives:

  • (a) NaOH, CO₂ and H₂O
  • (b) Na₂CO₃, CO₂ and H₂O
  • (c) Na₂O, CO₂ and H₂
  • (d) NaCl, CO₂ and H₂O
Answer: (b) Na₂CO₃, CO₂ and H₂O — 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂. The CO₂ makes dough rise.
26

Blue copper sulphate crystals turn white on heating because they lose:

  • (a) Copper
  • (b) Sulphur dioxide
  • (c) Water of crystallisation
  • (d) Oxygen
Answer: (c) Water of crystallisation — CuSO₄·5H₂O loses its five molecules of water of crystallisation to form white anhydrous CuSO₄; adding water brings back the blue colour.

HOTS — Higher Order Thinking 10

Think it through before you open the answer.

27

A shiny brown metal X turns black when heated in air. When hydrogen gas is passed over the heated black substance, the brown colour returns. Identify X and the black substance, write both equations, and name the substance oxidised and reduced in the second reaction.

Answer: X is copper and the black substance is copper(II) oxide. 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO. CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O. In the second reaction CuO is reduced (it loses oxygen) and H₂ is oxidised (it gains oxygen), so it is a redox reaction.
28

In the electrolysis of water, the volume of gas collected at one electrode is double that at the other. Explain why, and name the gas in each case.

Answer: Water (H₂O) contains hydrogen and oxygen in the ratio 2 : 1 by number of atoms: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂. So two volumes of hydrogen (at the cathode) are produced for every one volume of oxygen (at the anode).
29

Whitewashed walls look dull at first but develop a shiny finish two to three days later. Explain with an equation.

Answer: The whitewash is slaked lime, Ca(OH)₂. It slowly reacts with carbon dioxide in the air: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O. The thin layer of calcium carbonate formed on the wall gives it a shiny finish.
30

Fresh milk has a pH of about 6. How will its pH change as it turns into curd? Give a reason.

Answer: The pH will decrease (fall below 6). Bacteria convert lactose into lactic acid, so the milk becomes more acidic as curd forms.
31

Solution P has pH 3 and solution Q has pH 9. (a) Which has the higher concentration of H⁺ ions? (b) What happens to the pH of P if it is diluted a hundred times with water, and can it ever cross 7?

Answer: (a) P. A lower pH means a higher H⁺ ion concentration. (b) Dilution lowers the H⁺ concentration, so the pH of P rises towards 7 (to about 5). It approaches 7 but never crosses it, because adding water cannot make an acid basic.
32

10 mL of a sodium hydroxide solution is completely neutralised by 8 mL of a given hydrochloric acid. How much of the same acid will be needed to neutralise 20 mL of the same NaOH solution? Explain.

Answer: 16 mL. The amount of acid needed is directly proportional to the amount of base: doubling the base (10 → 20 mL) doubles the acid (8 → 16 mL).
33

Equal lengths of magnesium ribbon are put into test tube A containing hydrochloric acid and test tube B containing acetic acid of the same concentration. In which tube is the fizzing more vigorous, and why?

Answer: Tube A. HCl is a strong acid and ionises almost completely, so it gives a much higher concentration of H⁺ ions than acetic acid, a weak acid that ionises only partly. More H⁺ ions mean hydrogen gas is produced faster.
34

An iron nail dipped in blue copper sulphate solution becomes brownish and the solution turns pale green. What would you observe if a copper strip were dipped in iron sulphate solution? Justify.

Answer: No change. Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu happens because iron is more reactive than copper. Copper is less reactive than iron, so it cannot displace iron from iron sulphate.
35

A white compound A is heated to 373 K and forms B, which sets into a hard solid when mixed with water. Identify A and B, write the equation, and say why B must be stored in a moisture-proof container.

Answer: A is gypsum, CaSO₄·2H₂O, and B is plaster of Paris, CaSO₄·½H₂O. CaSO₄·2H₂O → CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O. B reacts with moisture to turn back into gypsum and sets hard, so it must be kept dry.
36

Why should curd and other sour foods not be stored in brass or copper vessels?

Answer: Sour foods contain acids. These acids react with copper and the zinc in brass to form soluble salts that can be poisonous, which spoils the food and can harm health.

Competency-Based Questions 10

Apply the idea to a real situation.

37

A soil test shows the pH of a field is 4.5. Which of these should the farmer add to make the soil suitable for most crops?

  • (a) Ammonium nitrate
  • (b) Slaked lime
  • (c) Vinegar
  • (d) Sulphur powder
Answer: (b) Slaked lime
Soil at pH 4.5 is too acidic; a base such as slaked lime raises the pH towards neutral.
38

A student with indigestion caused by excess stomach acid is advised to take an antacid. Which one is suitable?

  • (a) Milk of magnesia
  • (b) Lemon juice
  • (c) Sodium hydroxide solution
  • (d) Vinegar
Answer: (a) Milk of magnesia
Milk of magnesia, Mg(OH)₂, is a mild base that neutralises excess HCl in the stomach. NaOH is far too strong and corrosive.
39

A visually impaired student wants to test whether a solution is acidic or basic without seeing a colour change. Which indicator should she use?

  • (a) Litmus paper
  • (b) Phenolphthalein
  • (c) Vanilla essence
  • (d) Methyl orange
Answer: (c) Vanilla essence
Vanilla essence is an olfactory indicator: its smell disappears in a basic solution but stays in an acidic one.
40

While making a cake, a baker uses baking powder instead of plain baking soda. What is the main reason?

  • (a) Baking powder adds sweetness
  • (b) Its mild edible acid neutralises the sodium carbonate formed, so the cake does not taste bitter
  • (c) Baking powder makes the cake brown
  • (d) Baking soda does not release any gas
Answer: (b) Its mild edible acid neutralises the sodium carbonate formed, so the cake does not taste bitter
Baking powder is baking soda + a mild edible acid such as tartaric acid. The acid reacts with the soda to release CO₂ and stops bitter-tasting Na₂CO₃ from forming.
41

The municipal water supply is disinfected before it reaches homes. Which chemical is commonly used for this?

  • (a) Washing soda
  • (b) Baking soda
  • (c) Bleaching powder
  • (d) Plaster of Paris
Answer: (c) Bleaching powder
Bleaching powder (CaOCl₂) releases chlorine, which kills germs in drinking water.
42

A lab assistant needs to dilute concentrated sulphuric acid. What is the safe method?

  • (a) Add water to the acid quickly
  • (b) Add the acid to water slowly, with constant stirring
  • (c) Mix equal volumes at once
  • (d) Heat the acid and then add water
Answer: (b) Add the acid to water slowly, with constant stirring
Dilution is highly exothermic. Adding water to the acid can make the mixture boil and splash acid out; adding acid slowly to water spreads the heat safely.
43

A soda-acid fire extinguisher contains a solution of sodium hydrogen carbonate and a separate container of acid. Which gas does it release to put out the fire?

  • (a) Oxygen
  • (b) Hydrogen
  • (c) Carbon dioxide
  • (d) Chlorine
Answer: (c) Carbon dioxide
NaHCO₃ reacts with the acid to release CO₂, which does not support combustion and cuts off the oxygen supply.
44

Hard water in a hostel forms very little lather with soap. Which salt can be added to remove permanent hardness?

  • (a) Sodium chloride
  • (b) Sodium carbonate (washing soda)
  • (c) Calcium sulphate
  • (d) Copper sulphate
Answer: (b) Sodium carbonate (washing soda)
Washing soda, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, removes permanent hardness by precipitating calcium and magnesium ions as carbonates.
45

Before soldering, a technician cleans the black coating from an old copper wire by dipping it in a dilute acid. Which reaction is she using?

  • (a) Copper oxide (a basic oxide) reacts with the acid to form a salt and water
  • (b) Copper reacts with the acid to release hydrogen
  • (c) The acid reduces copper oxide to copper
  • (d) The acid makes the copper more reactive
Answer: (a) Copper oxide (a basic oxide) reacts with the acid to form a salt and water
CuO + 2HCl → CuCl₂ + H₂O. Metal oxides are basic and dissolve in acids; copper itself does not react with dilute HCl.
46

A food company wants to keep its packaged peanuts fresh for months. Which combination works best?

  • (a) Transparent packet filled with air
  • (b) Airtight packet flushed with nitrogen, with an antioxidant added
  • (c) Open tray in a warm room
  • (d) Packet filled with oxygen
Answer: (b) Airtight packet flushed with nitrogen, with an antioxidant added
Removing oxygen and adding antioxidants both slow the oxidation of oils, which is what causes rancidity.

Knowledge Check 10

Quick recall: one or two lines each.

47

Why should a chemical equation be balanced?

Answer: To satisfy the law of conservation of mass: mass is neither created nor destroyed in a reaction, so the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides.
48

What is a combination reaction? Give one example.

Answer: A reaction in which two or more substances combine to form a single product. Example: CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂.
49

What is a redox reaction?

Answer: A reaction in which one substance is oxidised and another is reduced at the same time. Example: ZnO + C → Zn + CO.
50

Write the balanced equation for the reaction of zinc granules with dilute sulphuric acid.

Answer: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂. Hydrogen gas is released, which burns with a pop sound.
51

Which gas is evolved when sodium carbonate reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid, and how is it tested?

Answer: Carbon dioxide. Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂. It turns lime water milky.
52

What is a universal indicator?

Answer: A mixture of several indicators that shows a different colour at each pH value, so it tells us how strong an acid or base is.
53

Write the chemical formulae of baking soda and washing soda.

Answer: Baking soda: NaHCO₃ (sodium hydrogen carbonate). Washing soda: Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (sodium carbonate decahydrate).
54

What is water of crystallisation? Give an example.

Answer: The fixed number of water molecules present in one formula unit of a salt crystal. Example: CuSO₄·5H₂O has five molecules of water of crystallisation.
55

What is a neutralisation reaction? Give an example.

Answer: A reaction in which an acid and a base react to form a salt and water. Example: NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O.
56

What is an exothermic reaction? Give one example from daily life.

Answer: A reaction in which heat is released along with the products. Example: respiration, C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy, or the burning of natural gas.
