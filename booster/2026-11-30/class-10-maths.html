Assertion and Reasoning 10

Read both statements and choose the correct option from the key.

Key: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A. (C) A is true, but R is false. (D) A is false, but R is true.
1

Assertion (A): For the numbers 26 and 91, HCF × LCM = 2366.

Reason (R): For any two positive integers a and b, HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. 26 = 2 × 13 and 91 = 7 × 13, so HCF = 13 and LCM = 182; 13 × 182 = 2366 = 26 × 91. A follows directly from R.
2

Assertion (A): The number 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 is a composite number.

Reason (R): Every composite number can be written as a product of primes.

Answer: (B) — Both are true. A is true because 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 = 13 × (77 + 1) = 13 × 78 = 1014, which has factors other than 1 and itself. R is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, but it does not show why this number is composite.
3

Assertion (A): √3 is an irrational number.

Reason (R): The sum of two irrational numbers is always irrational.

Answer: (C) — A is true (it can be proved by contradiction). R is false: √3 and −√3 are both irrational, but their sum is 0, which is rational.
4

Assertion (A): The product of two irrational numbers is always irrational.

Reason (R): The product of a non-zero rational number and an irrational number is irrational.

Answer: (D) — A is false: √2 × √2 = 2, which is rational. R is true: for example, 3 × √5 = 3√5 is irrational.
5

Assertion (A): The LCM of 12, 15 and 21 is 420.

Reason (R): The LCM of given numbers is the product of the greatest power of each prime factor that occurs in the numbers.

Answer: (A) — 12 = 2² × 3, 15 = 3 × 5, 21 = 3 × 7. Taking the greatest power of each prime: 2² × 3 × 5 × 7 = 420. R is the rule used to get A, so R explains A.
6

Assertion (A): The graph of y = x² + 1 does not meet the x-axis.

Reason (R): Every quadratic polynomial has exactly two real zeroes.

Answer: (C) — A is true: x² + 1 ≥ 1 for every real x, so the curve never touches the x-axis. R is false: a quadratic polynomial can have two, one or no real zeroes, x² + 1 being an example with none.
7

Assertion (A): If 2 and 3 are the zeroes of x² − 5x + k, then k = 6.

Reason (R): The product of the zeroes of ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0) is c/a.

Answer: (A) — Product of zeroes = c/a = k/1 = k, and 2 × 3 = 6, so k = 6. (The sum 2 + 3 = 5 = −(−5)/1 also matches.) R gives the result in A.
8

Assertion (A): The zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 are 4 and −2.

Reason (R): The sum of the zeroes of ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0) is −b/a.

Answer: (B) — Both are true: x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2). R only tells us the sum is 2; many pairs (such as 1 and 1) have sum 2, so R alone does not establish A.
9

Assertion (A): If the graph of y = p(x) cuts the x-axis at exactly three points, then p(x) must be a polynomial of degree 3.

Reason (R): A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes.

Answer: (D) — A is false: three zeroes only tells us the degree is at least 3; for example x(x − 1)(x − 2)(x² + 1) has degree 5 but only three real zeroes. R is true.
10

Assertion (A): If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 9, then α + β = 0.

Reason (R): The zeroes of x² − 9 are 3 and −3, which are additive inverses of each other.

Answer: (A) — x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3), so the zeroes are 3 and −3 and their sum is 0. This is exactly why α + β = 0 (also −b/a = 0/1 = 0).

Case Studies 16

Read each passage and answer the questions that follow.

Case 1: Republic Day parade practice

A school in Jaipur is preparing for its Republic Day parade. There are 144 NCC cadets and 216 scouts. The sports teacher wants to arrange them in rows so that every row has the same number of students and each row has either only cadets or only scouts. She also wants as few rows as possible, so each row should be as long as possible. A student volunteer starts by writing the prime factorisation of both numbers on the blackboard.
11

The prime factorisation of 216 is:

  • (a) 2² × 3³
  • (b) 2³ × 3³
  • (c) 2³ × 3²
  • (d) 2⁴ × 3²
Answer: (b) 2³ × 3³ — 216 = 8 × 27 = 2³ × 3³. (2⁴ × 3² = 144, the number of cadets.)
12

The greatest number of students that can stand in each row is:

  • (a) 36
  • (b) 24
  • (c) 72
  • (d) 432
Answer: (c) 72 — Row length must divide both 144 and 216, and be as large as possible: HCF = 2³ × 3² = 72.
13

How many rows will be formed in all?

  • (a) 5
  • (b) 6
  • (c) 10
  • (d) 12
Answer: (a) 5 — Cadets: 144 ÷ 72 = 2 rows. Scouts: 216 ÷ 72 = 3 rows. Total 2 + 3 = 5 rows.
14

The LCM of 144 and 216 is:

  • (a) 216
  • (b) 864
  • (c) 1296
  • (d) 432
Answer: (d) 432 — LCM = 2⁴ × 3³ = 16 × 27 = 432. Check: HCF × LCM = 72 × 432 = 31104 = 144 × 216.

Case 2: Traffic signals at a busy crossing

At a busy crossing in Pune, three traffic signals on different roads are timed independently. The first turns green every 48 seconds, the second every 72 seconds and the third every 108 seconds. A traffic police constable notes that all three turned green together at exactly 8:00 a.m. He wants to know when this will happen again, so that he can plan when to stand at the centre of the crossing.
15

After how many seconds will all three signals next turn green together?

  • (a) 216 s
  • (b) 432 s
  • (c) 864 s
  • (d) 12 s
Answer: (b) 432 s — 48 = 2⁴ × 3, 72 = 2³ × 3², 108 = 2² × 3³. LCM = 2⁴ × 3³ = 432 s.
16

At what time will they next turn green together?

  • (a) 8:04:32 a.m.
  • (b) 8:07:12 a.m.
  • (c) 8:07:20 a.m.
  • (d) 8:14:24 a.m.
Answer: (b) 8:07:12 a.m. — 432 s = 7 min 12 s (7 × 60 = 420, plus 12). So 8:00:00 + 7 min 12 s = 8:07:12 a.m.
17

After 8:00 a.m., how many more times will all three turn green together up to 9:00 a.m.?

  • (a) 7
  • (b) 9
  • (c) 8
  • (d) 10
Answer: (c) 8 — 3600 ÷ 432 = 8.33…, so it happens at 432 × 1, 2, …, 8 seconds (the 8th time is 3456 s, i.e. 8:57:36 a.m.).
18

The largest time interval (in seconds) that exactly divides all three signal timings is:

  • (a) 6
  • (b) 24
  • (c) 36
  • (d) 12
Answer: (d) 12 — HCF = lowest powers of the common primes = 2² × 3 = 12 s.

Case 3: The garden fountain

In a garden in Mysuru, a water jet leaves a nozzle on the ground, rises and falls back to the ground. A Class 10 student draws the path on graph paper and finds that the height of the jet above the ground is given by the polynomial p(x) = −x² + 6x − 8, where x is the horizontal position in metres measured from a fixed pole. The jet is at ground level exactly where p(x) = 0.
19

The zeroes of p(x) are:

  • (a) −2 and −4
  • (b) 2 and 4
  • (c) 1 and 8
  • (d) −2 and 4
Answer: (b) 2 and 4 — −x² + 6x − 8 = −(x² − 6x + 8) = −(x − 2)(x − 4), so p(x) = 0 at x = 2 and x = 4.
20

Using the coefficients, the sum of the zeroes of p(x) is:

  • (a) −6
  • (b) 8
  • (c) 6
  • (d) −8
Answer: (c) 6 — Here a = −1, b = 6, c = −8. Sum = −b/a = −6/(−1) = 6, which agrees with 2 + 4.
21

The graph of p(x) is a parabola that:

  • (a) opens upward, because c < 0
  • (b) opens downward, because a < 0
  • (c) opens upward, because b > 0
  • (d) is a straight line, because p(x) has two zeroes
Answer: (b) opens downward, because a < 0 — For ax² + bx + c, the parabola opens downward when a < 0. Here a = −1, matching a jet that rises and falls.
22

What is the height of the jet at x = 3 m?

  • (a) 1 m
  • (b) 2 m
  • (c) 0 m
  • (d) −1 m
Answer: (a) 1 m — p(3) = −9 + 18 − 8 = 1. This is the highest point, midway between the zeroes 2 and 4.

Case 4: Reading a graph in the maths lab

During a maths lab activity, Meera made a table of values for y = x² − x − 6 for x from −3 to 4 and plotted the points on graph paper. Joining them gave a smooth U-shaped curve. She marked where the curve crossed the x-axis and where it crossed the y-axis. Her partner then asked her to write a new polynomial whose zeroes are the negatives of the zeroes she had found.
23

The curve crosses the x-axis at:

  • (a) (3, 0) and (−2, 0)
  • (b) (−3, 0) and (2, 0)
  • (c) (6, 0) and (−1, 0)
  • (d) (0, 3) and (0, −2)
Answer: (a) (3, 0) and (−2, 0) — x² − x − 6 = (x − 3)(x + 2), so y = 0 at x = 3 and x = −2.
24

The curve crosses the y-axis at:

  • (a) (0, 6)
  • (b) (−6, 0)
  • (c) (0, −6)
  • (d) (0, −1)
Answer: (c) (0, −6) — On the y-axis x = 0, so y = 0 − 0 − 6 = −6.
25

The product of the zeroes of x² − x − 6 is:

  • (a) 6
  • (b) 1
  • (c) −1
  • (d) −6
Answer: (d) −6 — Product = c/a = −6/1 = −6, which agrees with 3 × (−2).
26

A quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are the negatives of Meera's zeroes is:

  • (a) x² − x + 6
  • (b) x² + x − 6
  • (c) x² − x − 6
  • (d) −x² + x + 6
Answer: (b) x² + x − 6 — New zeroes are −3 and 2: sum = −1, product = −6, so x² − (−1)x + (−6) = x² + x − 6. (Options (c) and (d) have the original zeroes.)

HOTS — Higher Order Thinking 10

Think it through before you open the answer.

27

Given that √5 is irrational, prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational.

Answer: Suppose 3 + 2√5 is rational, say 3 + 2√5 = a/b with integers a, b and b ≠ 0. Then √5 = (a − 3b)/(2b). The right side is rational because a, b are integers, so √5 would be rational. This contradicts the fact that √5 is irrational. Hence 3 + 2√5 is irrational.
28

Check whether 12ⁿ can end with the digit 0 for any natural number n. Justify.

Answer: No. A number ends with 0 only if it is divisible by both 2 and 5, i.e. its prime factorisation contains 5. 12ⁿ = (2² × 3)ⁿ = 2²ⁿ × 3ⁿ has no factor 5. By the uniqueness in the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, no other factorisation exists, so 12ⁿ never ends in 0.
29

The HCF of two numbers is 9 and their LCM is 360. If one number is 45, find the other and verify your answer.

Answer: Other number = (HCF × LCM) ÷ 45 = (9 × 360) ÷ 45 = 3240 ÷ 45 = 72. Check: 45 = 3² × 5 and 72 = 2³ × 3², so HCF = 3² = 9 and LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5 = 360.
30

Can two positive integers have HCF 16 and LCM 380? Give a reason.

Answer: No. The HCF of two numbers always divides their LCM (both numbers are multiples of the HCF, and the LCM is a multiple of each number). But 380 ÷ 16 = 23.75 is not an integer, so such numbers cannot exist.
31

Without calculating its value fully, explain why 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 + 5 is a composite number.

Answer: 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 + 5 = 5 × (4 × 3 × 2 × 1 + 1) = 5 × 25 = 125. It has 5 as a factor other than 1 and itself, so it is composite (in fact 125 = 5³).
32

If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 5x + 6, find α² + β² without finding the zeroes.

Answer: α + β = −b/a = 5 and αβ = c/a = 6. α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 25 − 12 = 13. (Check: the zeroes are 2 and 3, and 4 + 9 = 13.)
33

One zero of x² + kx − 12 is 3. Find k and the other zero.

Answer: Put x = 3: 9 + 3k − 12 = 0, so 3k = 3 and k = 1. The polynomial is x² + x − 12. Product of zeroes = −12, so the other zero = −12 ÷ 3 = −4. (Check: x² + x − 12 = (x + 4)(x − 3).)
34

If the zeroes of ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0) are reciprocals of each other, show that a = c.

Answer: Let the zeroes be α and 1/α. Their product is α × (1/α) = 1. But the product of zeroes is c/a. So c/a = 1, which gives a = c.
35

Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are 2 + √3 and 2 − √3.

Answer: Sum = (2 + √3) + (2 − √3) = 4. Product = (2 + √3)(2 − √3) = 4 − 3 = 1. Required polynomial: x² − (sum)x + product = x² − 4x + 1 (or any non-zero multiple k(x² − 4x + 1)).
36

If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 3x − 10, find the value of 1/α + 1/β.

Answer: α + β = 3 and αβ = −10. 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = 3/(−10) = −3/10. (Check: the zeroes are 5 and −2, and 1/5 − 1/2 = −3/10.)

Competency-Based Questions 10

Apply the idea to a real situation.

37

A dairy in Anand has two milk tankers holding 850 L and 680 L. What is the capacity of the largest can that can measure the milk of either tanker an exact number of times?

  • (a) 85 L
  • (b) 170 L
  • (c) 340 L
  • (d) 34 L
Answer: (b) 170 L
850 = 2 × 5² × 17 and 680 = 2³ × 5 × 17, so HCF = 2 × 5 × 17 = 170 L. 85 L and 34 L also divide both, but they are not the largest.
38

Two DTC buses leave a depot in Delhi, one every 15 minutes and the other every 25 minutes. Both leave together at 6:00 a.m. When will they next leave together?

  • (a) 6:40 a.m.
  • (b) 7:15 a.m.
  • (c) 6:25 a.m.
  • (d) 8:00 a.m.
Answer: (b) 7:15 a.m.
LCM(15, 25) = 3 × 5² = 75 minutes = 1 h 15 min, so 7:15 a.m. Adding 15 + 25 = 40 min is a common mistake.
39

A classroom floor measures 4.5 m by 3.6 m. The school wants to cover it with identical square tiles of the largest possible size, with no tile cut. What is the side of each tile?

  • (a) 45 cm
  • (b) 18 cm
  • (c) 90 cm
  • (d) 30 cm
Answer: (c) 90 cm
In cm: 450 = 2 × 3² × 5² and 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, so HCF = 2 × 3² × 5 = 90 cm. That needs 5 × 4 = 20 tiles.
40

For Raksha Bandhan, a shop packs 96 rakhis and 120 chocolates into identical gift boxes, with no item left over. What is the greatest number of boxes it can make?

  • (a) 12
  • (b) 24
  • (c) 48
  • (d) 8
Answer: (b) 24
HCF(96, 120): 96 = 2⁵ × 3, 120 = 2³ × 3 × 5, so HCF = 2³ × 3 = 24. Each box gets 4 rakhis and 5 chocolates.
41

A small business's profit (in ₹ thousand) on selling x hundred diyas is P(x) = −x² + 10x − 16. At which sales levels does the business just break even (P = 0)?

  • (a) 200 and 800 diyas
  • (b) −200 and −800 diyas
  • (c) 400 diyas only
  • (d) 100 and 1600 diyas
Answer: (a) 200 and 800 diyas
−x² + 10x − 16 = −(x − 2)(x − 8), so P = 0 at x = 2 and x = 8, i.e. 200 and 800 diyas. Negative sales make no sense.
42

The area of a rectangular kitchen garden is (x² + 7x + 12) m² and its length is (x + 4) m. What is its width?

  • (a) (x + 2) m
  • (b) (x + 6) m
  • (c) (x + 3) m
  • (d) (x − 3) m
Answer: (c) (x + 3) m
x² + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4), so width = area ÷ length = (x + 3) m.
43

A fielder throws a cricket ball straight up from ground level. Its height after t seconds is h(t) = −5t² + 20t metres. After how long does it come back to the ground?

  • (a) 2 s
  • (b) 4 s
  • (c) 5 s
  • (d) 20 s
Answer: (b) 4 s
h(t) = −5t(t − 4) = 0 gives t = 0 (the throw) and t = 4 s (landing). At t = 2 s the ball is at its highest point.
44

In a maths lab, a student draws the graph of y = p(x). It crosses the x-axis at x = −2, 1 and 4 and does not meet it anywhere else. How many zeroes does p(x) have?

  • (a) 1
  • (b) 2
  • (c) 4
  • (d) 3
Answer: (d) 3
The zeroes are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph meets the x-axis: −2, 1 and 4.
45

A school cyber club sets a locker puzzle: the code is the smallest natural number that is exactly divisible by every number from 1 to 10. What is the code?

  • (a) 1260
  • (b) 5040
  • (c) 2520
  • (d) 3628800
Answer: (c) 2520
LCM(1, …, 10) = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 = 2520. 1260 is not divisible by 8; 3628800 = 10! is a common multiple, but not the smallest.
46

Ananya measures the diagonal of a square floor tile of side 1 m as √2 m. Which statement about this length is correct?

  • (a) It is exactly 1.414 m
  • (b) It is exactly 7/5 m
  • (c) It is irrational, so it cannot be written as p/q with integers p, q (q ≠ 0)
  • (d) It is rational, because it can be measured with a scale
Answer: (c) It is irrational, so it cannot be written as p/q with integers p, q (q ≠ 0)
√2 is irrational. 1.414 and 7/5 are only approximations, and a measured value is always an approximation too.

Knowledge Check 10

Quick recall: one or two lines each.

47

State the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

Answer: Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.
48

Find the HCF and LCM of 6 and 20 by prime factorisation.

Answer: 6 = 2 × 3 and 20 = 2² × 5. HCF = 2 and LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60.
49

How are the HCF and LCM of two positive integers a and b related to a and b?

Answer: HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b. (This holds for two numbers, not in general for three.)
50

What is an irrational number? Give two examples.

Answer: A number that cannot be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. Examples: √2, √3 (also π).
51

Write the prime factorisation of 1620.

Answer: 1620 = 2² × 3⁴ × 5 (4 × 81 × 5 = 1620).
52

What is a zero of a polynomial p(x)?

Answer: A real number k is a zero of p(x) if p(k) = 0. For example, 2 is a zero of x − 2.
53

What is the greatest number of zeroes a polynomial of degree n can have? How many can a quadratic polynomial have?

Answer: At most n zeroes. A quadratic polynomial has at most 2 zeroes (it may have 2, 1 or 0 real zeroes).
54

Write the relationships between the zeroes α, β and the coefficients of ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0).

Answer: α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a.
55

Write a quadratic polynomial whose sum of zeroes is S and product of zeroes is P.

Answer: x² − Sx + P, or more generally k(x² − Sx + P), where k is any non-zero real number.
56

What is the geometrical meaning of the zeroes of a polynomial p(x)?

Answer: They are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y = p(x) meets the x-axis.
