Assertion and Reasoning 10

Read both statements and choose the correct option from the key.

Key: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A. (C) A is true, but R is false. (D) A is false, but R is true.
1

Assertion (A): The molality of a solution does not change when the solution is heated.

Reason (R): Molality is defined using the mass of the solvent, and mass does not change with temperature.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. Molality = moles of solute ÷ mass of solvent in kg. Neither quantity depends on temperature, so R explains A. (Molarity changes because volume changes with temperature.)
2

Assertion (A): Fish and other aquatic animals are more comfortable in cold water than in warm water.

Reason (R): The Henry's law constant (K_H) of oxygen increases with temperature, so its solubility in water decreases as water gets warmer.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. p = K_H·x; a larger K_H at higher temperature means a smaller mole fraction x of dissolved O₂ at the same partial pressure. Cold water holds more oxygen, so R explains A.
3

Assertion (A): A mixture of chloroform and acetone shows negative deviation from Raoult's law.

Reason (R): Chloroform forms hydrogen bonds with acetone, so the A–B attractions are stronger than the A–A and B–B attractions.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. The new C–H···O hydrogen bonds hold the molecules more tightly, fewer molecules escape, and the vapour pressure is lower than predicted. So R explains A.
4

Assertion (A): Reverse osmosis is used to obtain drinking water from sea water.

Reason (R): In osmosis, solvent flows through a semipermeable membrane from a less concentrated solution to a more concentrated one.

Answer: (B) — Both are true, but R only defines ordinary osmosis. A is true because a pressure larger than the osmotic pressure is applied on the sea-water side, which reverses the flow and pushes pure water out. R does not explain this.
5

Assertion (A): Elevation of boiling point is a colligative property.

Reason (R): The molal elevation constant (Kb) of water is 0.52 K kg mol⁻¹.

Answer: (B) — Both are true. A is true because ΔTb depends only on the number of solute particles, not on their nature. R just gives a value of Kb and does not explain why the property is colligative.
6

Assertion (A): For an ideal solution, the enthalpy of mixing and the volume change on mixing are both zero.

Reason (R): In an ideal solution, the solute–solvent attractions are much stronger than the solute–solute and solvent–solvent attractions.

Answer: (C) — A is true: ΔmixH = 0 and ΔmixV = 0. R is false: in an ideal solution the A–B attractions are nearly equal to the A–A and B–B attractions. Stronger A–B attractions give negative deviation.
7

Assertion (A): The van't Hoff factor of ethanoic (acetic) acid dissolved in benzene is less than 1.

Reason (R): Ethanoic acid dissociates into ions when dissolved in benzene.

Answer: (C) — A is true. R is false: in benzene, ethanoic acid molecules join in pairs (dimerise) through hydrogen bonds. Association reduces the number of particles, so i < 1 and the observed molar mass is about double.
8

Assertion (A): The mole fraction of a component in a solution changes when the solution is heated.

Reason (R): Mole fraction is a ratio of numbers of moles, so it is independent of temperature.

Answer: (D) — A is false and R is true. Heating does not change the number of moles of each component, so the mole fraction stays the same.
9

Assertion (A): Dissolving a non-volatile solute such as sugar in water increases the vapour pressure of the water.

Reason (R): Solute particles occupy part of the surface, so fewer solvent molecules escape into the vapour.

Answer: (D) — A is false: a non-volatile solute lowers the vapour pressure. R is true and is the reason for that lowering.
10

Assertion (A): A 0.1 m aqueous solution of K₂SO₄ freezes at a lower temperature than a 0.1 m aqueous solution of NaCl.

Reason (R): K₂SO₄ gives three ions per formula unit while NaCl gives two, so K₂SO₄ has the larger van't Hoff factor.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. ΔTf = i·Kf·m. With the same molality, i ≈ 3 for K₂SO₄ and i ≈ 2 for NaCl, so the depression is larger for K₂SO₄. R explains A.

Case Studies 16

Read each passage and answer the questions that follow.

Case 1: Gases that dissolve under pressure

At a soft-drink bottling plant in Pune, carbon dioxide is forced into chilled flavoured water at high pressure and the bottles are sealed at once. When a customer opens a bottle, it fizzes, and a bottle left open in the hot sun goes flat much faster than one kept in the fridge. A naval diver training off the coast of Goa learns that breathing compressed air deep underwater makes more nitrogen dissolve in the blood, and that tanks for deep dives are filled with air diluted with helium. Meanwhile, climbers in Ladakh feel weak and cannot think clearly because their blood carries less oxygen.
11

The fizzing when the bottle is opened is best explained by:

  • (a) Raoult's law
  • (b) Henry's law
  • (c) The law of conservation of mass
  • (d) The van't Hoff equation for osmotic pressure
Answer: (b) Henry's law — Solubility of a gas is proportional to its partial pressure (p = K_H·x). On opening, the CO₂ pressure above the liquid falls and the extra dissolved gas escapes as bubbles.
12

An open bottle in the hot sun goes flat faster than one in the fridge because, as temperature rises:

  • (a) K_H decreases and gas solubility increases
  • (b) K_H increases and gas solubility decreases
  • (c) the partial pressure of CO₂ in the air increases
  • (d) CO₂ reacts with sugar in the drink
Answer: (b) K_H increases and gas solubility decreases — Dissolving a gas is usually exothermic, so warming reduces solubility. In terms of Henry's law, K_H gets larger with temperature, so x = p/K_H gets smaller.
13

Helium is used to dilute the air in tanks for deep diving because helium:

  • (a) is much less soluble in blood than nitrogen
  • (b) supplies extra oxygen to the blood
  • (c) is heavier than nitrogen and sinks
  • (d) reacts with nitrogen and removes it
Answer: (a) is much less soluble in blood than nitrogen — Less gas dissolves in the blood at high pressure, so fewer bubbles form when the diver rises. Nitrogen bubbles in the blood cause the painful condition called 'the bends'.
14

The weakness felt by the climbers in Ladakh is called anoxia. It happens because at high altitude:

  • (a) the temperature is low, so oxygen becomes insoluble
  • (b) the partial pressure of oxygen is low, so less oxygen dissolves in blood
  • (c) the air contains more carbon dioxide
  • (d) the K_H of oxygen becomes zero
Answer: (b) the partial pressure of oxygen is low, so less oxygen dissolves in blood — Atmospheric pressure, and with it the partial pressure of O₂, is lower at high altitude. By Henry's law, less oxygen dissolves in the blood and tissues.

Case 2: Keeping a jeep running in a Himalayan winter

An army mechanic in Leh adds ethylene glycol (C₂H₆O₂, molar mass 62 g mol⁻¹) to the water in jeep radiators before winter, because night temperatures fall far below 0 °C and pure water would freeze and crack the engine. To test the idea, a Class 12 student dissolves 62 g of ethylene glycol in 500 g of water and measures the freezing point. Her teacher points out that road workers spread common salt on icy roads for the same reason, and that sweet-shop owners pack salt with ice around kulfi moulds. Take Kf of water = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹.
15

The molality of the student's ethylene glycol solution is:

  • (a) 1 mol kg⁻¹
  • (b) 2 mol kg⁻¹
  • (c) 0.5 mol kg⁻¹
  • (d) 0.124 mol kg⁻¹
Answer: (b) 2 mol kg⁻¹ — Moles = 62 g ÷ 62 g mol⁻¹ = 1 mol. Mass of water = 500 g = 0.5 kg. Molality = 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2 mol kg⁻¹.
16

The expected freezing point of her solution is about:

  • (a) −1.86 °C
  • (b) −3.72 °C
  • (c) −0.93 °C
  • (d) −7.44 °C
Answer: (b) −3.72 °C — Ethylene glycol does not ionise (i = 1). ΔTf = Kf·m = 1.86 × 2 = 3.72 K, so the solution freezes at 0 − 3.72 = −3.72 °C.
17

Mole for mole, common salt is more effective than ethylene glycol in lowering the freezing point of water because:

  • (a) salt has a higher molar mass
  • (b) salt dissociates into Na⁺ and Cl⁻, roughly doubling the number of particles
  • (c) salt raises the vapour pressure of water
  • (d) salt reacts chemically with ice
Answer: (b) salt dissociates into Na⁺ and Cl⁻, roughly doubling the number of particles — Freezing-point depression is colligative: ΔTf = i·Kf·m. For NaCl i ≈ 2, while for ethylene glycol i = 1.
18

The salt–ice mixture around the kulfi moulds works because:

  • (a) salt raises the melting point of ice
  • (b) the brine formed melts below 0 °C, so the mixture becomes colder than 0 °C
  • (c) salt releases heat when it dissolves
  • (d) salt stops the kulfi from melting by osmosis
Answer: (b) the brine formed melts below 0 °C, so the mixture becomes colder than 0 °C — Salt lowers the freezing point of water. The ice keeps melting into the brine, taking heat from its surroundings, and the mixture settles well below 0 °C — cold enough to set the kulfi.

Case 3: Saline drips and pickles

In a district hospital in Lucknow, a nurse explains to trainees that the saline used in intravenous drips contains 0.9 g of sodium chloride in every 100 mL (9 g per litre). This solution has the same osmotic pressure as blood. She warns that if red blood cells are placed in a much more concentrated salt solution they shrink, and in pure water they swell and may burst. At home, one of the trainees notices that raw mango pieces left in salt for pickling become limp and give out juice. Take molar mass of NaCl = 58.5 g mol⁻¹, R = 0.0821 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and body temperature = 310 K.
19

A solution that has the same osmotic pressure as blood is called:

  • (a) hypertonic
  • (b) hypotonic
  • (c) isotonic
  • (d) azeotropic
Answer: (c) isotonic — Isotonic solutions have equal osmotic pressure, so there is no net flow of water into or out of the cells.
20

Assuming complete dissociation of NaCl, the osmotic pressure of the 0.9% saline at 310 K is closest to:

  • (a) 3.9 atm
  • (b) 7.8 atm
  • (c) 15.7 atm
  • (d) 0.78 atm
Answer: (b) 7.8 atm — C = 9 ÷ 58.5 = 0.154 mol L⁻¹, i = 2. π = iCRT = 2 × 0.154 × 0.0821 × 310 ≈ 7.8 atm. (3.9 atm is the answer if i is forgotten.)
21

Red blood cells placed in pure water swell because:

  • (a) water moves out of the cells by osmosis
  • (b) water moves into the cells, where the solute concentration is higher
  • (c) salt moves out of the cells
  • (d) pure water is hypertonic to the cells
Answer: (b) water moves into the cells, where the solute concentration is higher — Pure water is hypotonic to the cell contents. Solvent flows through the cell membrane towards the higher solute concentration, so the cells swell.
22

The mango pieces become limp in salt because:

  • (a) salt enters the cells and makes them burst
  • (b) water leaves the cells by osmosis into the concentrated salt solution outside
  • (c) salt dissolves the cell walls
  • (d) the mango absorbs water from the air
Answer: (b) water leaves the cells by osmosis into the concentrated salt solution outside — The salty layer is hypertonic to the cell sap, so water moves out and the cells shrink. The same loss of water kills microbes, which is why salting preserves food.

Case 4: Mixing liquids in the school laboratory

In a practical class in Chennai, students mix pairs of liquids and observe what happens. When acetone is mixed with chloroform, the test tube becomes slightly warm and the final volume is a little less than the sum of the two volumes. When ethanol is mixed with acetone, the tube cools and the volume increases slightly. The teacher then shows them data for two liquids P and Q that form an ideal solution: at 300 K the vapour pressure of pure P is 450 mm Hg and that of pure Q is 700 mm Hg. She adds that 95% ethanol cannot be made pure by simple fractional distillation.
23

The acetone–chloroform mixture shows:

  • (a) positive deviation, with ΔmixH > 0
  • (b) negative deviation, with ΔmixH < 0
  • (c) ideal behaviour, with ΔmixH = 0
  • (d) positive deviation, with ΔmixV < 0
Answer: (b) negative deviation, with ΔmixH < 0 — Heat is released (ΔmixH < 0) and the volume decreases (ΔmixV < 0) because new hydrogen bonds form between the two liquids. Both are signs of negative deviation.
24

For a solution of P and Q with mole fraction of Q = 0.4, the total vapour pressure is:

  • (a) 550 mm Hg
  • (b) 600 mm Hg
  • (c) 575 mm Hg
  • (d) 1150 mm Hg
Answer: (a) 550 mm Hg — p_total = x_P·p°_P + x_Q·p°_Q = 0.6 × 450 + 0.4 × 700 = 270 + 280 = 550 mm Hg.
25

In the vapour above this solution, the mole fraction of Q is about:

  • (a) 0.40
  • (b) 0.51
  • (c) 0.60
  • (d) 0.28
Answer: (b) 0.51 — y_Q = p_Q ÷ p_total = 280 ÷ 550 ≈ 0.51. The vapour is richer in the more volatile liquid Q than the liquid (0.4).
26

95% ethanol cannot be purified further by fractional distillation because it:

  • (a) is a maximum boiling azeotrope
  • (b) is a minimum boiling azeotrope that boils with the same composition in liquid and vapour
  • (c) decomposes on boiling
  • (d) obeys Raoult's law exactly
Answer: (b) is a minimum boiling azeotrope that boils with the same composition in liquid and vapour — Ethanol–water shows large positive deviation and forms a minimum boiling azeotrope at about 95% ethanol by volume. Liquid and vapour have the same composition, so distillation cannot separate them further.

HOTS — Higher Order Thinking 10

Think it through before you open the answer.

27

Arrange 0.1 m aqueous solutions of glucose, NaCl and CaCl₂ in increasing order of their freezing points. Explain your order.

Answer: CaCl₂ < NaCl < glucose. ΔTf = i·Kf·m, and the molality is the same. CaCl₂ gives 3 ions (i ≈ 3), NaCl gives 2 (i ≈ 2) and glucose does not ionise (i = 1). The largest i gives the largest depression and so the lowest freezing point.
28

Liquids A and B form an ideal solution. At a certain temperature p°_A = 450 mm Hg and p°_B = 700 mm Hg. For a liquid mixture with x_B = 0.4, find the total vapour pressure and the mole fraction of B in the vapour. What do you conclude?

Answer: p_A = 0.6 × 450 = 270 mm Hg; p_B = 0.4 × 700 = 280 mm Hg; p_total = 550 mm Hg. y_B = 280 ÷ 550 ≈ 0.51. The vapour (0.51) is richer in B than the liquid (0.40): the vapour is always richer in the more volatile component, which is the basis of fractional distillation.
29

A transporter in Himachal wants the water in a 5 kg radiator to freeze only at −6 °C. What mass of ethylene glycol (molar mass 62 g mol⁻¹) must be added? Kf = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹.

Answer: ΔTf = 6 K. m = ΔTf ÷ Kf = 6 ÷ 1.86 = 3.23 mol kg⁻¹. Moles needed = 3.23 × 5 kg = 16.1 mol. Mass = 16.1 × 62 ≈ 1.0 × 10³ g, i.e. about 1.0 kg of ethylene glycol.
30

The molar mass of ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH) found from freezing-point depression in benzene comes out to be about 120 g mol⁻¹, although its formula mass is 60 g mol⁻¹. Explain, and find the van't Hoff factor.

Answer: In benzene, two molecules of ethanoic acid join through hydrogen bonds to form a dimer, (CH₃COOH)₂. This halves the number of particles, so the colligative effect is halved and the calculated molar mass doubles. i = normal molar mass ÷ observed molar mass = 60 ÷ 120 = 0.5.
31

A 0.1 m aqueous solution of a weak monobasic acid HA freezes at −0.2046 °C. Find the van't Hoff factor and the degree of dissociation of HA. Kf = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹.

Answer: ΔTf = 0.2046 K. i = ΔTf ÷ (Kf·m) = 0.2046 ÷ (1.86 × 0.1) = 1.10. For HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻, i = 1 + α, so α = 0.10, i.e. the acid is 10% dissociated.
32

When acetone and chloroform are mixed, the mixture warms up. Predict the sign of ΔmixH and ΔmixV, the type of deviation from Raoult's law and the type of azeotrope this pair can form. Give a reason.

Answer: ΔmixH < 0 (heat released) and ΔmixV < 0. Chloroform forms hydrogen bonds with acetone, so the attractions between unlike molecules are stronger than between like ones. Fewer molecules escape, the vapour pressure is lower than expected — negative deviation — and such a pair can form a maximum boiling azeotrope.
33

A 20% (w/w) aqueous solution of NaOH has a density of 1.22 g mL⁻¹. Calculate its molality and molarity, and explain why the two values differ. (Molar mass of NaOH = 40 g mol⁻¹.)

Answer: Take 100 g of solution: 20 g NaOH = 0.5 mol, water = 80 g = 0.080 kg. Molality = 0.5 ÷ 0.080 = 6.25 mol kg⁻¹. Volume = 100 ÷ 1.22 = 82.0 mL = 0.0820 L. Molarity = 0.5 ÷ 0.0820 = 6.10 mol L⁻¹. They differ because molality uses the mass of solvent only, while molarity uses the volume of the whole solution.
34

Treating sea water as 0.55 M NaCl that dissociates completely, estimate the minimum pressure a reverse-osmosis plant in Chennai must apply at 300 K. Why must the applied pressure be greater than this value? (R = 0.0821 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.)

Answer: π = iCRT = 2 × 0.55 × 0.0821 × 300 ≈ 27.1 atm. Only a pressure larger than the osmotic pressure of the sea water can stop normal osmosis and force water molecules to move the other way, from the sea water through the membrane into the fresh-water side.
35

A soda bottle opened in a cold room fizzes gently, but an identical bottle opened after lying in a hot car fizzes violently and loses most of its gas. Explain using Henry's law.

Answer: On opening, the CO₂ pressure above the drink falls, so by Henry's law (p = K_H·x) less gas can stay dissolved and bubbles form. In the warm bottle K_H is larger, so even less CO₂ can stay dissolved at the lower pressure; much more gas escapes, and it escapes faster.
36

Two beakers, one with pure water and one with a sugar solution, are kept side by side under a sealed glass bell jar. After a few days the water level in the first beaker has fallen and the level in the second has risen. Explain.

Answer: The sugar solution has a lower vapour pressure than pure water because the non-volatile solute reduces the escape of water molecules. Inside the closed jar, water evaporates from the pure-water beaker and condenses into the solution, where the vapour pressure is lower, until the water in the first beaker is used up or the pressures become equal.

Competency-Based Questions 10

Apply the idea to a real situation.

37

A water-testing lab in Rajasthan finds 3 mg of fluoride ions in 2 kg of a village's well water. The fluoride concentration is:

  • (a) 1.5 ppm
  • (b) 3 ppm
  • (c) 0.15 ppm
  • (d) 6 ppm
Answer: (a) 1.5 ppm
ppm = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × 10⁶ = (3 × 10⁻³ g ÷ 2000 g) × 10⁶ = 1.5 ppm.
38

A lab assistant must prepare 250 mL of 0.1 M NaOH solution for a titration. What mass of NaOH (molar mass 40 g mol⁻¹) should she dissolve?

  • (a) 4.0 g
  • (b) 1.0 g
  • (c) 0.4 g
  • (d) 10 g
Answer: (b) 1.0 g
Moles = M × V = 0.1 × 0.250 = 0.025 mol. Mass = 0.025 × 40 = 1.0 g, dissolved and made up to 250 mL.
39

During a hot May afternoon, fish in a village pond come up to the surface and gasp. The main reason is:

  • (a) warm water has less dissolved oxygen
  • (b) warm water has more dissolved carbon dioxide only
  • (c) sunlight makes the water hypertonic
  • (d) the vapour pressure of water falls in summer
Answer: (a) warm water has less dissolved oxygen
The solubility of gases in water decreases as temperature rises (K_H increases), so warm water holds less O₂ for the fish.
40

A home RO water purifier works by:

  • (a) boiling the water and condensing the steam
  • (b) applying a pressure greater than the osmotic pressure to push water through a semipermeable membrane
  • (c) adding salt so that impurities freeze out
  • (d) letting water flow naturally from a pure to an impure side
Answer: (b) applying a pressure greater than the osmotic pressure to push water through a semipermeable membrane
This is reverse osmosis: the applied pressure exceeds the osmotic pressure, so water molecules pass through the membrane while dissolved salts are held back.
41

A doctor tells a patient with swollen feet to cut down on salty food. The swelling, called edema, is caused by:

  • (a) excess salt in the tissues drawing in and holding water by osmosis
  • (b) salt raising the boiling point of blood
  • (c) salt lowering the osmotic pressure of body fluids
  • (d) salt reacting with water to form a gas
Answer: (a) excess salt in the tissues drawing in and holding water by osmosis
A high salt concentration in the tissues and spaces between cells raises their osmotic pressure, so water is retained there and causes puffiness.
42

Wilted marigold flowers left out after a wedding become fresh again when their stems are kept in fresh water. This is due to:

  • (a) reverse osmosis
  • (b) water entering the cells by osmosis
  • (c) diffusion of salt into the flowers
  • (d) evaporation from the petals
Answer: (b) water entering the cells by osmosis
The cell sap is more concentrated than fresh water, so water moves into the cells, they become turgid again and the flowers revive.
43

A hospital runs out of 0.9% saline. Which of these would be safe to inject into a patient's vein in its place?

  • (a) Distilled water
  • (b) A 5% NaCl solution
  • (c) Another solution isotonic with blood
  • (d) Sea water
Answer: (c) Another solution isotonic with blood
Only an isotonic solution leaves red blood cells unchanged. Distilled water is hypotonic (cells swell and burst); 5% NaCl and sea water are hypertonic (cells shrink).
44

A cook adds a spoonful of salt to water before boiling rice. The effect on the boiling point is:

  • (a) it falls slightly, because salt lowers the vapour pressure
  • (b) it rises slightly, because salt lowers the vapour pressure of water
  • (c) it rises a lot, because salt is volatile
  • (d) no change, because salt is an ionic compound
Answer: (b) it rises slightly, because salt lowers the vapour pressure of water
A non-volatile solute lowers the vapour pressure, so the water must be heated a little more to reach atmospheric pressure. With one spoonful the rise is small.
45

A student in Shimla makes a cold drink at home and wants it to stay fizzy for longer after opening. Which storage is best?

  • (a) Keep it tightly capped in the fridge
  • (b) Keep it open in the fridge
  • (c) Keep it capped near a heater
  • (d) Shake it before storing
Answer: (a) Keep it tightly capped in the fridge
A closed cap keeps the CO₂ pressure above the liquid high, and the low temperature keeps K_H small, so more gas stays dissolved (Henry's law).
46

Two juice samples are tested in a food lab. Sample X freezes at −0.40 °C and sample Y at −0.80 °C. Which conclusion is correct?

  • (a) X has twice as many dissolved particles per kg of water as Y
  • (b) Y has about twice as many dissolved particles per kg of water as X
  • (c) Both have the same concentration of dissolved particles
  • (d) Y contains less sugar than X
Answer: (b) Y has about twice as many dissolved particles per kg of water as X
ΔTf = i·Kf·m is proportional to the total molality of dissolved particles. Y's depression (0.80 K) is double X's (0.40 K).

Knowledge Check 10

Quick recall: one or two lines each.

47

Define molality. Give its unit.

Answer: Molality (m) is the number of moles of solute dissolved in 1 kg of solvent. Unit: mol kg⁻¹.
48

What is the mole fraction of a component in a solution? What is the sum of the mole fractions in a binary solution?

Answer: Mole fraction x_A = moles of A ÷ total moles of all components. In a binary solution x_A + x_B = 1.
49

State Henry's law in its common form.

Answer: The partial pressure of a gas in the vapour phase is proportional to its mole fraction in the solution: p = K_H·x, where K_H is Henry's law constant.
50

In a mixture of two volatile liquids, how is the partial vapour pressure of each liquid related to its mole fraction? Write the equations.

Answer: By Raoult's law, the partial vapour pressure of each component equals its mole fraction times the vapour pressure of the pure component: p_A = x_A·p°_A and p_B = x_B·p°_B; p_total = p_A + p_B.
51

What are colligative properties? Name the four.

Answer: Properties that depend only on the number of solute particles, not on their nature: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point and osmotic pressure.
52

Write the expression for relative lowering of vapour pressure for a dilute solution of a non-volatile solute.

Answer: (p°₁ − p₁) ÷ p°₁ = x₂, where p°₁ is the vapour pressure of the pure solvent, p₁ that of the solution and x₂ the mole fraction of the solute.
53

Write two expressions for the van't Hoff factor (i). Is i greater or less than 1 when solute particles dimerise?

Answer: i = normal molar mass ÷ abnormal (observed) molar mass, or observed colligative property ÷ calculated colligative property. When particles dimerise (associate), i < 1; for a dimer it approaches 0.5.
54

What is an azeotrope?

Answer: A binary mixture that has the same composition in the liquid and vapour phases and boils at a constant temperature, so it cannot be separated by fractional distillation.
55

Write the van't Hoff equation for osmotic pressure and name the terms.

Answer: π = CRT (π = iCRT for electrolytes), where C is the molar concentration, R the gas constant and T the temperature in kelvin.
56

Give the SI unit of the cryoscopic constant (Kf) and its value for water.

Answer: K kg mol⁻¹. For water, Kf = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹.
