Assertion and Reasoning 10

Read both statements and choose the correct option from the key.

Key: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A. (C) A is true, but R is false. (D) A is false, but R is true.
1

Assertion (A): Two electric field lines never cross each other.

Reason (R): At any point, the electric field has a unique direction.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. If two lines crossed, the tangents at the crossing point would give two directions of the field at one point, which is impossible. So R explains A.
2

Assertion (A): The charge on any body is an integral multiple of e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

Reason (R): The total charge of an isolated system remains constant.

Answer: (B) — Both are true: A is quantisation of charge and R is conservation of charge. They are independent properties, so R does not explain A.
3

Assertion (A): The electric field inside a uniformly charged thin spherical shell is zero.

Reason (R): The electric flux through any closed surface is always zero.

Answer: (C) — A is true: a Gaussian sphere inside the shell encloses no charge, so E = 0. R is false: by Gauss's law the flux through a closed surface is q_enclosed/ε₀, which is zero only if no net charge is enclosed.
4

Assertion (A): An electric dipole placed in a uniform electric field experiences a net force.

Reason (R): An electric dipole in a uniform electric field experiences a torque that tends to align it along the field.

Answer: (D) — A is false: the forces qE and −qE on the two charges are equal and opposite, so the net force is zero. R is true: τ = p × E, of magnitude pE sin θ.
5

Assertion (A): No work is done in moving a charge from one point to another on an equipotential surface.

Reason (R): The potential difference between any two points on an equipotential surface is zero.

Answer: (A) — W = q(V_B − V_A). On an equipotential surface V_B = V_A, so W = 0. R directly explains A.
6

Assertion (A): The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor increases when a dielectric slab completely fills the space between the plates.

Reason (R): Dielectrics are insulators.

Answer: (B) — Both are true. But capacitance increases (C = Kε₀A/d) because the dielectric gets polarised and reduces the field and potential difference for the same charge — not simply because it is an insulator.
7

Assertion (A): In electrostatic equilibrium, the electric field inside a conductor is zero.

Reason (R): Free electrons in a conductor move under any field until their arrangement makes the net field inside zero.

Answer: (A) — Both are true. If any field remained inside, the free electrons would keep drifting; equilibrium is reached only when the net internal field is zero. So R explains A.
8

Assertion (A): The electric potential at the midpoint of the line joining charges +q and −q is zero.

Reason (R): The electric field at the midpoint of the line joining charges +q and −q is zero.

Answer: (C) — A is true: V = kq/r + k(−q)/r = 0. R is false: at the midpoint both fields point from +q towards −q and add up, so E = 2kq/r² ≠ 0.
9

Assertion (A): The equivalent capacitance of two capacitors connected in series is greater than either capacitance.

Reason (R): In a series combination, each capacitor carries the same charge.

Answer: (D) — A is false: 1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂, so C is smaller than the smallest capacitance. R is true.
10

Assertion (A): The electric field just outside the surface of a charged conductor is perpendicular to the surface.

Reason (R): The surface of a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is an equipotential surface, and the field is always normal to equipotential surfaces.

Answer: (A) — Any tangential component of field would move charges along the surface. Since the surface is equipotential and E is normal to equipotentials, R correctly explains A.

Case Studies 16

Read each passage and answer the questions that follow.

Case 1: Two charged spheres on insulating stands

In a physics lab in Kota, a teacher mounts two identical small metal spheres A and B on insulating stands. Sphere A carries a charge of +6 μC and sphere B carries −2 μC. Their centres are 30 cm apart in air. She then lets the spheres touch for a moment and puts them back at the same separation. Finally she asks the class what would happen if the original arrangement were placed in water, whose dielectric constant is about 80. (Take k = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻².)
11

The force between the spheres in the original arrangement is:

  • (a) 1.2 N, repulsive
  • (b) 0.4 N, attractive
  • (c) 1.2 N, attractive
  • (d) 3.6 N, attractive
Answer: (c) 1.2 N, attractive — F = kq₁q₂/r² = 9 × 10⁹ × 6 × 10⁻⁶ × 2 × 10⁻⁶ / (0.3)² = 0.108/0.09 = 1.2 N. Unlike charges attract.
12

After touching and returning to 30 cm, the force between them is:

  • (a) 0.4 N, repulsive
  • (b) 0.4 N, attractive
  • (c) 1.6 N, repulsive
  • (d) Zero
Answer: (a) 0.4 N, repulsive — Total charge +4 μC is shared equally: +2 μC each. F = 9 × 10⁹ × (2 × 10⁻⁶)² / 0.09 = 0.4 N; like charges repel.
13

How many electrons left sphere B when the spheres touched?

  • (a) 1.25 × 10¹³
  • (b) 5.0 × 10¹³
  • (c) 2.5 × 10¹³
  • (d) 2.5 × 10¹²
Answer: (c) 2.5 × 10¹³ — B changed from −2 μC to +2 μC, i.e. lost 4 μC of negative charge. n = q/e = 4 × 10⁻⁶ / 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.5 × 10¹³ electrons.
14

If the original arrangement (+6 μC and −2 μC, 30 cm apart) were placed in water (K = 80), the force would be:

  • (a) 96 N
  • (b) 1.2 N
  • (c) 0.15 N
  • (d) 0.015 N
Answer: (d) 0.015 N — In a medium, F = F_air/K = 1.2/80 = 0.015 N. The force decreases, it does not increase.

Case 2: Gauss's law at work

Gauss's law states that the total electric flux through a closed surface equals the net charge enclosed divided by ε₀. It makes it easy to find the field of symmetric charge distributions. A student places a point charge of 8.85 μC at the centre of an imaginary cube. In another problem, a very long straight wire on a lab bench carries a uniform linear charge density of 2 × 10⁻⁷ C/m. A third problem involves a large, uniformly charged flat sheet. (Take ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C² N⁻¹ m⁻².)
15

The total electric flux through the cube is:

  • (a) 1.0 × 10⁶ N m² C⁻¹
  • (b) 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ N m² C⁻¹
  • (c) 1.67 × 10⁵ N m² C⁻¹
  • (d) Zero
Answer: (a) 1.0 × 10⁶ N m² C⁻¹ — Φ = q/ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻⁶ / 8.85 × 10⁻¹² = 1.0 × 10⁶ N m² C⁻¹.
16

The student now shifts the same charge to one corner of the cube. The total flux through the cube becomes about:

  • (a) 1.0 × 10⁶ N m² C⁻¹
  • (b) 1.67 × 10⁵ N m² C⁻¹
  • (c) 1.25 × 10⁵ N m² C⁻¹
  • (d) Zero
Answer: (c) 1.25 × 10⁵ N m² C⁻¹ — A charge at a corner is shared equally by the 8 cubes that meet there, so this cube gets 1/8 of the total: (q/ε₀)/8 = 1.0 × 10⁶ / 8 = 1.25 × 10⁵ N m² C⁻¹.
17

The electric field at a distance of 10 cm from the long wire is about:

  • (a) 1.8 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹
  • (b) 3.6 × 10³ N C⁻¹
  • (c) 3.6 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹
  • (d) 7.2 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹
Answer: (c) 3.6 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹ — E = λ/(2πε₀r) = 2kλ/r = 2 × 9 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻⁷ / 0.1 = 3.6 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹, directed radially outward.
18

For the large uniformly charged sheet (surface charge density σ), the field near it:

  • (a) is σ/ε₀ and falls as 1/r
  • (b) is σ/2ε₀ and does not depend on the distance from the sheet
  • (c) is σ/2ε₀ and falls as 1/r²
  • (d) is zero on both sides
Answer: (b) is σ/2ε₀ and does not depend on the distance from the sheet — Using a cylindrical Gaussian surface through the sheet: 2EA = σA/ε₀, so E = σ/2ε₀, independent of distance.

Case 3: A home-made capacitor

For a project, Riya makes a parallel plate capacitor from two aluminium sheets, each of area 0.02 m², kept 1 mm apart with air between them. She connects it to a 100 V DC supply and leaves it connected. Later, still with the supply connected, she slides a sheet of glass-like material of dielectric constant 5 so that it completely fills the gap. (Take ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C² N⁻¹ m⁻².)
19

The capacitance of the air-filled capacitor is about:

  • (a) 17.7 pF
  • (b) 177 pF
  • (c) 1.77 μF
  • (d) 885 pF
Answer: (b) 177 pF — C = ε₀A/d = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.02 / 10⁻³ = 1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰ F = 177 pF.
20

The charge on each plate with air between them is about:

  • (a) 1.77 × 10⁻⁸ C
  • (b) 1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰ C
  • (c) 1.77 × 10⁻⁶ C
  • (d) 8.85 × 10⁻⁸ C
Answer: (a) 1.77 × 10⁻⁸ C — Q = CV = 1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 100 = 1.77 × 10⁻⁸ C.
21

The energy stored with air between the plates is about:

  • (a) 1.77 × 10⁻⁶ J
  • (b) 8.85 × 10⁻⁵ J
  • (c) 4.4 × 10⁻⁷ J
  • (d) 8.85 × 10⁻⁷ J
Answer: (d) 8.85 × 10⁻⁷ J — U = ½CV² = ½ × 1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰ × (100)² = 8.85 × 10⁻⁷ J.
22

When the dielectric slab is inserted with the supply still connected:

  • (a) the charge stays the same and the voltage falls to 20 V
  • (b) the charge on the plates becomes 5 times
  • (c) the capacitance falls to one-fifth
  • (d) the stored energy falls to one-fifth
Answer: (b) the charge on the plates becomes 5 times — V stays fixed at 100 V (supply connected) and C becomes KC = 5C, so Q = CV and the energy ½CV² both become 5 times. Option (a) describes an isolated capacitor.

Case 4: Energy of a pair of charges

A student studies a system of two point charges fixed in air: q₁ = +4 μC and q₂ = −2 μC, 20 cm apart. She calculates the electrostatic potential energy of the system and the work an external agent must do to pull the charges far apart. She also looks for a point on the line between them where the net electric potential is zero, and sketches equipotential surfaces around a single point charge. (Take k = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻².)
23

The electrostatic potential energy of the system is:

  • (a) +0.36 J
  • (b) −0.36 J
  • (c) −1.8 J
  • (d) −0.072 J
Answer: (b) −0.36 J — U = kq₁q₂/r = 9 × 10⁹ × (4 × 10⁻⁶)(−2 × 10⁻⁶) / 0.2 = −0.36 J. It is negative because the charges attract.
24

The minimum work an external agent must do to separate the charges to infinity is:

  • (a) −0.36 J
  • (b) Zero
  • (c) +0.36 J
  • (d) +0.72 J
Answer: (c) +0.36 J — W = U_final − U_initial = 0 − (−0.36 J) = +0.36 J.
25

On the line segment between the charges, the potential is zero at a distance of:

  • (a) 10 cm from q₁
  • (b) 6.7 cm from q₁
  • (c) 13.3 cm from q₁
  • (d) 40 cm from q₁
Answer: (c) 13.3 cm from q₁ — k(4 μC)/x = k(2 μC)/(0.2 − x) ⇒ 4(0.2 − x) = 2x ⇒ x = 0.8/6 = 0.133 m ≈ 13.3 cm from q₁ (6.7 cm from q₂).
26

The equipotential surfaces of a single point charge are:

  • (a) concentric spheres centred on the charge
  • (b) parallel planes
  • (c) coaxial cylinders
  • (d) radial lines from the charge
Answer: (a) concentric spheres centred on the charge — V = kq/r depends only on r, so all points at the same distance from the charge are at the same potential. Radial lines are the field lines.

HOTS — Higher Order Thinking 10

Think it through before you open the answer.

27

Two point charges +q and +4q are kept a distance r apart. Where should a third charge be placed on the line joining them so that the net force on it is zero?

Answer: Between them, at a distance x from +q. For zero force: kq/x² = k(4q)/(r − x)² ⇒ (r − x)² = 4x² ⇒ r − x = 2x ⇒ x = r/3. So the third charge goes at r/3 from +q (2r/3 from +4q). The result is the same whatever the sign or size of the third charge.
28

A plastic comb rubbed on dry hair attracts small bits of paper, although the paper is neutral. Explain.

Answer: The charged comb induces opposite charge on the near side of each paper bit and like charge on the far side (polarisation). The attraction on the nearer opposite charge is stronger than the repulsion on the farther like charge, because force decreases with distance. So there is a net attraction.
29

An electric dipole of moment 4 × 10⁻⁹ C m is placed at 30° to a uniform electric field of 5 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹. Find the torque on it. In which orientation is it in stable equilibrium?

Answer: τ = pE sin θ = 4 × 10⁻⁹ × 5 × 10⁴ × sin 30° = 2 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.5 = 1 × 10⁻⁴ N m. Stable equilibrium is when p is parallel to E (θ = 0°): the torque is zero and the potential energy −pE is minimum.
30

The charge enclosed by a closed Gaussian surface is doubled and the surface is made three times larger. How does the flux through it change? Does the electric field at points on the surface also stay in the same proportion?

Answer: The flux doubles, because Φ = q_enclosed/ε₀ depends only on the charge enclosed, not on the size or shape of the surface. The field at points on the surface does change with size (for a point charge at the centre of a sphere, E = kq/r² becomes 2/9 of its value), but the larger area exactly compensates.
31

Can the electric field be zero at a point where the electric potential is not zero? Give an example.

Answer: Yes. At the midpoint between two equal positive charges q separated by d, the fields are equal and opposite, so E = 0. But the potentials add: V = kq/(d/2) + kq/(d/2) = 4kq/d ≠ 0. E depends on how V changes with position, not on the value of V.
32

Capacitors of 2 μF, 3 μF and 6 μF are connected in series across a 12 V battery. Find the equivalent capacitance, the charge on each capacitor and the voltage across the 2 μF capacitor. What would the equivalent capacitance be in parallel?

Answer: Series: 1/C = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = (3 + 2 + 1)/6 = 1, so C = 1 μF. Charge on each = CV = 1 μF × 12 V = 12 μC. Voltage across 2 μF = 12 μC / 2 μF = 6 V (the others get 4 V and 2 V; 6 + 4 + 2 = 12 V). Parallel: C = 2 + 3 + 6 = 11 μF.
33

A capacitor is charged by a battery and then disconnected. A dielectric slab of dielectric constant K is now inserted to fill the gap. How do the charge, capacitance, potential difference and stored energy change?

Answer: Charge Q stays the same (the capacitor is isolated). Capacitance becomes KC. Potential difference V = Q/C becomes V/K. Energy U = Q²/2C becomes U/K, so energy decreases; the field pulls the slab in and does work on it.
34

A capacitor C charged to a potential V is connected to an identical uncharged capacitor. Find the common potential and compare the final energy with the initial energy.

Answer: Charge CV is shared between two equal capacitances (total 2C), so the common potential is V/2. Initial energy = ½CV². Final energy = 2 × ½C(V/2)² = ¼CV², i.e. half the initial energy. The other half is lost as heat (and radiation) in the connecting wires.
35

A test charge is carried once around a circle centred on a fixed point charge Q. How much work is done by the electric field? Would the answer change for any closed path?

Answer: Zero. Every point on the circle is at the same distance from Q, so it is an equipotential path and the field is always perpendicular to the motion. For any closed path the work is also zero, because the electrostatic force is conservative: W = q(V_final − V_initial) = 0 when start and end points are the same.
36

Two large parallel plane sheets carry uniform surface charge densities +σ and −σ. Find the electric field between the sheets and outside them.

Answer: Each sheet produces a field of magnitude σ/2ε₀. Between the sheets both fields point from the +σ sheet to the −σ sheet and add: E = σ/ε₀. Outside, the two fields are opposite and cancel: E = 0. This is the field in a parallel plate capacitor.

Competency-Based Questions 10

Apply the idea to a real situation.

37

Petrol tankers on Indian highways often have a metal chain hanging down that touches the road. Why?

  • (a) To reduce air resistance
  • (b) To let charge built up by friction flow to the earth and avoid sparks
  • (c) To warn other vehicles by its sound
  • (d) To keep the tanker cool
Answer: (b) To let charge built up by friction flow to the earth and avoid sparks
Friction between the moving tanker, air and fuel charges the body. The chain earths it, preventing a spark that could ignite petrol vapour.
38

Caught in a thunderstorm on a highway, which is the safest place to wait?

  • (a) Under a tall isolated tree
  • (b) Inside a car with its windows closed
  • (c) On top of an open field
  • (d) Next to an electric pole
Answer: (b) Inside a car with its windows closed
A car's metal body acts as a closed conductor: charge stays on the outside and the field inside is nearly zero (electrostatic shielding).
39

In dry winter weather in Shimla, a woollen sweater crackles and clings to a synthetic shirt when pulled off. What causes this?

  • (a) Protons move from one fabric to the other
  • (b) Electrons are transferred between the fabrics by rubbing
  • (c) Charge is created by friction
  • (d) The fabrics become magnetised
Answer: (b) Electrons are transferred between the fabrics by rubbing
Rubbing transfers electrons, so one fabric becomes negative and the other positive; unlike charges attract. Charge is not created (it is conserved), and protons do not move.
40

A technician must protect a sensitive electronic instrument from external electric fields. What should he do?

  • (a) Wrap it in plastic sheet
  • (b) Keep it inside a closed metal box
  • (c) Keep it near a charged object
  • (d) Wrap it in cotton cloth
Answer: (b) Keep it inside a closed metal box
The electric field inside a cavity in a conductor is zero, whatever the outside field. Plastic and cloth are insulators and do not shield.
41

A ceiling fan needs a 2.5 μF capacitor, but the electrician has only 5 μF capacitors. How can he get 2.5 μF?

  • (a) Two 5 μF capacitors in parallel
  • (b) Two 5 μF capacitors in series
  • (c) One 5 μF capacitor alone
  • (d) Three 5 μF capacitors in parallel
Answer: (b) Two 5 μF capacitors in series
Series: 1/C = 1/5 + 1/5 = 2/5, so C = 2.5 μF. Parallel would give 10 μF.
42

Old FM radios are tuned by turning a knob that changes how much the plates of a variable capacitor overlap. If turning the knob increases the overlapping area, the capacitance:

  • (a) decreases
  • (b) stays the same
  • (c) increases
  • (d) becomes zero
Answer: (c) increases
For parallel plates C = ε₀A/d, so C is directly proportional to the overlapping area A.
43

A bird sits safely on a single high-voltage overhead wire. Which reason is correct?

  • (a) The wire carries no charge
  • (b) Birds have very high resistance to electricity
  • (c) Both feet are at practically the same potential, so there is no potential difference across the bird
  • (d) The electric field near the wire is zero
Answer: (c) Both feet are at practically the same potential, so there is no potential difference across the bird
Charge flows only when there is a potential difference. Touching another wire or a pole at the same time would be dangerous.
44

A defibrillator in an ambulance uses a 50 μF capacitor charged to 2000 V. How much energy can it deliver?

  • (a) 50 J
  • (b) 200 J
  • (c) 0.1 J
  • (d) 100 J
Answer: (d) 100 J
U = ½CV² = ½ × 50 × 10⁻⁶ × (2000)² = ½ × 50 × 10⁻⁶ × 4 × 10⁶ = 100 J. Forgetting the ½ gives 200 J.
45

Thermal power plants use electrostatic precipitators to remove fly ash from chimney smoke. How do they work?

  • (a) Ash particles are charged and then attracted to oppositely charged plates
  • (b) Ash particles are cooled until they fall
  • (c) Ash is dissolved in water sprayed in the chimney
  • (d) Ash particles are magnetised and pulled to magnets
Answer: (a) Ash particles are charged and then attracted to oppositely charged plates
A strong electric field charges the particles; the charged particles experience a force qE and collect on the plates.
46

A charged parallel plate capacitor is disconnected from its battery, and its plate separation is then doubled. What happens to the potential difference across it?

  • (a) It halves
  • (b) It stays the same
  • (c) It doubles
  • (d) It becomes four times
Answer: (c) It doubles
Q stays constant (isolated). C = ε₀A/d halves when d doubles, so V = Q/C doubles. (The field Q/(ε₀A) between the plates stays the same.)

Knowledge Check 10

Quick recall: one or two lines each.

47

State Coulomb's law and give the value of k in SI units.

Answer: The force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them: F = kq₁q₂/r², with k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻².
48

Write the SI unit of electric field.

Answer: Newton per coulomb (N C⁻¹), which is the same as volt per metre (V m⁻¹).
49

Define electric dipole moment. State its SI unit and direction.

Answer: p = q × 2a, the magnitude of either charge multiplied by the separation between them. SI unit: C m. It is a vector directed from −q to +q.
50

State Gauss's law in electrostatics.

Answer: The total electric flux through any closed surface is 1/ε₀ times the net charge enclosed by it: Φ = ∮E·dS = q_enclosed/ε₀.
51

Write the expression for the electric field due to an infinitely long straight wire with uniform linear charge density λ.

Answer: E = λ/(2πε₀r), directed radially (outward for positive λ), where r is the perpendicular distance from the wire.
52

Define electric potential at a point. What is its SI unit?

Answer: The work done by an external agent in bringing a unit positive test charge from infinity to that point, without acceleration. SI unit: volt (1 V = 1 J C⁻¹).
53

How is the electric field related to the electric potential?

Answer: E = −dV/dr. The field points in the direction in which the potential decreases most steeply.
54

Write the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor with a dielectric of constant K filling the gap.

Answer: C = Kε₀A/d, where A is the plate area and d the separation; it is K times the capacitance with air.
55

Write three expressions for the energy stored in a capacitor.

Answer: U = ½CV² = Q²/2C = ½QV.
56

What is an equipotential surface? Give an example.

Answer: A surface on which every point is at the same electric potential. Example: concentric spheres around a point charge, or planes perpendicular to a uniform field.
