Why is sin²θ + cos²θ = 1?

MathsCBSE Class 10⏱ 5 min readUpdated 15 November 2026
BM
Biswarup Mukherjee · Teaching Administrator, CBSEMock20 years of teaching · Reviewed by subject teachers · About us

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the most used identity in trigonometry. It appears in proofs, simplifications and “find the value” questions in almost every Class 10 paper. It is also one of the easiest formulas to see.

The short answer

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 1, the two shorter sides are cos θ and sin θ. Pythagoras' theorem then says cos²θ + sin²θ = 1² = 1.

Step 1: draw a unit circle

Draw a circle of radius 1 with its centre at the origin. Mark a point P on the circle so that the radius OP makes an angle θ with the x-axis.

Step 2: make a right-angled triangle

Drop a perpendicular from P to the x-axis at M. Triangle OMP is right-angled at M, with:

  • hypotenuse OP = 1 (the radius),
  • base OM = OP cos θ = cos θ,
  • height MP = OP sin θ = sin θ.

Step 3: apply Pythagoras

OM² + MP² = OP² gives cos²θ + sin²θ = 1. As P moves round the circle the triangle changes shape, but the hypotenuse is always the radius, 1 — so the identity holds for every angle.

The same proof without a circle

In any right triangle ABC (right angle at B), sin A = BC/AC and cos A = AB/AC. Then sin²A + cos²A = (BC² + AB²)/AC² = AC²/AC² = 1, using Pythagoras (AB² + BC² = AC²). This is the version CBSE expects in a written proof.

Two more identities for free

Divide sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by cos²θ and you get tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ. Divide it by sin²θ and you get 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ. Remember one identity and you have all three.

A worked example

Q. If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find cos θ and tan θ.

A. cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25, so cos θ = 4/5 (positive, since θ is acute). tan θ = sin θ / cos θ = (3/5) ÷ (4/5) = 3/4.

Common mistakes

  • Writing sin²θ as sin(θ²). sin²θ means (sin θ)².
  • Forgetting that cos θ could be negative for angles beyond 90°. In Class 10, angles are acute, so take the positive root — but say so.
  • Starting a “prove that” question from the result. Begin from one side (usually the more complicated one) and work towards the other.

Try it yourself

If cos θ = 12/13 (θ acute), find sin θ.

sin²θ = 1 − 144/169 = 25/169, so sin θ = 5/13.

Simplify (1 − sin²θ) sec²θ.

1 − sin²θ = cos²θ, and cos²θ × sec²θ = 1.

Prove that sec²θ − tan²θ = 1.

Divide sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by cos²θ: tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ, so sec²θ − tan²θ = 1.
