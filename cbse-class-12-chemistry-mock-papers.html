Q1. The osmotic pressure of 0.1 M NaCl solution at 300 K (fully dissociated) is:
  1. 0.1 RT
  2. 0.2 RT
  3. 0.05 RT
  4. 0.4 RT

Answer: (B) 0.2 RT

Q2. The IUPAC name of [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ is:
  1. tetraamminedichlorocobalt(III) ion
  2. tetraaminedichlorocobalt(II) ion
  3. diamminetetrachlorocobaltate(III)
  4. tetrachlorodiamminecobalt(III)

Answer: (C) diamminetetrachlorocobaltate(III)

Q3. Which of the following electrolytes shows maximum depression in freezing point at the same molality?
  1. KCl
  2. MgSO₄
  3. Al₂(SO₄)₃
  4. NaCl

Answer: (C) Al₂(SO₄)₃

Q4. The rate of reaction doubles when temperature increases from 27°C to 37°C. Activation energy (R=8.314 J/mol·K) is approximately:
  1. 53.6 kJ/mol
  2. 16 kJ/mol
  3. 26.8 kJ/mol
  4. 5.36 kJ/mol

Answer: (A) 53.6 kJ/mol

Q5. In S_N1 reaction of (CH₃)₃CBr with OH⁻, the rate depends on:
  1. [(CH₃)₃CBr][OH⁻]
  2. [OH⁻]
  3. [(CH₃)₃CBr]
  4. both equally

Answer: (C) [(CH₃)₃CBr]

Q6. The increasing order of basic strength of amines: (CH₃)₂NH, NH₃, C₆H₅NH₂

Q9. Explain why Frenkel defect is not found in alkali metal halides but Schottky defect is.

Model answer: Alkali halides: large cation/anion radius ratio → cation too large to fit in interstitial spaces → Frenkel defect not possible. Schottky defect occurs when equal numbers of cations and anions leave lattice → electrical neutrality maintained → common in NaCl.

Q10. (i) What is meant by kosmotropic (structure-making) ions in aqueous solution? Give an example. (ii) Explain the significance of linkage isomerism with an example. (iii) Why do transition metals form coloured compounds?

Model answer: (i) Kosmotropic ions strongly hydrate and organize water structure around them. E.g., F⁻, Li⁺ (small, high charge density). (ii) Linkage isomers: ambidentate ligand bonds via different atoms. [Co(NH₃)₅NO₂]²⁺ (N-bonded nitro) vs [Co(NH₃)₅ONO]²⁺ (O-bonded nitrito). (iii) Transition metals have partially filled d orbitals. d-d transitions absorb visible light → complementary colour observed.

Q11. [CBQ] Electrochemistry Lab Investigation Students study a zinc-copper electrochemical cell. Salt bridge contains KCl in agar. (i) Why does the cell stop working without salt bridge? [1m] (ii) The cell voltage decreases as reaction proceeds. Explain using Nernst equation. [1m] (iii) If E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = −0.76V and E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34V, calculate E°cell. [1m] (iv) Define standard electrode potential. [1m]

Model answer: (i) Without salt bridge: charge builds up — anode becomes +ve (Zn²⁺ accumulate) and cathode −ve → cell potential drops to zero. Salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality. (ii) Nernst: E = E° − (RT/nF)ln Q. As [Zn²⁺] increases and [Cu²⁺] decreases, Q increases → ln Q increases → E decreases. (iii) E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = 0.34 − (−0.76) = 1.10 V. (iv) Standard electrode potential: potential of electrode when all species at unit activity (1M concentration, 1 atm, 298 K) relative to SHE.

Q12. (A) (i) Draw orbital diagrams showing t₂g and eg configuration for [CoF₆]³⁻ and [Co(CN)₆]³⁻. (ii) Explain why one is high spin and other is low spin. (iii) Predict magnetic properties of both.

Model answer: (i) Co³⁺ is d⁶. [CoF₆]³⁻: weak field F⁻ → small Δ → electrons fill all d orbitals before pairing → t₂g⁴ eg² (high spin). [Co(CN)₆]³⁻: strong field CN⁻ → large Δ → t₂g⁶ eg⁰ (low spin). (ii) CN⁻ causes large crystal field splitting (Δ > P, pairing energy) → low spin. F⁻ causes small Δ < P → high spin. (iii) [CoF₆]³⁻: 4 unpaired electrons → paramagnetic. [Co(CN)₆]³⁻: 0 unpaired → diamagnetic.
