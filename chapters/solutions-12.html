About this chapter

A solution is a uniform mixture of two or more substances. This chapter covers ways of expressing concentration, Henry's law for gases, Raoult's law and ideal and non-ideal solutions, and colligative properties — relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point and osmotic pressure — together with the van't Hoff factor. Numerical problems on colligative properties are very common.

Answers & explanations — Solutions

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Raoult's law states that vapour pressure of solvent above solution is:
  1. Equal to vapour pressure of pure solvent
  2. Equal to mole fraction of solvent × vapour pressure of pure solvent
  3. Proportional to solute concentration
  4. Independent of composition

Answer: (B) Equal to mole fraction of solvent × vapour pressure of pure solvent — Raoult's law: p_A = x_A × p°_A, where x_A is mole fraction of solvent.

Q2. Relative lowering of vapour pressure is equal to:
  1. Mole fraction of solute
  2. Mole fraction of solvent
  3. Molality of solution
  4. Molarity of solution

Answer: (A) Mole fraction of solute — (p°−p)/p° = x_solute. Relative lowering = mole fraction of solute (for ideal dilute solution).

Q3. Osmotic pressure π of a dilute solution is given by:
  1. π = MRT
  2. π = nRT/V
  3. π = CRT
  4. All of these (C=M=n/V)

Answer: (D) All of these (C=M=n/V) — Van't Hoff equation: π = CRT = MRT = nRT/V. All are equivalent expressions.

Q4. Elevation in boiling point is a colligative property because it depends on:
  1. Nature of solute
  2. Colour of solution
  3. Number of solute particles
  4. Molecular weight of solute alone

Answer: (C) Number of solute particles — Colligative properties depend on number of solute particles, not their nature.

Q5. If a solute dimerises in solution, the van't Hoff factor i is:
  1. Greater than 1
  2. Equal to 1
  3. Less than 1
  4. Equal to 2

Answer: (C) Less than 1 — Dimerisation reduces number of particles (2 monomers → 1 dimer), so i < 1.

Q6. Henry's law states that at constant temperature, solubility of a gas in liquid is:
  1. Proportional to temperature
  2. Inversely proportional to pressure
  3. Proportional to partial pressure of gas
  4. Independent of pressure

Answer: (C) Proportional to partial pressure of gas — Henry's law: p = K_H × x. Partial pressure ∝ mole fraction (solubility) of dissolved gas.

Q7. Depression in freezing point ΔTf = Kf × m. For electrolyte NaCl, effective molality is:
  1. m
  2. 2m
  3. m/2
  4. 3m

Answer: (B) 2m — NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻, i=2. Effective molality = i×m = 2m.

Q8. Azeotrope is a mixture that:
  1. Separates completely on distillation
  2. Distils without change in composition
  3. Has zero vapour pressure
  4. Is always 50:50 mixture

Answer: (B) Distils without change in composition — Azeotrope: composition of vapour = composition of liquid at boiling point — cannot be separated by simple distillation.

Q9. Mole fraction of a component in solution is always:
  1. Greater than 1
  2. Between 0 and 1
  3. Equal to molarity
  4. Equal to molality

Answer: (B) Between 0 and 1 — Mole fraction x = n_i/n_total; it ranges from 0 (absent) to 1 (pure component). Sum of all = 1.

Q10. Reverse osmosis involves applying pressure to:
  1. Solvent side to push water through membrane
  2. Solution side to push solvent through membrane
  3. Both sides equally
  4. Either side, it doesn't matter

Answer: (B) Solution side to push solvent through membrane — In reverse osmosis, pressure greater than osmotic pressure is applied on solution side, forcing solvent (water) through semipermeable membrane.

Q11. Boiling point elevation constant Kb depends on:
  1. Nature of solute
  2. Concentration of solution
  3. Nature of solvent only
  4. Both solute and solvent

Answer: (C) Nature of solvent only — Kb is a property of the solvent only: Kb = RT²b×M/(1000×ΔHvap). Different for each solvent.

Q12. Isotonic solutions have:
  1. Same molarity
  2. Same osmotic pressure
  3. Same boiling point
  4. Same density

Answer: (B) Same osmotic pressure — Isotonic solutions: same osmotic pressure (π = CRT), so no net osmosis between them.
