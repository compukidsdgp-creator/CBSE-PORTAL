About this chapter

Class 10 grammar focuses on tenses, modals, subject–verb agreement, determiners, reported speech, and active and passive voice, tested through gap-filling, editing and transformation exercises. The quickest gains come from learning the rules for reported speech and subject–verb agreement and practising a few editing passages under time pressure.

Answers & explanations — Grammar

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Identify the passive voice: 'The book was read by Priya.'
  1. Active voice
  2. Passive voice
  3. Reported speech
  4. Indirect speech

Answer: (B) Passive voice — Passive voice: subject receives the action. 'The book' (subject) 'was read' (passive verb) 'by Priya' (agent).

Q2. Change to reported speech: He said, 'I am happy.' →
  1. He said he is happy.
  2. He said that he was happy.
  3. He told that I am happy.
  4. He said I was happy.

Answer: (B) He said that he was happy. — Reported speech: 'said' (no object) stays; tense shifts back (am → was); pronoun I → he. 'He said that he was happy.'

Q3. Which sentence uses the present perfect tense?
  1. She sings well.
  2. She sang well.
  3. She has sung well.
  4. She will sing well.

Answer: (C) She has sung well. — Present perfect: has/have + past participle. 'She has sung' = present perfect.

Q4. A relative clause is introduced by:
  1. because, since, as
  2. who, which, that, whom, whose
  3. if, unless, until
  4. and, but, or

Answer: (B) who, which, that, whom, whose — Relative pronouns (who, which, that, whom, whose) introduce relative clauses that give more information about a noun.

Q5. Fill in the blank: He _____ the letter yesterday. (Correct simple past)
  1. writes
  2. has written
  3. wrote
  4. is writing

Answer: (C) wrote — Simple past for completed action at a specific past time (yesterday). 'wrote' is the simple past of 'write'.

Q6. Subject-verb agreement: 'Each of the students _____ passed.'
  1. have
  2. has
  3. are
  4. were

Answer: (B) has — 'Each' is singular → singular verb 'has'. Rule: Each, every, either, neither → singular verb.

Q7. Active to passive: 'She wrote a poem.' →
  1. A poem was written by her.
  2. A poem is written by she.
  3. A poem was write by her.
  4. A poem has written by her.

Answer: (A) A poem was written by her. — Passive: Object + was/were + past participle + by + subject (objective case). 'She' → 'her'. 'A poem was written by her.'

Q8. What is a phrase?
  1. A group of words with a subject and verb
  2. A group of words without a subject-verb pair
  3. A single word
  4. A complete sentence

Answer: (B) A group of words without a subject-verb pair — Phrase: group of words without a subject-verb pair. It does not make a complete statement. E.g., 'in the morning'.

Q9. Identify the conjunction in: 'He worked hard but could not pass.'
  1. worked
  2. hard
  3. but
  4. pass

Answer: (C) but — 'but' is a coordinating conjunction linking two independent clauses showing contrast.

Q10. 'Neither the manager nor the employees were present.' — verb agrees with:
  1. Manager (singular)
  2. Employees (plural, closer to verb)
  3. Both equally
  4. Neither

Answer: (B) Employees (plural, closer to verb) — With neither...nor / either...or, the verb agrees with the subject CLOSER to it. Employees (plural) → were.

Q11. The sentence 'If I were you, I would apologise.' is in:
  1. Simple condition (Type 1)
  2. Unlikely/hypothetical condition (Type 2)
  3. Impossible condition (Type 3)
  4. Zero conditional

Answer: (B) Unlikely/hypothetical condition (Type 2) — Type 2 conditional: If + past simple, would + base form — for unlikely or imaginary present/future situations.

Q12. Modals are used to express:
  1. Action verbs
  2. Possibility, permission, obligation, ability (can, could, may, must, should)
  3. Adjectives
  4. Conjunctions

Answer: (B) Possibility, permission, obligation, ability (can, could, may, must, should) — Modal verbs (can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would) express modality — possibility, permission, obligation, ability.
