About this chapter

Current electricity explains how charges flow through conductors. You will work with drift velocity, Ohm's law, resistivity and its temperature dependence, combinations of resistors and cells, Kirchhoff's laws and the Wheatstone bridge. Most board marks here come from circuit problems, so practise applying Kirchhoff's loop and junction rules systematically and finding equivalent resistance in series–parallel networks.

Answers & explanations — Current Electricity

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Ohm's law holds when:
  1. Temperature varies
  2. Physical conditions remain constant
  3. Voltage is very high
  4. Current is very small

Answer: (B) Physical conditions remain constant — Ohm's law V=IR is valid only at constant temperature and physical conditions.

Q2. The drift velocity of electrons in a conductor is of the order of:
  1. 10⁸ m/s
  2. 10³ m/s
  3. 10⁻³ m/s
  4. 10⁻⁷ m/s

Answer: (C) 10⁻³ m/s — Drift velocity is very small ~10⁻³ m/s even though electric signal travels at ~c.

Q3. Resistivity of a conductor depends on:
  1. Length
  2. Cross-sectional area
  3. Both length and area
  4. Nature and temperature of material

Answer: (D) Nature and temperature of material — Resistivity ρ is an intrinsic property depending on the material and temperature, not dimensions.

Q4. In Wheatstone bridge at balance condition:
  1. P/Q = R/S
  2. P/R = Q/S
  3. P×S = Q×R
  4. All of these

Answer: (D) All of these — Balance: P/Q = R/S, which rearranges to P×S = Q×R, all equivalent forms.

Q5. EMF of a cell is measured when:
  1. Cell is under load
  2. No current flows through cell
  3. Current is maximum
  4. Cell is short-circuited

Answer: (B) No current flows through cell — EMF = terminal voltage only when no current flows (open circuit), so internal resistance drop = 0.

Q6. Three resistors 1Ω, 2Ω, 3Ω are in parallel. Effective resistance is:
  1. 6Ω
  2. 1Ω
  3. 6/11Ω
  4. 11/6Ω

Answer: (C) 6/11Ω — 1/R = 1/1+1/2+1/3 = 6/6+3/6+2/6 = 11/6 → R = 6/11 Ω.

Q7. Kirchhoff's current law (KCL) is based on:
  1. Conservation of energy
  2. Conservation of charge
  3. Conservation of momentum
  4. Ohm's law

Answer: (B) Conservation of charge — KCL: algebraic sum of currents at a junction = 0, which follows from charge conservation.

Q8. Power dissipated in a resistor R carrying current I is:
  1. IR
  2. I²R
  3. IR²
  4. I/R

Answer: (B) I²R — P = I²R = V²/R = IV. For a given current, P = I²R.

Q9. A cell of EMF 2V and internal resistance 1Ω delivers current to 3Ω resistor. Terminal voltage is:
  1. 2V
  2. 1.5V
  3. 0.5V
  4. 3V

Answer: (B) 1.5V — I = 2/(1+3) = 0.5 A. V_terminal = EMF − Ir = 2 − 0.5×1 = 1.5 V.

Q10. The colour code for a resistor with resistance 47 kΩ ± 5% is:
  1. Yellow-Violet-Orange-Gold
  2. Yellow-Violet-Red-Gold
  3. Blue-Violet-Orange-Gold
  4. Red-Violet-Orange-Gold

Answer: (A) Yellow-Violet-Orange-Gold — 4=Yellow, 7=Violet, 3 zeros=Orange (×10³), Gold=±5%. 47×10³ = 47 kΩ.

Q11. Potentiometer is preferred over voltmeter to measure EMF because:
  1. It is cheaper
  2. It draws no current from cell
  3. It is more rugged
  4. It has lower resistance

Answer: (B) It draws no current from cell — At null point, potentiometer draws zero current so EMF is measured without internal resistance drop.

Q12. Mobility of charge carriers is defined as:
  1. Drift velocity per unit electric field
  2. Current per unit area
  3. Charge per unit mass
  4. Resistance per unit length

Answer: (A) Drift velocity per unit electric field — μ = v_d/E. It represents how easily carriers move in a field.
