About this chapter

Electromagnetic induction explains how a changing magnetic flux produces an EMF. The chapter covers Faraday's laws, Lenz's law, motional EMF, eddy currents, and self and mutual inductance, and leads into alternating current — RMS values, reactance, impedance, resonance in LCR circuits and the transformer. Lenz's law reasoning and LCR resonance numericals appear regularly.

Answers & explanations — Electromagnetic Induction

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction states that induced EMF is:
  1. Proportional to magnetic flux
  2. Proportional to rate of change of flux
  3. Inversely proportional to flux
  4. Equal to magnetic field

Answer: (B) Proportional to rate of change of flux — ε = −dΦ/dt. The induced EMF equals the negative rate of change of magnetic flux.

Q2. Lenz's law is a consequence of:
  1. Conservation of charge
  2. Conservation of energy
  3. Newton's third law
  4. Gauss's law

Answer: (B) Conservation of energy — Lenz's law (induced current opposes change) ensures energy is conserved — work must be done against the opposing force.

Q3. The SI unit of magnetic flux is:
  1. Tesla
  2. Weber
  3. Henry
  4. Ampere

Answer: (B) Weber — Magnetic flux Φ = BA cosθ. Unit: Weber (Wb) = T·m².

Q4. Self-inductance of a coil depends on:
  1. Current through it
  2. Voltage across it
  3. Geometry and material
  4. Temperature only

Answer: (C) Geometry and material — L = NΦ/I, where NΦ depends on geometry (turns, area, length) and core material.

Q5. A transformer steps up voltage. This means:
  1. N₂ > N₁ and I₂ > I₁
  2. N₂ > N₁ and I₂ < I₁
  3. N₂ < N₁ and I₂ > I₁
  4. N₂ < N₁ and I₂ < I₁

Answer: (B) N₂ > N₁ and I₂ < I₁ — Step-up: N₂>N₁ → V₂>V₁. By conservation of power, higher voltage means lower current: I₂ < I₁.

Q6. Energy stored in an inductor of inductance L carrying current I is:
  1. LI
  2. ½LI
  3. LI²
  4. ½LI²

Answer: (D) ½LI² — U = ½LI², analogous to ½CV² for capacitors.

Q7. Eddy currents are used in:
  1. Transformers to increase efficiency
  2. Induction cookers
  3. Increasing magnetic flux
  4. Capacitors

Answer: (B) Induction cookers — Induction cookers generate eddy currents in the cooking vessel, heating it directly.

Q8. If the number of turns in a coil is doubled, its self-inductance becomes:
  1. Half
  2. Double
  3. Four times
  4. Same

Answer: (C) Four times — L ∝ N². Doubling N → L becomes 4L.

Q9. A rectangular loop moves with constant velocity in a uniform magnetic field. EMF induced is:
  1. Constant and non-zero
  2. Zero
  3. Increasing
  4. Oscillating

Answer: (B) Zero — In a uniform field, flux through the loop doesn't change as it moves, so dΦ/dt = 0, hence EMF = 0.

Q10. Mutual inductance between two coils depends on:
  1. Current in primary
  2. Voltage in secondary
  3. Relative geometry and core
  4. Resistance of coils

Answer: (C) Relative geometry and core — M = NΦ₂/I₁ depends on how much flux from primary links with secondary — a function of geometry.

Q11. An AC generator converts:
  1. Electrical to mechanical energy
  2. Mechanical to electrical energy
  3. DC to AC
  4. Chemical to electrical energy

Answer: (B) Mechanical to electrical energy — Generator uses electromagnetic induction to convert mechanical (rotational) energy to electrical energy.

Q12. The phenomenon of electromagnetic induction was discovered by:
  1. Maxwell
  2. Oersted
  3. Faraday
  4. Ampere

Answer: (C) Faraday — Michael Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction in 1831.
