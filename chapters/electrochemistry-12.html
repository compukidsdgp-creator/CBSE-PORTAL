About this chapter

Electrochemistry links chemical reactions with electricity. The chapter covers galvanic cells, electrode potential and the electrochemical series, the Nernst equation, the relation between cell EMF and Gibbs energy, conductance and molar conductivity, Kohlrausch's law, electrolysis with Faraday's laws, batteries and fuel cells, and corrosion. Nernst equation numericals and questions on molar conductivity versus concentration are among the most frequently asked.

Answers & explanations — Electrochemistry

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) has electrode potential:
  1. 1.0 V
  2. −1.0 V
  3. 0.0 V
  4. Depends on temperature

Answer: (C) 0.0 V — SHE is the reference electrode assigned E° = 0.00 V under standard conditions.

Q2. In an electrolytic cell, oxidation occurs at:
  1. Cathode
  2. Anode
  3. Both electrodes
  4. Electrolyte

Answer: (B) Anode — In electrolytic cells, anode is positive; anions migrate and get oxidised at anode.

Q3. Molar conductivity of an electrolyte on dilution:
  1. Decreases
  2. Increases
  3. Remains same
  4. First increases then decreases

Answer: (B) Increases — Dilution increases dissociation and reduces inter-ionic interactions, so molar conductivity increases.

Q4. Kohlrausch's law states that at infinite dilution:
  1. Molar conductivity = sum of individual ionic conductances
  2. All ions move with same speed
  3. Resistance is zero
  4. Conductance is zero

Answer: (A) Molar conductivity = sum of individual ionic conductances — Λ°m = ν₊λ°₊ + ν₋λ°₋. Each ion contributes independently at infinite dilution.

Q5. The EMF of a galvanic cell is related to Gibbs energy by:
  1. ΔG = nFE
  2. ΔG = −nFE
  3. ΔG = nF/E
  4. ΔG = −nF/E

Answer: (B) ΔG = −nFE — ΔG = −nFE_cell. Spontaneous reaction (ΔG<0) requires positive EMF.

Q6. During charging of a lead storage battery, PbSO₄ at cathode is converted to:
  1. Pb
  2. PbO₂
  3. H₂SO₄
  4. PbO

Answer: (A) Pb — During charging: PbSO₄ + 2e⁻ → Pb + SO₄²⁻ (cathode, reduction).

Q7. Faraday's first law of electrolysis states mass deposited is proportional to:
  1. Current only
  2. Time only
  3. Charge passed
  4. Voltage applied

Answer: (C) Charge passed — m = ZIt = ZQ. Mass is proportional to total charge Q = It.

Q8. Nernst equation gives EMF of cell at:
  1. Standard conditions only
  2. Any concentration
  3. Only 25°C
  4. Only 0°C

Answer: (B) Any concentration — Nernst equation: E = E° − (RT/nF)ln Q applies at any concentration and temperature.

Q9. Which metal is used as anode in cathodic protection of iron?
  1. Copper
  2. Silver
  3. Zinc
  4. Platinum

Answer: (C) Zinc — Zinc (more reactive) acts as sacrificial anode, protecting iron from corrosion.

Q10. The specific conductance (κ) of a solution is:
  1. Conductance × cell constant
  2. Conductance / cell constant
  3. 1/conductance
  4. Conductance only

Answer: (A) Conductance × cell constant — κ = G × (l/A) = G × cell constant.

Q11. Electrolysis of dilute H₂SO₄ produces at cathode:
  1. O₂
  2. SO₃
  3. H₂
  4. H₂SO₄

Answer: (C) H₂ — H⁺ ions are reduced at cathode: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂.

Q12. For a spontaneous electrochemical cell, E°cell must be:
  1. Zero
  2. Negative
  3. Positive
  4. One

Answer: (C) Positive — Positive E°cell → negative ΔG° → spontaneous reaction.
