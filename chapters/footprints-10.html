About this chapter

Footprints Without Feet is the supplementary reader for Class 10 English. Its stories — science fiction, mystery and stories of human goodness — are tested through long-answer questions on plot, character and moral values. Keep a one-paragraph summary and two or three character sketches ready for each chapter.

Answers & explanations — Footprints Without Feet

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. In 'A Triumph of Surgery', why does Mr Herriot take Tricki away from Mrs Pumphrey?
  1. To sell the dog
  2. Because Tricki is overweight and ill from overfeeding
  3. Mrs Pumphrey asked him to
  4. Tricki is dangerous

Answer: (B) Because Tricki is overweight and ill from overfeeding — Tricki had become hugely fat and lethargic due to Mrs Pumphrey's overindulgence in feeding. Herriot takes him to get fit.

Q2. In 'The Thief's Story', why does Hari Singh not run away with Anil's money?
  1. He gets caught
  2. He realises the money is not enough
  3. He feels genuine affection and doesn't want to lose the chance to learn
  4. Anil wakes up

Answer: (C) He feels genuine affection and doesn't want to lose the chance to learn — Hari Singh returns the money because Anil was teaching him to read and write — a future of honest opportunity he didn't want to lose.

Q3. In 'The Midnight Visitor', Ausable is described as:
  1. A tall, athletic spy
  2. A fat, unattractive man — surprising for a secret agent
  3. A young, clever agent
  4. A French detective

Answer: (B) A fat, unattractive man — surprising for a secret agent — Fowler is disappointed that Ausable is a fat, sloppy-looking man — not the glamorous spy he imagined. Yet Ausable outwits Max.

Q4. In 'A Question of Trust', Horace Danby is a thief who:
  1. Steals for personal greed
  2. Steals once a year to buy rare and expensive books
  3. Works for a gang
  4. Only steals jewellery

Answer: (B) Steals once a year to buy rare and expensive books — Horace was a good, respectable citizen who robbed one safe a year to buy the rare books he loved.

Q5. In 'Footprints Without Feet', Griffin is a scientist who discovers:
  1. How to fly
  2. A drug to become invisible
  3. Time travel
  4. How to become very small

Answer: (B) A drug to become invisible — Griffin discovered a rare drug that could make the human body transparent (invisible). He swallowed it and became invisible.

Q6. In 'The Making of a Scientist', Richard Ebright's first science project was about:
  1. Cells of frogs
  2. Tagging monarch butterflies to study their migration
  3. DNA research
  4. Astronomy

Answer: (B) Tagging monarch butterflies to study their migration — Ebright's first project was tagging monarch butterflies to study their migration — this began his scientific journey.

Q7. In 'The Necklace' (not in Footprints but commonly studied), Matilda borrows what?
  1. A diamond ring
  2. A pearl necklace
  3. A diamond necklace
  4. An emerald bracelet

Answer: (C) A diamond necklace — Matilda Loisel borrows a diamond necklace from her friend Madame Forestier for the ball — loses it and spends 10 years in poverty replacing it.

Q8. In 'The Hack Driver', the narrator is tricked by:
  1. The sheriff
  2. Oliver Lutkins himself, who was driving the hack
  3. A lawyer
  4. A farmer

Answer: (B) Oliver Lutkins himself, who was driving the hack — The narrator is fooled by Oliver Lutkins who poses as Bill the hack driver and takes him all over town — hiding himself.

Q9. In 'Bholi', what transforms Bholi's life?
  1. Marriage
  2. Education and a kind teacher
  3. Moving to a city
  4. A scholarship

Answer: (B) Education and a kind teacher — Bholi's teacher encourages her with kindness and belief, transforming the timid, scarred girl into a confident, educated woman.

Q10. In 'The Book That Saved the Earth', what book almost prevented a Martian invasion of Earth?
  1. An encyclopedia
  2. A nursery rhyme book (Mother Goose)
  3. A science textbook
  4. A novel

Answer: (B) A nursery rhyme book (Mother Goose) — The Martians misinterpret a nursery rhyme (Mother Goose) as a great prophecy, causing them to retreat.

Q11. In 'Footprints Without Feet', Griffin steals clothes and food from:
  1. A school
  2. A theatrical company's store
  3. A supermarket
  4. A police station

Answer: (B) A theatrical company's store — Griffin breaks into a theatrical company and steals stage make-up and clothes (wig, bandages, glasses) to cover his invisible body.

Q12. What is the moral of 'The Thief's Story'?
  1. Crime pays
  2. Education and trust can reform even a thief
  3. Rich people are always kind
  4. Always lock your money

Answer: (B) Education and trust can reform even a thief — Anil's trust and willingness to teach Hari to read transforms the thief — showing that education and genuine care can bring moral change.
