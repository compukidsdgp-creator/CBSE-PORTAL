About this chapter

Aldehydes and ketones both contain the carbonyl group (C=O), and its polarity explains almost everything in this chapter: nucleophilic addition, the reactivity order (aldehydes are more reactive than ketones), and the acidity of α-hydrogens. Board papers regularly test named reactions — aldol condensation, Cannizzaro, Clemmensen and Wolff–Kishner reductions — together with distinction tests such as Tollens', Fehling's and the iodoform test. Carboxylic acids and their acidity trends are part of the same unit.

Answers & explanations — Aldehydes & Ketones

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Aldehydes differ from ketones in having:
  1. Carbonyl group
  2. At least one H on carbonyl carbon
  3. Two alkyl groups
  4. Higher boiling points

Answer: (B) At least one H on carbonyl carbon — Aldehyde: R−CHO (H on carbonyl C). Ketone: R−CO−R' (no H on carbonyl C).

Q2. Which test distinguishes aldehyde from ketone?
  1. 2,4-DNP test
  2. Iodoform test
  3. Tollens' test
  4. Benedict's test

Answer: (C) Tollens' test — Tollens' test (silver mirror): aldehydes reduce Ag⁺ to Ag metal; ketones do not (except methyl ketones in some versions).

Q3. Nucleophilic addition to carbonyl group: the nucleophile attacks:
  1. Oxygen atom
  2. Carbon atom of C=O
  3. Both atoms
  4. Adjacent carbon

Answer: (B) Carbon atom of C=O — Carbonyl carbon is electrophilic (δ+ due to electron withdrawal by O). Nucleophile attacks C.

Q4. Aldol condensation occurs between two molecules of aldehyde/ketone in presence of:
  1. Conc. H₂SO₄
  2. Dilute NaOH or HCl
  3. Anhydrous AlCl₃
  4. HI

Answer: (B) Dilute NaOH or HCl — Aldol condensation requires dilute base (NaOH) or dilute acid; produces β-hydroxy carbonyl compound.

Q5. Iodoform test gives positive result with:
  1. Methanol
  2. Methanal
  3. Ethanal
  4. Propan-1-ol

Answer: (C) Ethanal — Iodoform test: CH₃CHO (ethanal) gives CHI₃. Also positive: methyl ketones, ethanol, secondary alcohols with CH₃CHOH group.

Q6. Clemmensen reduction converts C=O to CH₂ using:
  1. Zn-Hg/HCl
  2. NaBH₄
  3. LiAlH₄
  4. H₂/Pd

Answer: (A) Zn-Hg/HCl — Clemmensen: Zn(Hg) amalgam in conc. HCl reduces ketone/aldehyde C=O to CH₂.

Q7. Cannizzaro reaction is shown by aldehydes having:
  1. α-hydrogen
  2. No α-hydrogen
  3. Two α-hydrogens
  4. Branched chain

Answer: (B) No α-hydrogen — Cannizzaro: aldehydes with no α-H undergo disproportionation in conc. base (e.g., HCHO, benzaldehyde).

Q8. Boiling point of aldehydes is higher than alkanes but lower than alcohols because:
  1. No H-bonding; dipole-dipole only
  2. Strong H-bonding like alcohols
  3. Ionic interactions
  4. London forces only

Answer: (A) No H-bonding; dipole-dipole only — Carbonyl compounds have dipole-dipole interactions (no O−H bond for intermolecular H-bonding), so BP > alkanes but < alcohols.

Q9. Benzaldehyde is obtained industrially by:
  1. Oxidation of toluene
  2. Reduction of benzoic acid
  3. Hydrolysis of benzyl chloride
  4. Ozonolysis of styrene

Answer: (A) Oxidation of toluene — Industrial method: catalytic oxidation of toluene (C₆H₅CH₃) gives benzaldehyde.

Q10. Which compound gives only one product on Clemmensen reduction?
  1. CH₃CHO
  2. CH₃COCH₃
  3. HCHO
  4. C₆H₅CHO

Answer: (C) HCHO — HCHO (formaldehyde) → CH₄ (methane) on Clemmensen. It has no additional carbon framework.

Q11. Semicarbazone is formed by reaction of carbonyl compound with:
  1. Semicarbazide
  2. NH₂OH
  3. PhNHNH₂
  4. HCN

Answer: (A) Semicarbazide — Carbonyl + H₂N−NH−CO−NH₂ (semicarbazide) → semicarbazone + H₂O. Used to characterise carbonyls.

Q12. The product of reduction of acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO) with NaBH₄ is:
  1. Methanol
  2. Ethanol
  3. Ethane
  4. Acetic acid

Answer: (B) Ethanol — NaBH₄ reduces CH₃CHO to CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol). It is a selective reducing agent for C=O.
