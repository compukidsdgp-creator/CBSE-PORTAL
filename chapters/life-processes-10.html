About this chapter

Life processes are the basic functions that keep organisms alive: nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion. The chapter explains photosynthesis, human digestion, aerobic and anaerobic respiration, the human heart and double circulation, transport in plants through xylem and phloem, and the structure of the nephron. Labelled diagrams of the digestive system, heart and nephron are frequently asked.

Answers & explanations — Life Processes

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Photosynthesis occurs in:
  1. Mitochondria
  2. Nucleus
  3. Chloroplasts
  4. Ribosomes

Answer: (C) Chloroplasts — Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll; light reactions occur in thylakoid membrane, dark reactions in stroma.

Q2. The equation for aerobic respiration is:
  1. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
  2. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP
  3. 6CO₂+6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆+6O₂
  4. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₄O₃

Answer: (B) C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP — Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy (ATP). Net: ~36-38 ATP.

Q3. Stomata in leaves help in:
  1. Water absorption
  2. Gas exchange and transpiration
  3. Photosynthesis directly
  4. Mineral absorption

Answer: (B) Gas exchange and transpiration — Stomata: pores in leaves for CO₂ in / O₂ out (gas exchange) and water vapour out (transpiration).

Q4. In human digestion, bile is produced by the:
  1. Stomach
  2. Pancreas
  3. Liver
  4. Small intestine

Answer: (C) Liver — Bile is produced in the liver, stored in gall bladder, released into duodenum to emulsify fats.

Q5. Blood entering the right atrium comes from:
  1. Lungs (oxygenated)
  2. Body tissues (deoxygenated)
  3. Heart muscle
  4. Aorta

Answer: (B) Body tissues (deoxygenated) — Deoxygenated blood from body → Superior/Inferior Vena Cava → Right atrium → Right ventricle → Lungs.

Q6. Excretion in humans is primarily by the:
  1. Liver
  2. Lungs
  3. Kidneys
  4. Skin

Answer: (C) Kidneys — Kidneys filter blood, form urine (urea, water, salts). Primary excretory organs. Lungs excrete CO₂.

Q7. The functional unit of kidney is:
  1. Neuron
  2. Nephron
  3. Alveolus
  4. Villus

Answer: (B) Nephron — Nephron is the filtration unit of kidney: glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, tubules, collecting duct.

Q8. Transpiration in plants helps in:
  1. Food transport
  2. Water and mineral transport upward
  3. CO₂ absorption
  4. Photosynthesis only

Answer: (B) Water and mineral transport upward — Transpiration pull creates suction that draws water and minerals upward through xylem.

Q9. Fermentation is an example of:
  1. Aerobic respiration
  2. Anaerobic respiration
  3. Photosynthesis
  4. Digestion

Answer: (B) Anaerobic respiration — Fermentation: glucose → ethanol + CO₂ (by yeast) in absence of oxygen — anaerobic respiration.

Q10. Haemoglobin carries oxygen and is present in:
  1. White blood cells
  2. Plasma
  3. Red blood cells
  4. Platelets

Answer: (C) Red blood cells — Haemoglobin in RBCs (erythrocytes) carries O₂ from lungs to tissues.

Q11. Villi in small intestine increase:
  1. Length
  2. Surface area for absorption
  3. Blood supply
  4. Peristalsis

Answer: (B) Surface area for absorption — Villi (and microvilli = brush border) enormously increase surface area, maximising nutrient absorption.

Q12. Arteries carry blood:
  1. Away from heart
  2. To the heart
  3. Only oxygenated blood
  4. Only deoxygenated blood

Answer: (A) Away from heart — Arteries always carry blood AWAY from heart. Veins carry blood TO heart. Oxygenation content varies.
