About this chapter

Real numbers include rational and irrational numbers. This chapter covers the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, using prime factorisation to find the HCF and LCM, and proving that numbers such as √2 and √3 are irrational. The relationship HCF × LCM = product of two numbers is frequently tested in short-answer questions.

Answers & explanations — Real Numbers

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Euclid's Division Lemma states: for integers a and b (b>0), there exist unique q and r such that:
  1. a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b
  2. a = bq − r, r > 0
  3. a = b + qr
  4. a + b = qr

Answer: (A) a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b — Euclid's Division Lemma: a = bq + r where 0 ≤ r < b. Used to find HCF by repeated division.

Q2. HCF of 36 and 48 is:
  1. 6
  2. 12
  3. 4
  4. 24

Answer: (B) 12 — 36 = 2²×3², 48 = 2⁴×3. HCF = 2²×3 = 12.

Q3. LCM × HCF = :
  1. a + b
  2. a × b
  3. a/b
  4. a − b

Answer: (B) a × b — For any two positive integers a and b: HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b.

Q4. √2 is:
  1. Rational
  2. Irrational
  3. Integer
  4. Natural number

Answer: (B) Irrational — √2 cannot be expressed as p/q (integers, q≠0) — proved by contradiction assuming it is rational.

Q5. The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic states every integer >1 has:
  1. Many prime factorisations
  2. A unique prime factorisation
  3. At least one composite factor
  4. No prime factors if even

Answer: (B) A unique prime factorisation — Every integer >1 is either prime or can be expressed as a unique product of primes (up to order).

Q6. If HCF(a,b) = 1, the numbers are called:
  1. Equal
  2. Co-prime
  3. Prime
  4. Twin primes

Answer: (B) Co-prime — Co-prime (relatively prime): HCF = 1. They share no common factor other than 1.

Q7. The decimal expansion of 7/8 is:
  1. 0.875 (terminating)
  2. Non-terminating repeating
  3. Non-terminating non-repeating
  4. Cannot be found

Answer: (A) 0.875 (terminating) — 8 = 2³. Denominator has only factor 2 → terminating decimal. 7/8 = 0.875.

Q8. For the decimal expansion of p/q to terminate, q must have prime factors:
  1. 2 and 5 only
  2. Any primes
  3. Only 2
  4. Only 3

Answer: (A) 2 and 5 only — p/q terminates iff q = 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ (m,n ≥ 0). If q has any other prime factor, decimal is non-terminating repeating.

Q9. The number 0.2bar (0.2222...) as a fraction is:
  1. 2/9
  2. 2/10
  3. 1/5
  4. 2/99

Answer: (A) 2/9 — Let x=0.222... → 10x=2.222... → 9x=2 → x=2/9.

Q10. Prime factorisation of 2520 includes the prime 7 with power:
  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 7

Answer: (A) 1 — 2520 = 2³×3²×5×7. The prime 7 appears with power 1.

Q11. If p is prime and p | a², then p | a is due to:
  1. Euclid's lemma
  2. Fermat's theorem
  3. Fundamental theorem of algebra
  4. Division algorithm

Answer: (A) Euclid's lemma — Euclid's lemma: if prime p divides product ab, then p|a or p|b. Applied twice: p|a²=a·a → p|a.

Q12. log₁₀(100) =
  1. 10
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 100

Answer: (C) 2 — log₁₀(100) = log₁₀(10²) = 2.
