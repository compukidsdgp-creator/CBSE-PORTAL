About this chapter

Electrostatics studies charges at rest. The unit covers Coulomb's law, electric field and field lines, electric dipoles, Gauss's law and its applications, electric potential and potential energy, and capacitors with and without dielectrics. Board papers often include a derivation using Gauss's law and numerical questions on capacitor combinations and stored energy.

Answers & explanations — Electrostatics

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. Two point charges +4 μC and −4 μC are placed 10 cm apart. The electric field at the midpoint between them is:
  1. Zero
  2. 1.44×10⁷ N/C directed from +q to −q
  3. 2.88×10⁷ N/C directed from +q to −q
  4. 1.44×10⁷ N/C directed from −q to +q

Answer: (C) 2.88×10⁷ N/C directed from +q to −q — At midpoint both fields point same direction (from + to −). E = 2×(kq/r²) = 2×9×10⁹×4×10⁻⁶/(0.05)² = 2.88×10⁷ N/C.

Q2. The electric potential inside a charged hollow conductor is:
  1. Zero
  2. Equal to that on the surface
  3. Greater than the surface
  4. Varies with position

Answer: (B) Equal to that on the surface — Inside a conductor, E=0 so no work is done moving charges — potential is constant and equals surface potential.

Q3. Capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is C. If a dielectric slab of dielectric constant K completely fills the gap, new capacitance is:
  1. C/K
  2. KC
  3. C + K
  4. C − K

Answer: (B) KC — Inserting dielectric of constant K multiplies capacitance: C' = KC.

Q4. Gauss's law states that total electric flux through any closed surface equals:
  1. Total charge inside divided by ε₀
  2. Total charge outside divided by ε₀
  3. Zero always
  4. Charge on the surface divided by ε₀

Answer: (A) Total charge inside divided by ε₀ — Φ = Q_enc/ε₀. Only charge enclosed by the Gaussian surface contributes to net flux.

Q5. Equipotential surfaces for a uniform electric field are:
  1. Concentric spheres
  2. Concentric cylinders
  3. Planes perpendicular to field lines
  4. Planes parallel to field lines

Answer: (C) Planes perpendicular to field lines — In a uniform field, potential changes only along the field direction, so equipotential surfaces are planes ⊥ to field.

Q6. Energy stored in a capacitor of capacitance C charged to voltage V is:
  1. CV
  2. ½CV
  3. CV²
  4. ½CV²

Answer: (D) ½CV² — U = ½CV² (also = Q²/2C = ½QV).

Q7. A dipole of moment p is placed in a uniform field E at angle θ. Torque experienced is:
  1. pE sinθ
  2. pE cosθ
  3. pE tanθ
  4. pE

Answer: (A) pE sinθ — τ = p × E; magnitude τ = pE sinθ.

Q8. If the distance between two charges is doubled, the Coulomb force becomes:
  1. Double
  2. Half
  3. One-quarter
  4. Four times

Answer: (C) One-quarter — F ∝ 1/r². Doubling r → F becomes F/4.

Q9. Which of the following is a polar molecule?
  1. O₂
  2. CO₂
  3. H₂O
  4. N₂

Answer: (C) H₂O — H₂O has a bent geometry; the bond dipoles don't cancel, giving a net dipole moment.

Q10. Three capacitors of 2 μF each are connected in series. Equivalent capacitance is:
  1. 6 μF
  2. 2/3 μF
  3. 3/2 μF
  4. 2 μF

Answer: (B) 2/3 μF — 1/C = 1/2+1/2+1/2 = 3/2 → C = 2/3 μF.

Q11. Electric field lines never intersect because:
  1. They are imaginary
  2. At intersection point field would have two directions
  3. They are always straight
  4. Charges repel

Answer: (B) At intersection point field would have two directions — If two field lines crossed, there would be two directions of E at that point — impossible since E is unique at any point.

Q12. For a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, the electric field just outside its surface is:
  1. Zero
  2. σ/ε₀ perpendicular to surface
  3. σ/2ε₀ perpendicular to surface
  4. σε₀ parallel to surface

Answer: (B) σ/ε₀ perpendicular to surface — Applying Gauss's law to a small pillbox at the surface gives E = σ/ε₀.
