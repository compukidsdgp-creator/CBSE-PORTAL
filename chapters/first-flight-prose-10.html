About this chapter

The prose chapters of First Flight cover memorable stories and true accounts — from Nelson Mandela's long walk to freedom to light-hearted tales about everyday characters. Questions ask about themes, character traits, important events and the values each story conveys. Prepare short summaries of every chapter and practise answering extract-based and long-answer questions.

Answers & explanations — First Flight — Prose Chapters

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. In 'A Letter to God', Lencho compares the raindrops to:
  1. Silver coins
  2. New coins
  3. Pearls
  4. Diamonds

Answer: (B) New coins — Lencho compared the large raindrops to new coins and the smaller ones to coins of lesser value.

Q2. What does Lencho ask God for in his letter?
  1. A new house
  2. 100 pesos
  3. Seeds for sowing
  4. Rain

Answer: (B) 100 pesos — After his crop was destroyed by hail, Lencho wrote to God asking for 100 pesos to sow his field again.

Q3. In 'Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom', who is called a twin obligation?
  1. Obligation to family and to God
  2. Obligation to himself and to his country
  3. Obligation to his party and to his tribe
  4. Obligation to white and black people

Answer: (B) Obligation to himself and to his country — Mandela said every man has twin obligations — to his family and to his people (country).

Q4. In 'Two Stories About Flying', the black aeroplane pilot cannot be explained because:
  1. He was a ghost
  2. There were no clouds for him to fly in
  3. No such aeroplane or pilot existed in records
  4. He landed before the narrator

Answer: (C) No such aeroplane or pilot existed in records — The mysterious pilot who guided the narrator through the storm could not be found in records — he remains unexplained.

Q5. In 'From the Diary of Anne Frank', Anne's best friend is considered to be:
  1. Her sister Margot
  2. Her diary (Kitty)
  3. Her teacher Mrs Kuperus
  4. Her mother

Answer: (B) Her diary (Kitty) — Anne Frank regarded her diary 'Kitty' as her best friend — she wrote to it as if writing to a person.

Q6. In 'The Hundred Dresses — Part I', Wanda Petronski claims to have how many dresses?
  1. 50
  2. 75
  3. 100
  4. 200

Answer: (C) 100 — Wanda claimed she had a hundred dresses, all lined up in her closet — which was not believed by the other girls.

Q7. In 'Glimpses of India', what is Coorg famous for?
  1. Tea and spices
  2. Coffee and spices
  3. Silk and sandal
  4. Rice and jute

Answer: (B) Coffee and spices — Coorg (Kodagu) in Karnataka is famous for coffee and spices (pepper, cardamom). It is called the 'Scotland of India'.

Q8. In 'Mijbil the Otter', Gavin Maxwell's otter was from:
  1. Iraq (Tigris marshes)
  2. Africa
  3. Scotland
  4. India

Answer: (A) Iraq (Tigris marshes) — Mijbil was an otter Maxwell acquired in the Tigris marshes of Iraq. He brought it to London.

Q9. In 'Madam Rides the Bus', Valli's most fascinating thing was:
  1. The sea
  2. The mountains
  3. The bus that passed her home
  4. The town market

Answer: (C) The bus that passed her home — Valli was fascinated by the bus that passed her village and had a great desire to ride it.

Q10. In 'The Sermon at Benares', where was the Buddha when he found enlightenment?
  1. Benares (Varanasi)
  2. Bodh Gaya (under the fig/Bodhi tree)
  3. Kapilavastu
  4. Sarnath

Answer: (B) Bodh Gaya (under the fig/Bodhi tree) — Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya (not Benares). He gave his first sermon at Benares.

Q11. In 'The Proposal' (play), Lomov comes to Chubukov's house to:
  1. Buy the Oxen Meadows
  2. Propose to Natalya
  3. Challenge Chubukov
  4. Borrow money

Answer: (B) Propose to Natalya — Lomov comes to propose marriage to Chubukov's daughter Natalya, but they keep arguing about trivial matters.

Q12. In 'A Triumph of Surgery', Tricki is the name of:
  1. The doctor
  2. Mr Herriot's dog
  3. Mrs Pumphrey's dog
  4. The surgery clinic

Answer: (C) Mrs Pumphrey's dog — Tricki is Mrs Pumphrey's pet dog who falls ill due to overfeeding. Mr Herriot (vet) successfully treats him.
