About this chapter

Electricity in Class 10 covers electric current, potential difference, Ohm's law, the factors that affect resistance, resistors in series and parallel, the heating effect of current and electric power. Learn the formulas V = IR, R = ρl/A and P = VI = I²R = V²/R, and practise circuit problems — they form a large share of the Science paper.

Answers & explanations — Electricity

Try the quiz above first, then open each question to check the answer and the reasoning.

Q1. SI unit of electric charge is:
  1. Ampere
  2. Volt
  3. Coulomb
  4. Ohm

Answer: (C) Coulomb — Charge is measured in Coulombs (C). Current I = Q/t.

Q2. Resistance of a wire depends on:
  1. Current through it
  2. Voltage across it
  3. Length, area and material
  4. Temperature only

Answer: (C) Length, area and material — R = ρl/A where ρ is resistivity, l is length, A is cross-sectional area.

Q3. Three 2Ω resistors in parallel. Equivalent resistance is:
  1. 6Ω
  2. 2Ω
  3. 2/3Ω
  4. 3/2Ω

Answer: (C) 2/3Ω — 1/R = 1/2+1/2+1/2 = 3/2 → R = 2/3 Ω.

Q4. Electric power P = V²/R. If voltage doubles and R unchanged, power becomes:
  1. Double
  2. Half
  3. 4 times
  4. Same

Answer: (C) 4 times — P ∝ V². Doubling V → P becomes 4P.

Q5. 1 kWh is equal to:
  1. 1000 J
  2. 3.6×10³ J
  3. 3.6×10⁶ J
  4. 1000 W

Answer: (C) 3.6×10⁶ J — 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6×10⁶ J = 3.6 MJ.

Q6. A fuse wire has:
  1. Very high melting point
  2. High resistance and low melting point
  3. Low resistance
  4. Same as connecting wire

Answer: (B) High resistance and low melting point — Fuse: high resistance (heats up at excess current) and low melting point (melts to break circuit).

Q7. Ohm's law: V = IR is valid when:
  1. Temperature is constant
  2. Current is DC only
  3. Resistance is high
  4. Voltage is very low

Answer: (A) Temperature is constant — Ohm's law holds only at constant temperature; resistance changes with temperature.

Q8. Direction of conventional current is:
  1. Direction of electron flow
  2. Opposite to electron flow
  3. From − to + inside the cell
  4. Both same

Answer: (B) Opposite to electron flow — Conventional current flows from + to − (outside), which is opposite to electron flow (from − to +).

Q9. The heating effect of current is used in:
  1. Fans
  2. Electric motors
  3. Electric iron and bulb
  4. Transformers

Answer: (C) Electric iron and bulb — H = I²Rt (Joule's law). Electric iron, bulbs, heaters — all use the heating effect.

Q10. If charge Q flows through a circuit in time t, the current I =
  1. Q/t
  2. Qt
  3. t/Q
  4. Q²t

Answer: (A) Q/t — Current I = Q/t (charge per unit time).

Q11. Potential difference of 6V is applied across a 3Ω resistor. Current is:
  1. 2A
  2. 18A
  3. 0.5A
  4. 3A

Answer: (A) 2A — I = V/R = 6/3 = 2 A.

Q12. An electric device of 500W runs for 2 hours daily. Monthly bill (₹5/unit, 30 days) is:
  1. ₹100
  2. ₹150
  3. ₹200
  4. ₹250

Answer: (B) ₹150 — Energy = 0.5kW × 2h × 30 = 30 kWh. Cost = 30 × 5 = ₹150.
